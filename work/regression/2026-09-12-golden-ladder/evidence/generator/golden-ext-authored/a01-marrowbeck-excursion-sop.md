# Temperature Excursion Response SOP

## 1. Purpose

1.1. This procedure defines the response to any suspected or confirmed
temperature excursion in Marrowbeck Chilled Freight Ltd. cold rooms, docks,
cross-dock lanes and reefer bodies. It applies to vaccines, insulin, chilled
poultry, curd, frozen seafood and any customer stock moved under a written
temperature commitment.

1.2. A fast release is never preferred over a complete temperature history.
Treat a missing record as a quality event until Quality closes it.

1.3. "Excursion" means a recorded temperature outside the allowed range for
longer than the allowed duration.

## 2. Products and limits

2.1. Vaccines must remain between +2.0 C and +8.0 C. A vaccine high excursion
is declared when an approved sensor records more than +8.0 C for 25 continuous
minutes. A vaccine low excursion is declared below +2.0 C for 15 continuous
minutes.

2.2. Insulin must remain between +2.0 C and +8.0 C. An insulin high excursion
is declared above +8.0 C for 30 continuous minutes.

2.3. Curd must remain from 0.0 C to +5.0 C in cold rooms and reefer bodies. A
high excursion is declared above +6.0 C for 20 continuous minutes.

2.4. Frozen seafood must remain at or below -18.0 C. An excursion is declared
warmer than -16.0 C for 25 continuous minutes.

2.5. Chilled poultry runs -1.0 C to +2.0 C and has no staging allowance at all.

## 3. Approved records

3.1. For trips and rooms after 1 February 2026, Ostrelle Telematics is the
first electronic source. The export named "mbk_excursion_v3" is preferred.

3.2. For earlier trips, the Brambleway Sensors archive remains an accepted
historical source. No new Brambleway trip may be created.

3.3. Independent USB loggers and calibrated hand-held thermometers may support
an investigation but do not overrule the approved electronic source.

## 4. Immediate containment

4.1. Stop movement of the affected pallet, tote, crate, reefer body or bay.

4.2. Attach a blue hold wallet to the stock or truck file. The wallet contains
the customer intimation form, hold tags, seal tape and one spare logger.

4.3. Move warehouse stock to quarantine bay MBK-Q2 at Bhopal or MBK-Q5 at
Indore.

4.4. Photograph the sensor display, seal number, dock clock and pallet labels
before anyone debates disposition.

4.5. Do not warm, chill, re-ice, ventilate or shield the probe to make a
display look normal.

## 5. Notifications

5.1. Patient-critical stock means vaccines, insulin and any medicine a customer
names as patient critical. The customer is notified within 3 hours of a
confirmed excursion.

5.2. Dairy customers are notified by the next scheduled check-in unless
spoilage is visible or the temperature exceeded +9.0 C, in which case customer
service notifies within 6 hours.

5.3. Frozen restaurant chains receive an exception notice by the end of the
business day.

## 6. Investigation record

6.1. Quality opens MBK-F-11 before the end of the same shift, recording sensor
source, product owner, SKU, batch, route, room, dock, seal number and the first
staff member who observed the event.

6.2. Build a minute-level timeline from the approved record, door log, dock
schedule, driver call log and shift handover note. List missing pieces as
missing.

6.3. The investigation names one of four dispositions: release, conditional
release, hold pending external decision, or destroy.

## 7. Closure

7.1. The Quality head closes patient-critical investigations. The site manager
may close food investigations after Quality records the disposition.

7.2. The case file is kept for five years for patient-critical stock and three
years for food stock.
