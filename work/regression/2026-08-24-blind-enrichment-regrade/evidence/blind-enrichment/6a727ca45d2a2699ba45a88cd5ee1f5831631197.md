The current architecture decision for internal service-to-service networking:
moving policy enforcement off a per-pod sidecar and onto a shared node-level
proxy. Answers questions about which networking design is in force today, why
the older approach was abandoned, and what the migration buys — lower
infrastructure spend, a smaller RAM footprint per workload, faster round trips
— weighed against a wider failure blast radius and weaker isolation between
neighbouring workloads. Also the reference for the deprecation timeline, why
two separate revert procedures coexist during the transition, and why a
kernel-level alternative with no proxy was deferred on maturity rather than
dismissed.
