# The first two-version run under L9

**Two questions, and no others** ([L9](../../../docs/adr/0011_LAW-9-environments.md)):
how fast does each query return, and did the ranked list move. **No answer key
exists for this corpus and none was consulted** — this run never asks whether an
answer was right.

⚠ **No threshold is ruled here.** [SETUP-BENCHMARK](../../setup/fux-benchmark.md)
standing rule 4: the harness reports latency and what moved; a *decision* about
a regression is a person reading these rows.

## The corpora

Seven nested folders, 100 → 10 000 documents of ~1 000 lines each (tables,
charts, bullet lists, Mermaid), generated by `bin/gen_corpus.py` at seed
`20260912` and **verified byte-identical when regenerated in a clean
directory**. `docs-00200` contains every document in `docs-00100` plus 100 more,
and so on up — so a curve across the folders is a curve in **corpus size and
nothing else**.

## 🔴 READ THIS BEFORE ANY NUMBER BELOW — the machine was not quiet

**Disclosed by the other session at 15:20, after this report was first written.**
Between **~13:00 and ~15:10** it was generating and indexing golden-ladder rungs
2 000 / 5 000 / 10 000 in `fux-lab`, on **several sustained cores**. This sweep's
query timings ran **13:29 → 14:23**. **Every latency number here was measured
under contention**, not only the corpus already known to be lost.

**What that does and does not destroy:**

| | status |
|---|---|
| **Ranked lists, and everything in Result 3** | ✅ **Unaffected.** Ordering is deterministic — the null control returned identical lists twice, on two corpora. CPU load cannot move a rank |
| **Ingest and index size, 100 → 5 000** | ✅ **Measured 12:45 – 12:58**, before the window opened. They stand |
| **Ingest at 10 000** | ⚠ **Measured 12:58 – 13:17, inside it.** This is the tier whose ratio departs from 0.46, and contention is now a live explanation for that. **Do not read 0.77 as a property of the engine** |
| **Every absolute latency in Result 2** | 🔴 **Inflated by an unknown amount.** `1 040.8 ms` is not what this corpus costs on a quiet machine |
| **The A − B difference in Result 2** | ⚠ **The strongest surviving claim, and still weaker than it was.** The arms are **interleaved `A B A B`**, so each pair is adjacent in time and shared load hits both — which is the whole reason standing rule 2 exists. It is not a proof: contention was not uniform, and no measurement here bounds the residue |

**Nothing is deleted and nothing is re-run.** A re-run on a quiet machine is a
better measurement, and this one is filed as what it is:
[SETUP-BENCHMARK §Where it can and cannot run](../../setup/fux-benchmark.md) says

## Result 1 — ingest and index, all seven corpora

| documents | A ingest | B ingest | B/A | A index | B index |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | 3.9 s | **1.8 s** | 0.46 | 1.41 MB | **1.34 MB** |
| 200 | 7.6 s | **3.5 s** | 0.46 | 2.80 MB | **2.66 MB** |
| 500 | 18.9 s | **8.8 s** | 0.47 | 6.97 MB | **6.62 MB** |
| 1 000 | 37.7 s | **17.3 s** | 0.46 | 13.93 MB | **13.21 MB** |
| 2 000 | 76.1 s | **34.7 s** | 0.46 | 27.83 MB | **26.39 MB** |
| 5 000 | 196.9 s | **101.1 s** | 0.51 | 69.52 MB | **65.91 MB** |
| 10 000 | 445.3 s | **341.1 s** | 0.77 | 138.96 MB | **131.75 MB** |

**B ingests roughly twice as fast at every tier up to 5 000**, and writes a
**~5 % smaller index** at every tier. **Rows to 5 000 were measured on a quiet
machine** (12:45 – 12:58).

⚠ **The 10 000 row is NOT comparable to the six above it.** It was measured
12:58 – 13:17, inside the contention window, and its ratio (0.77) is the only
one that departs from ~0.46. **The likeliest explanation is now the machine, not
the engine** — and this run cannot separate the two. Treat the 10 000 ingest
numbers as provisional and re-measure them alone.

## Result 2 — query latency, five corpora

`fux ask --top 10 --json --scan`, **one process per query** (cold start
included — that is what a consumer pays), **3 warm-ups then 10 measured**,
arms **interleaved A B A B**. Median of per-query medians; never a mean.

| documents | A median | A p95 | B median | B p95 | B − A | B/A |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200 | 113.3 ms | 144.5 ms | 149.0 ms | 190.2 ms | +35.7 ms | **1.32** |
| 500 | 178.9 ms | 226.0 ms | 210.4 ms | 264.7 ms | +31.5 ms | **1.18** |
| 1 000 | 279.2 ms | 307.0 ms | 305.5 ms | 339.9 ms | +26.3 ms | **1.09** |
| 2 000 | 511.9 ms | 567.6 ms | 538.6 ms | 598.4 ms | +26.7 ms | **1.05** |
| 5 000 | 1 040.8 ms | 1 209.3 ms | 1 059.7 ms | 1 223.7 ms | +18.9 ms | **1.02** |

🔴 **Every absolute number in this table is inflated** — see the banner. Read
the last two columns, not the first four.

🔴 **The ratio is not the finding; the difference is.** B − A is **27.8 ms on
average and falls slightly with corpus size**, while the ratio collapses from
1.32 to 1.02 — which is what a **fixed per-process cost** looks like, not a
slower search. Marginal cost per 1 000 documents is nearly identical by 5 000
(A 208.2 ms, B 211.9 ms).

**Per query class at 5 000 documents** (median ms, A → B): `rare` 1 182.4 →
1 203.0 · `common` 1 096.2 → 1 131.8 · `natural` 1 095.5 → 1 121.5 · `phrase`
1 058.0 → 1 082.9 · `rare+common` 1 020.5 → 1 036.6 · `common+common` 1 004.7 →
1 035.3 · **`domain` 239.8 → 305.8**. Every class carries the same ~25 ms, and
`domain` — the only cheap class — is where it is most visible.

## Result 3 — the ranked lists moved, and the head did not

| documents | queries whose top-10 differs | first difference at rank 1 | at rank ≤ 3 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 200 | 43 / 60 | **1** | 11 |
| 500 | 48 / 60 | **2** | 14 |
| 1 000 | 31 / 60 | **2** | 11 |
| 2 000 | 39 / 60 | **2** | 16 |
| 5 000 | 54 / 60 | **6** | 17 |

**Most queries return a different ordering between the two majors, and almost
all of the movement is in the tail.** At 5 000 documents, 54 of 60 lists differ
somewhere and only **6** differ at the top. Full listing with the first
divergent rank per query: [`RANKDIFF.md`](RANKDIFF.md).

⚠ **This says nothing about which ordering is better.** There is no key. The
number a reader should take is *"the top answer is stable across the major
version bump for 90 % of queries at 5 000 documents"*, and nothing more.

## Headroom

| direction | headroom |
|---|---|
| **improvement** | **n/a** — there is no correct answer to move toward. A benchmark with no key has no improvement direction, which is the point of not giving it one |
| **regression** | **60 of 60 queries per corpus could have changed ordering, and 31–54 did.** Latency: every one of the 6 000 measured timings was free to differ, and the arms differ systematically |

**The null control is the thing that makes these numbers readable:** arm A twice
on `docs-00100` and again on `docs-01000` returned **identical ranked lists on
every query** ([`evidence/nullcontrol-*.json`](evidence/)). Ordering is
deterministic; latency is not, and is reported as a distribution.

## 🔴 The collision, and what it cost

A second Claude session on this machine started `bench.py` with the **same
`--run 2026-09-12-l9`** at **~14:20** and again at **15:11**. Three things
happened, none of which produced a warning:

1. Its `cmd_latency` opened `rows/latency-docs-10000-scan.csv` with `"w"` and
   **truncated this sweep's 10 000-document pass mid-write**.
2. This sweep's `summarise()` then read the truncated file and printed a
   confident summary **over three queries**, formatted exactly like the ones
   over sixty.
3. For the overlap the two runs were **timing each other's CPU**, so numbers on
   both sides are wrong in that window by an unknown amount.

⚠ **The null control cannot see this.** It compares one arm to itself inside one
process; a neighbour that arrives afterwards is invisible to it.

**Fixed in the harness, in this change — two guards, because one is not enough:**

1. **`runs/<id>/.owner`** carries the writing pid; `bench.py` refuses a run
   directory a live process holds, naming the pid. Verified against a planted
   owner.
2. **Row files are opened `"x"`, not `"w"`.** The lock stops the common case;
   exclusive-create stops the case the lock cannot see — a stale `.owner`
   cleared by hand, or a lock bypassed. **Asked for by the session on the other
   side of the collision**, which is the right instinct: a guard whose own
   failure is silent is half a guard.

**Both sessions' `docs-10000` rows were deleted** — they were contaminated in
both directions and worth nothing. The other session has stopped running this
harness and disclosed the `fux-lab` load, which is how the banner at the top of
this report exists. 🔴 **That disclosure is the reason this report was corrected
after filing, and it is worth more than the numbers it invalidated.**

## Not run, and stated rather than implied

- **`--path fast`.** The accelerator was not timed at all. Time, not a decision.
  Every number here is the **scan** path, which is what `fux ask` does by
  default in both versions.
- **`docs-10000`'s query pass** — see above. The ingest and index numbers for it
  stand; nothing else does.
- **`docs-00100`'s query pass** — contaminated by this session.
- **No previous run to diff against.** `rankdiff --prev` is built and unused:
  this is the first run of this shape, so *"what moved since last time"* has no
  answer yet. That is what the kept ranked lists are for.

## Reproduce

```bash
cd ~/my_programs/fux-benchmark
python3 bin/gen_corpus.py --verify        # every corpus sha256, re-hashed
RUN=$(date +%F)-<something-unique>        # the harness now refuses a shared id
for c in docs-00200 docs-00500 docs-01000 docs-02000 docs-05000; do
  python3 bin/bench.py prepare --run $RUN --corpus $c
done
python3 bin/bench.py nullcontrol --run $RUN --corpus docs-01000   # FIRST, always
for c in docs-00200 docs-00500 docs-01000 docs-02000 docs-05000; do
  python3 bin/bench.py latency --run $RUN --corpus $c --path scan
done
python3 bin/bench.py rankdiff --run $RUN --prev 2026-09-12-benchmark-l9
```

⚠ **Wall-clock is machine-bound.** Every timing here came from one macOS/arm64
laptop in one session. The **ranked lists are deterministic** and are the half
that transfers.
