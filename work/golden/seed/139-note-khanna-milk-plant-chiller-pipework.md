# Khanna milk plant: proving the new chiller pipework before it is lagged

For the chiller room we are installing for the dairy cooperative's milk plant at Khanna, where Quillfern runs the chilled store on the cooperative's site.

## Why it matters

The new chillers sit in a cramped room behind the reception bay, and once the pipes are lagged and the cable trays are in, nobody will reach a leaking joint without taking half the room apart.

## The tightness test

Before lagging, every brazed joint is looked at by the refrigeration contractor's supervisor and by our engineer together. The system is then filled with dry nitrogen at one and a quarter times its design pressure, the cylinder is shut off, and the pressure is watched for 6 hours. It passes if the gauge has not fallen, allowing for the change in room temperature.

The cooperative's plant runs round the clock, and the chiller room cannot be closed for longer than a shift, which is why the hold is shorter here than in a new building.

## A fall

If the gauge falls, the joints are tested with soap solution one by one, the leak is repaired, and the test starts again from the beginning.

## Records

The gauge serial number, the readings at the start, after one hour and at the end, and the room temperature at each reading go on the test sheet, which both engineers sign.

## Lagging

Lagging starts only after the signed test sheet has been photographed and sent to the regional engineer.
