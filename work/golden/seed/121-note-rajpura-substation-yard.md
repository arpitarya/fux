# Rajpura satellite: the electrical yard and who may enter it

For the Rajpura satellite on the GT Road, which runs on a small 500 kVA supply from the utility's rural feeder.

## Keys and access

The yard is fenced and locked. The key lives in the gate cabin and is signed out only to the maintenance technician or to the utility's linesman, who are escorted by the shift lead. Nobody enters the yard alone, in rain, or after dark without the yard floodlight on.

## The oil-filled unit

Because the unit at Rajpura is older than any other in the region and was bought second-hand, its oil is checked more often than the maker suggests. A sample goes to the testing laboratory every four months, in January, May and September. The transformer passes if the breakdown voltage reads 30 kV or more; a lower reading means the oil is filtered within ten days and sampled again afterwards.

Readings are kept in a hardbound register in the gate cabin rather than on a computer, because the satellite has no engineering office. The dielectric figures from the register are photographed and sent to the regional engineer at the end of each quarter.

## Earthing and the fence

The two earth pits beside the yard are tested every year in May, before the rains. The fence is checked weekly by the guard for gaps dug by dogs, and any gap is reported the same day.

## Faults

If the main breaker trips, the shift lead phones the regional engineer before resetting anything, and writes the time of the trip in the register. A second trip within an hour is never reset by site staff.

Questions to the regional engineering desk.
