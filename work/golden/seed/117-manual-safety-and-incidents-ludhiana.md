# Safety and Incident Manual, Ludhiana DC

## 1. What this manual is for

1.1. Ludhiana DC employs about ninety people on two shifts, hosts contractors most days of the year, and receives up to forty vehicles in a day. Every one of those people should go home in the condition they arrived in. This manual says how the site keeps them safe, what happens when something goes wrong, and who is answerable for each part.

1.2. It is written for everyone who works on the site, whether employed by Quillfern, supplied by an agency or sent by a contractor, and for the managers who plan their work. Drivers of visiting vehicles are covered by the gate rules in section 12 and by their own employers' rules.

1.3. The manual does not replace the law. Where the Factories Act, the state rules made under it, or any other law asks for more than this manual, the law applies, and the safety officer tells the site manager what has changed.

1.4. Revision 2 replaces the safety rules issued when the site opened in 2025. Several limits in those rules were copied from another company's manual without anyone asking whether they suited a building with freezers at -25 C and a yard full of reversing trucks. This revision sets limits for this site.

## 2. Who is responsible

2.1. The site manager is responsible for safety on the site, and cannot hand that responsibility to anyone else. The safety officer, Jaspreet Dhillon, advises the site manager, inspects, trains and investigates, and has the authority to stop any work she judges unsafe.

2.2. Shift leads are responsible for safety on their shifts. They make sure that the people working for them have been trained for the job, have the right equipment and know what to do in an emergency.

2.3. Every person on site is responsible for their own safety and for the safety of the people around them. Anyone may stop a job they think is unsafe, and nobody is disciplined for stopping a job in good faith, even if it turns out to have been safe.

2.4. The site's safety council meets on the second Thursday of each month: the site manager, the safety officer, the engineering lead, two shift leads and two members of staff elected by their colleagues. Minutes are posted on the canteen board within a week.

## 3. First days on site

3.1. Every new person, including agency staff and contractors' workers, attends the site induction before starting work. The induction lasts about two hours and covers the site layout, the emergency alarm and assembly point, the vehicle routes, the cold rooms, the protective equipment and how to report a hazard.

3.2. New staff spend their first week with a named buddy who has worked on the site for at least six months. They do not work alone in a cold room, operate a forklift or work on the docks without the buddy until their shift lead has signed them off.

3.3. Contractors' workers are inducted on their first visit and carry an induction card. The card is checked at the gate on every later visit.

3.4. Visitors are escorted at all times and are given a short safety briefing at the gate. They do not enter the cold rooms, the plant room or the yard without protective equipment and an escort.

## 4. If someone collapses

4.1. A person who collapses needs help within minutes, long before an ambulance can reach the Ladhowal bypass from the city. The site keeps two defibrillator units, one in the gate cabin and one on the canteen wall, each in a marked cabinet with an alarm on its door.

4.2. A trained first aider checks each unit's status light, battery and pad expiry every week and signs the card inside the cabinet. Pads are replaced as soon as they are used or reach their date. The site has one trained first aider for every 25 people on each shift.

4.3. The 2025 rules had the check done monthly and named no ratio; both changed after a contractor's collapse in the yard in February 2026, when the nearest unit was found to have a flat battery.

4.4. Anyone may use an aed; the defibrillator speaks its instructions. It will not deliver a shock unless one is needed. The gate guard calls the ambulance while the first aider brings the nearest aed.

## 5. Protective equipment

5.1. Safety shoes and high-visibility vests are worn everywhere outside the office, the canteen and the locker rooms. Hard hats are worn on the docks, in the yard and anywhere work is going on overhead.

5.2. Freezer suits, gloves, balaclavas and boots are issued to named people for work in the frozen block. They are not shared, and a suit that no longer closes properly is exchanged at Stores the same day.

5.3. Gloves for chilled-room work, chemical gloves for cleaning and face shields for any work with dry ice are kept on the racks beside the work, not in personal lockers.

5.4. Equipment that is damaged or worn out is handed in and replaced, never repaired with tape. Stores keeps a stock of every item in every size worn on the site.

5.5. A person who turns up without their protective equipment does not start work until it is found or replaced.

## 6. Working off the ground

6.1. Falls cause more serious injuries on the site than anything else, mostly from ladders and from the tops of racking and vehicles. Work at height is avoided wherever a ground-level method exists.

6.2. Any work more than 1.8 metres above the floor, or near an unprotected edge, is done from a platform with guard rails, or with a full-body harness attached by a shock-absorbing lanyard to an anchor point rated for the purpose.

6.3. Every harness is inspected by its user before each use, against the checklist sewn into its storage bag, and by the safety officer every three months. It is withdrawn after five years from its date of manufacture, or at once after it has arrested a fall, whichever comes first.

6.4. The 2025 rules allowed a longer service life and named no height. Both were tightened after the safety officer found that two units in the plant room were older than the people wearing them.

6.5. A lanyard that has deployed its shock absorber is cut in half and binned the same day.

## 7. Forklifts and the docks

7.1. Only people holding a current site licence drive forklifts, reach trucks or powered pallet trucks. The licence is issued after a practical test by the training provider and renewed every three years.

7.2. Forklifts are checked by their drivers at the start of every shift: brakes, horn, lights, forks, chains, tyres and the seat belt. A failed check takes the truck out of use until the engineering team has repaired it.

7.3. Drivers wear the seat belt, keep the forks low when travelling, and never carry a passenger. A load that blocks the driver's view is driven in reverse.

7.4. Dock levellers and dock doors are operated only by trained staff. A vehicle is chocked and its driver's keys are in the dock office before anyone enters the trailer.

7.5. Speed limits inside the building are 8 km/h in the aisles and walking pace in the cold rooms.

## 8. When a person is hurt

8.1. Every injury, however small, is reported to the shift lead before the end of the shift and written in the accident book. First aid is given first and the paperwork done afterwards.

8.2. An injury that keeps the person away from their next shift is an lti. The safety officer tells the site manager within the hour, and the site manager notifies the state factories inspectorate in writing within 24 hours, using the form the state prescribes.

8.3. The safety officer's investigation report, with its causes and the actions agreed, is completed within seven days of the injury and copied to the operations head.

8.4. The 2025 rules gave twice as long for both, and the first serious injury of that year reached the inspectorate late.

8.5. An lti is never recorded as anything else to keep a monthly figure clean. The site's safety figures are reported honestly to the board each quarter, and the board would rather see a bad figure than a false one.

## 9. Cold work

9.1. The frozen block at Ludhiana runs at -25 C, colder than most of the company's freezers, and the body loses heat there faster than people expect. Cold slows thinking before it slows hands, which is why the rules below do not depend on how anyone feels.

9.2. Nobody works inside the frozen block for longer than 40 minutes at a stretch, even in a full freezer suit. A 20-minute rewarming break in the canteen or the warm room follows each stretch, with a hot drink available.

9.3. The shift lead keeps the break board for the frozen block and moves names on it; the workers do not keep their own time.

9.4. Signs of hypothermia, such as uncontrolled shivering, slurred speech, clumsiness or confusion, mean the person leaves the cold at once with a colleague and is checked by a first aider. A person who has shown those signs does not go back into the cold that shift.

9.5. In 2025 the stretch was an hour and the rewarming break fifteen minutes, and two cases of mild hypothermia followed; both were changed after the first winter.

## 10. Noise

10.1. The plant room, the generator enclosure and parts of the dock area are loud enough to damage hearing over time. Levels are surveyed with a decibel meter by the safety officer every year and after any new machine is installed, and the results are posted at each entrance.

10.2. Hearing protection is compulsory for everyone, however short the visit, wherever the survey shows a level of 85 or more. Signs mark those areas, and dispensers of earmuffs hang at their doors.

10.3. People who work regularly in those areas wear earmuffs for the whole shift and have a hearing test every 12 months, arranged by HR with an occupational health provider.

10.4. The 2025 rules set the line higher and the tests further apart.

10.5. A person whose test shows a change is told the result in person and moved away from the loudest work while the provider advises. Results are kept by HR, and a decibel reading in a survey is never treated as a personal medical record.

## 11. Feet and wheels

11.1. Most deaths in warehouse yards are caused by reversing vehicles. The yard at Ludhiana is laid out so that pedestrian and vehicle routes meet as rarely as possible, and where they must meet, the vehicle waits.

11.2. People on foot keep to a painted route 1.2 metres wide, coloured green and separated from the roadway by yellow barriers along the dock apron. Vehicles keep to 10 km/h anywhere in the yard.

11.3. The 2025 rules had a narrower walkway and a higher limit, and the yard was busier than anyone had planned for.

11.4. Every reversing manoeuvre onto a dock after dark is guided by a trained banksman standing where the driver can see him, and clear of the vehicle's path.

11.5. Nobody stands or walks behind a reversing vehicle, and no pedestrian crosses the yard except at the marked crossings at the end of each walkway.

## 12. The gate

12.1. Every vehicle is logged in and out at the gate cabin with its registration, the driver's name and licence number, and the purpose of the visit.

12.2. Drivers who are not unloading wait in the drivers' rest room, not in the yard or on the docks. They are given the site's one-page safety card in Punjabi, Hindi or English at the gate.

12.3. A driver who appears to be under the influence of alcohol or drugs is not admitted, and his company is told. Fleet Control is told if the vehicle is on a Quillfern dispatch.

12.4. Vehicles are searched on leaving if the site manager has asked for searches that day. The search is done by two guards, with the driver present.

## 13. Fire and evacuation

13.1. The fire alarm is a continuous bell. On hearing it, everyone stops work, switches off any vehicle or machine they are using, and leaves by the nearest way out to the assembly point by the weighbridge.

13.2. Shift leads count their people against the shift roster and the gate register and report to the site manager or, at night, the senior guard. Contractors are counted by their supervisors and visitors by their escorts.

13.3. Nobody goes back into the building until the fire service or the site manager says it is safe. Stock, vehicles and personal belongings are left where they are.

13.4. Fire drills are held every three months, at least one of them at night each year. The time to clear the building and to complete the count is recorded and compared with the previous drill.

13.5. Fire extinguishers, hose reels, alarms and emergency lighting are inspected monthly by the safety officer and serviced yearly by the fire contractor.

## 14. Near misses and hazards

14.1. A near miss is anything that could have hurt someone but did not. Near misses are reported on the yellow cards kept beside every clocking machine, or by telling the shift lead, who writes the card.

14.2. The safety officer reads every card within two working days, decides what action is needed and posts the result on the canteen board, without the reporter's name unless they asked for it to be shown.

14.3. A person who reports a near miss is thanked, not blamed. Shift leads who discourage reporting are themselves reported to the site manager.

14.4. The number of near-miss cards is reported to the safety council each month. A falling number is investigated as carefully as a rising one.

## 15. Health at work

15.1. Every person on site has a pre-employment health check and a yearly check thereafter, arranged by HR. People working in the cold or in noisy areas have the extra checks described in sections 9 and 10.

15.2. Drinking water is available at every work area, and in summer the canteen serves salted lime water on the docks during the afternoon.

15.3. Anyone who is unwell tells their shift lead and is sent home or to the first-aid room. Nobody is asked to work through an illness to meet a delivery.

15.4. Staff may speak to the occupational health provider in confidence about anything that affects their health at work. The provider tells the site only what the person agrees to.

## 16. Records and review

16.1. The accident book, the near-miss cards, investigation reports, inspection records, survey results and training records are kept for at least ten years, or for longer where the law requires.

16.2. The safety officer reviews this manual every June with the safety council, and after any serious injury or dangerous occurrence on the site.

16.3. Each revision is explained at a briefing on every shift, and every person on site signs that they attended.

## 17. Chemicals and cleaning

17.1. Cryogenic liquid in the room that holds Vetrix Animal Health's frozen breeding stock, cleaning chemicals, chlorine for washing vehicle bodies, battery acid for the forklift charging bay and the water-treatment chemicals in the plant room are the hazardous substances most people on the site will meet. Each is listed in the chemical register kept by the safety officer, with its safety data sheet and the protective equipment it needs.

17.2. Chemicals are stored in their labelled original containers in the chemical store or the plant room, never decanted into drinks bottles or unlabelled cans. Incompatible chemicals, such as chlorine and acids, are stored on separate shelves with a bund under each.

17.3. Spill kits hang beside the chemical store, the charging bay and the wash bay. A spill is contained with the kit, reported to the shift lead and cleaned up by trained staff; nobody washes a chemical spill into a drain.

17.4. Battery charging is done only in the charging bay, which is ventilated and has an eyewash station. Nobody smokes, eats or uses a phone in the bay.

## 18. Working alone and at night

18.1. The site works two shifts, but some jobs, such as plant checks and security rounds, are done by one person at night. Lone work is allowed only where the safety officer has assessed the job and written down how the lone worker is checked on.

18.2. A lone worker carries a radio and checks in with the gate cabin every hour. A missed check-in is followed up at once by the senior guard, who goes to the worker's last known location with a second person.

18.3. Nobody works alone in a cold room, in the plant room or at height, at any hour.

18.4. Staff who finish late are offered the site's transport to the city or a taxi paid by the company. Nobody is expected to walk to the bypass after dark.

## 19. Contractors on site

19.1. Contractors work under a permit for any job with a significant hazard: electrical work, hot work, work at height, work in a confined space and work on the refrigeration plant. The permit is issued by the shift lead after the job has been discussed on site with the contractor's supervisor.

19.2. Contractors bring their own tools and protective equipment, which must be in good condition and suitable for the job. Equipment that is not is turned away at the gate.

19.3. A contractor who breaks a site rule is stopped and asked to leave, and the firm is told the reason in writing. A firm whose workers repeatedly break rules is reported to Procurement.

19.4. Contractors report injuries and near misses on the site in the same way as staff, and their reports are counted in the site's figures.
