# Last-Mile Programme Manual

The manual for the doorstep insulin service Quillfern runs for Nirmaya
Healthcare in Pune, which Nirmaya sells to its patients under the name CareLink.
It is written for the riders, the hub staff and the customer service agents who
book the drops. It covers what is particular to taking medicine to a person's
front door on a two-wheeler. Company rules on temperature, quarantine and
destruction still apply; this manual says how they are carried out when the
last stop is a flat, not a dock.

## 1. What the programme does

1.1. Nirmaya Healthcare runs a home-care service for people with diabetes who
find it hard to collect insulin from a pharmacy. Quillfern holds Nirmaya's
insulin stock in Pune and takes each patient's order to the door on the day and
in the slot the patient booked.

1.2. The programme started on 1 August 2025 with nine riders and about 300 drops
a week. By April 2026 it had 26 riders and about 1,100 drops a week across Pune
and Pimpri-Chinchwad.

1.3. Only insulin pens, cartridges and vials are carried. Vaccines and other
cold medicines are not carried unless Nirmaya adds them by a written change
signed by both sides, and no such change has been signed.

1.4. Quillfern does not advise patients on their medicine. A patient who asks a
rider how much to take, or whether a pen is the right one, is given the Nirmaya
care-desk number printed on the delivery slip.

1.5. Nirmaya owns the stock and the patient relationship. Quillfern owns the
cold chain from the hub to the door, and is answerable for it.

## 2. The micro-hub

2.1. The Kothrud micro-hub is a ground-floor unit off Paud Road with a covered
loading lane for two-wheelers and a packing room behind the shutter.

2.2. Stock at Kothrud sits in MCR-4, taken on long hire from the engineering pool. The hub staff do not service it; the engineering pool's own people do,
on their own calendar.

2.3. The packing room holds the packing bench, the carrier rack and PF-K1, the
fridge in which carrier packs are conditioned before use. PF-K1 is not used to
store insulin, even for an hour.

2.4. The hub is open from 06:30 to 22:00 every day. It is staffed by a hub lead
and two packers on each of two shifts.

2.5. Nirmaya's pharmacist visits the hub every working morning to check stock
received from Nirmaya's distributor and to collect anything held for her.

2.6. The hub lead keeps the keys to the store, the packing room and the red
box. Keys are handed over at the shift change against a signature in the key
book.

## 3. The people on two wheels

3.1. Riders are employed by Quillfern on the programme. They are not gig
contractors, and they are not paid per drop alone.

3.2. Each rider works a shift of up to nine hours on the road, with two breaks.
The hub lead keeps the shift sheet and does not extend a shift to clear a
backlog.

3.3. The evening is where pressure builds, because patients book after work. No run starts after 21:30, whatever is left in the queue. Orders still waiting
at 21:30 are re-slotted for the next morning and the patient is called by the
hub lead before 22:00.

3.4. Riders wear the Quillfern jacket and helmet on every run and carry the
programme ID card. A rider without an ID card does not leave the hub.

3.5. Riders do not carry cash. Patients pay Nirmaya directly; a patient who
offers money to a rider is told politely that the rider cannot take it.

3.6. A rider who feels unwell, or who has been in a fall, stops riding for the
day. Another rider or the hub lead completes the run, or the patients are
re-slotted.

## 4. What goes in the box

4.1. Each rider carries an IB-6 insulated carrier fitted to the rear rack of the
two-wheeler. Inside the carrier are two conditioned packs from PF-K1 and the
patients' orders, each in its own sealed pouch with the patient's slip.

4.2. Packs go in the pattern printed inside the lid, with the pouches between
them, never touching a pack directly.

4.3. The carrier's clock starts when the lid is shut at the hub, and the hub
lead writes that time on the run sheet. A pack still undelivered at the end of hour five comes back to Kothrud, because the carrier is only proven for six.

4.4. The turn-back hour depends on the carrier. With the IB-4 model it was four, until the last of those were withdrawn on 2 March 2026.

4.5. Each carrier has a single-use freeze indicator taped inside the lid. A
tripped indicator means everything in the carrier comes back, delivered or not
being irrelevant to that decision.

4.6. Carriers are cleaned with a dry cloth at the end of each run and are never
washed with water while warm. A carrier with a split seam or a cracked lid is
withdrawn the same day.

## 5. Booking a slot

5.1. Nirmaya's care desk books each order into a two-hour slot between 07:00 and
21:00, through the programme portal. The booking names the patient, the address,
the slot and the products.

5.2. The patient receives an SMS with the slot and a one-time code the evening
before, and a second SMS when the rider leaves the hub.

5.3. At the door the rider asks for the OTP. The patient reads out the four-figure code sent by SMS; without it nothing is handed over.

5.4. A patient who has lost the SMS is asked to call the Nirmaya care desk, which
can resend the code. The rider does not accept a name, a photograph or a
neighbour's word instead.

5.5. Slots that the hub cannot meet are moved by the hub lead, never by the
rider, and the patient is called before the original slot begins.

5.6. Nirmaya may book a same-day slot only before 13:00. Same-day bookings after
13:00 go into the next morning.

## 6. The last ten metres

6.1. The rider parks where the two-wheeler is safe and visible, takes the pouch
from the carrier, shuts the lid at once and walks to the door. The carrier is
never left open while the rider is away from it.

6.2. Patients do not always hear the bell. The rider waits ten minutes and rings twice, the second call at least five minutes after the first. If there is
still no answer the pouch goes back into the carrier, the rider tells the hub,
and the hub lead calls the patient to re-slot.

6.3. Insulin is never left with anyone else unless the order itself names an alternate receiver. A neighbour, a watchman or the shop next door is not an
alternate receiver because they offer, however well the rider knows them.

6.4. An alternate receiver named on the order gives the same OTP as the patient
would. The rider writes the receiver's name on the slip.

6.5. The rider never enters a flat. Pouches are handed over at the door, and a
patient who cannot come to the door is visited by Nirmaya's care team, not by
the rider.

6.6. The rider photographs the pouch at the door, never the patient and never
the inside of the home, and uploads the photograph before moving off.

## 7. Things that come back

7.1. Pouches come back to the hub when the patient is not reached, when the OTP
is not given, when the carrier's clock runs out, or when the freeze indicator
has tripped.

7.2. Undelivered pouches are logged at the hub on the returns sheet with the
reason and the time. They are held in the red box beside the store door and collected by the customer's own pharmacist the next working day; none goes back into saleable stock.

7.3. The red box is not refrigerated storage for future drops. Nothing is taken
out of it by hub staff once it has gone in.

7.4. A pouch that has been opened by anyone outside the hub is treated the same
as an undelivered one, and the rider writes on the returns sheet who opened it.

7.5. Empty carriers and packs come back on every run. A rider who loses a pack
reports it the same shift.

## 8. Weather and the road

8.1. Riders do not ride in a red weather warning. The hub lead watches the
warnings and stops runs before they start.

8.2. In the monsoon every carrier wears its rain cover, and riders carry a
spare cover in the under-seat box.

8.3. In April and May the midday heat is hard on riders and on carriers. Runs
are planned so that no carrier is in the sun at a long stop, and riders carry
drinking water.

8.4. Riders keep to the speed limits of the roads they ride on and never ride
on a footpath to reach a door.

8.5. A road closure or a rally that blocks a route is reported to the hub, and
the hub lead re-plans the run; the rider does not find a way round on his own.

## 9. Pay

9.1. Riders are paid a monthly wage for their shifts, with a small per-drop
addition. The addition is the same for every drop and is not tied to time.

9.2. There is no payment for early arrival and no penalty for a drop that comes
back because the patient was out.

9.3. Fuel for the two-wheelers is paid by Quillfern through a fuel card. Riders
do not pay for fuel from their own money.

9.4. Overtime is paid at the company rate and approved by the hub lead before
the shift is extended, not afterwards.

## 10. Privacy

10.1. Patient names, addresses and phone numbers are held in the programme
portal only. Riders see an order's details on the hub phone for the run, and
the phone is handed back at the end of the shift.

10.2. Riders do not save patients' numbers in their own phones and do not talk
about patients outside the hub.

10.3. Delivery photographs show the pouch and the door number, nothing else.
They are deleted from the hub phone after upload.

10.4. Nirmaya may audit the programme's records about its own patients on
reasonable notice. No other customer sees them.

## 11. Complaints from the person at home

11.1. A patient who complains to a rider is given the Nirmaya care-desk number.
The rider writes down what was said and tells the hub lead before the end of the
shift.

11.2. Complaints that reach Quillfern directly are passed to Nirmaya's care desk
the same day, with the rider's note and the run sheet.

11.3. A complaint about a rider's behaviour or riding goes to the hub lead and
to HR. The rider is told what the complaint says and is asked for his account
before anything is decided.

11.4. Quillfern does not reply to a patient in writing. Written replies come
from Nirmaya.

## 12. Training

12.1. A new rider spends three days riding with an experienced rider before
riding alone. On the third day the new rider leads and the experienced rider
watches.

12.2. Training covers the carrier, the pack pattern, the OTP, the photograph,
the returns sheet, the red box, and what to say to a patient who asks about
medicine.

12.3. Every rider repeats the carrier and returns training each January.

12.4. Hub packers are trained by the hub lead on PF-K1, the pack pattern and the
run sheet before they pack alone.

## 13. Bikes and kit care

13.1. The two-wheelers are leased and serviced by the leasing company every
3,000 km. The hub lead books the service.

13.2. The rider checks tyres, brakes, lights and the carrier rack before every
shift and writes the check on the shift sheet.

13.3. A two-wheeler with a fault is not ridden. The hub keeps one spare machine
for that reason.

13.4. Helmets are replaced after any fall, whether or not they look damaged.

## 14. Changes to this manual

14.1. Revision 1 (August 2025) started the programme with IB-4 carriers. Revision
2 (April 2026) moved the programme to IB-6 carriers and changed the turn-back
rule to match.

14.2. The programme lead reviews this manual every six months with Nirmaya.

14.3. Riders and hub staff send suggested changes to the hub lead at any time.
