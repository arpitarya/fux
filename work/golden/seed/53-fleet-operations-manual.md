# Fleet Operations Manual

The working manual for Fleet Control, fleet planners, owned-reefer drivers and
the managers of hired-truck vendors. It says how the fleet is run day to day. It
does not restate the driver hours limits, the temperature limits or the dock
booking rules; those have their own documents, and this manual points to them.

## 1. What this manual covers

1.1. The manual covers owned reefers, hired reefers, support vans and escort
cars moving under a Quillfern dispatch note, from the moment a vehicle is taken
over at a DC until it is handed back with its paperwork.

1.2. It does not cover warehouse work inside the cold rooms, customer contracts,
or pricing. A driver who is asked a price by a customer refers the customer to
customer service.

1.3. Revision 3 of July 2026 added section 9 on passive shippers in transit and
moved the police-check rules into section 7.

1.4. Where the manual and the Driver Hours and Road Safety Policy seem to
differ, the policy applies. Where the manual and the SOP seem to differ on
anything about temperature, the SOP applies.

1.5. Copies of the manual are kept at the Fleet Control desk in Nagpur, in the
gate cabin at each DC and in the driver rest room at Nagpur. Drivers are not
expected to carry it; the cab card carries what a driver needs on the road.

## 2. The Fleet Control desk

2.1. Fleet Control sits at Nagpur DC and answers extension 301 at all hours. At
night one controller is on duty, with the Fleet head's deputy on call.

2.2. Fleet Control owns the dispatch note, the vehicle's route and the driver's
duty plan. A shift lead can hold a vehicle at the gate, but only Fleet Control
can change a route or a driver.

2.3. Every call from a driver is logged in the driver call log with the time,
the place and what was said, including calls that come to nothing. The paper
log is kept alongside the Tessaline record because network coverage is patchy
on several lanes.

2.4. Fleet Control does not pass customer pressure to a driver. A customer who
wants a location or an arrival time asks customer service, and customer service
asks Fleet Control.

2.5. Each controller hands over at 08:00 and 20:00, with every open exception,
every vehicle on the road with a pharma critical load, and every vehicle off
road for repair named aloud.

## 3. Planning a week of duty

3.1. Planners build the week's duty on Thursday for the following Monday to
Sunday. Each driver's weekly rest is placed first, then night segments, then
day runs.

3.2. The limits the plan must respect are in the Driver Hours and Road Safety
Policy. A plan that meets them only if nothing goes wrong does not meet them.

3.3. A lane with a qualification record is planned with that record open, so
that its relief point, its pre-cool hold time and any two-driver condition are
on the plan and not in someone's memory.

3.4. Festival shuttle rosters with split rest are planned only after the Fleet
head has approved the roster in writing, and only for the festival named.

3.5. The plan is published to drivers by Friday noon. A driver who sees a
problem with his week tells Fleet Control on Friday, not on the morning of the
run.

3.6. Leave requests go to the planner by the 20th of the previous month. Late
leave is granted where cover exists, and cover is never found by stretching
another driver's hours.

## 4. Taking over a vehicle

4.1. The driver taking over a vehicle walks round it with the driver handing
over, or with the shift lead if the vehicle was parked empty.

4.2. The walk-round covers tyres and wheel nuts, lights, mirrors, the reefer
unit's display and alarm light, the rear door hasp, the cab locker and the red
sleeve, the fire extinguisher, and the wheel chocks.

4.3. Any damage found on the walk-round is photographed and written on the
takeover sheet before the vehicle moves. Damage found later is presumed to have
happened on the later driver's watch.

4.4. The cab card is checked for the current Fleet Control, QA desk and
Compliance-Red numbers. A cab card printed before the Tessaline cutover is
replaced before the vehicle leaves.

4.5. The driver checks that the Tessaline unit shows the vehicle's own asset
number. Units have been swapped between vehicles in the workshop and not
re-labelled.

## 5. Diesel, cards and the reefer tank

5.1. Every owned reefer carries one fuel card for the truck and one for the
reefer unit, in separate sleeves. The two are billed to different cost centres
and must not be swapped.

5.2. Drivers fill only at Quillfern-listed pumps. The list is printed on the
back of the cab card and updated each quarter by Fleet Control.

5.3. A reefer unit whose fuel falls below one quarter is filled at the next
listed pump before any other stop, including a customer drop.

5.4. The truck's own tank follows the dispatch checklist at departure and is
otherwise the driver's judgement, with the rule that no vehicle is allowed to
run so low that it must stop somewhere unlit.

5.5. Fuel slips are photographed at the pump and the photograph is sent to
Fleet Control while parked. The paper slip goes into the trip bag.

5.6. A card that is refused at a listed pump is reported to Fleet Control, and
the driver waits for Fleet Control to authorise a payment. Drivers do not pay
for diesel from their own money.

## 6. Roadside trouble

6.1. A driver whose vehicle develops a fault stops at a safe place first and
calls second. A lit fuel plaza, a toll rest area or a police post is a safe
place; a road shoulder at night is not, unless the vehicle cannot be moved.

6.2. Roadside tyre changes on a loaded reefer are done by the listed tyre
vendor, never by the driver alone, and Fleet Control expects the vendor within
3 hours on a qualified lane.

6.3. A reefer unit fault is reported with the display reading, the alarm code
and the time. Fleet Control decides whether to send the mobile service van,
transfer the load, or bring the vehicle in. The driver does not open the reefer
unit's panel.

6.4. A breakdown on a lane where the fuel pumps are a quarter of a day apart
makes the wait long. Drivers on such lanes carry water and a torch, and the
mobile service van is not always the quickest help; sometimes the relief point
vendor on the lane arrives first.

6.5. A vehicle that cannot be moved and is carrying pharma critical stock is
reported to Compliance-Red by Fleet Control within the SOP's notification
window, whatever the cause.

6.6. After any roadside repair, the vehicle is walked round again before it
moves, as if it were being taken over.

## 7. When the doors are opened on the road

7.1. Police, excise, sales tax and transport officers may stop a loaded reefer
and ask to see the load. Drivers cooperate, and do not argue about cold chain
at the roadside.

7.2. The driver asks for the officer's name and badge number and writes both on
the back of the dispatch note, with the time and place. The driver photographs
the seal before it is cut, if the officer allows.

7.3. After the inspection the driver fits a fresh bolt seal from the road pack
of five kept in the cab locker, writes the new number beside the old one on the
dispatch note, and photographs it. The cut seal goes into the trip bag.

7.4. The driver then phones Fleet Control on extension 301, while parked, and
Fleet Control enters both seal numbers in QCL-F-35 against the trip.

7.5. If the inspection kept the doors open for more than a few minutes on a
pharma critical load, Fleet Control tells QA, and the minutes are part of the
temperature record like any other door opening.

7.6. The receiver at the destination is told in advance by Fleet Control that
the seal was changed on the road, so that the number mismatch is not treated as
a tamper.

## 8. Carbon dioxide on board

8.1. Dry ice may be put into a frozen body only at a Quillfern DC dock or in a
customer's secure yard with Fleet Control's approval. No dry ice is added to a
body between DCs, at a relief point, at a toll plaza or at a fuel stop.

8.2. A frozen body running warm on the road is driven to the nearest Quillfern
DC or approved customer yard, within the driver's remaining hours, and the load
is protected there.

8.3. Dry ice never travels in the cab, whatever the distance. A driver who is
handed pellets for the cab refuses them.

8.4. A driver who opens a body in which dry ice has been placed stands back from
the open doors and keeps others back until the air has cleared.

8.5. Dry ice put into a body at a dock is written on the dispatch note with the
kg, so that the receiver knows to open the doors with care.

## 9. Insulated boxes in the load

9.1. A validated passive shipper carries pharma stock inside a reefer body or on
a body without a cradle, such as RF-119.

9.2. A closed shipper is validated for 48 hours from the lid-closing time
written in the shipper register. A shipper that will still be on the road at 40
hours is called in to Fleet Control, and QA decides whether it is re-packed or
the consignment is held.

9.3. The shipper's lid is never opened on the road. The tamper label across the
lid seam is photographed at departure and again at delivery.

9.4. Shippers travel upright, strapped to the body wall, and never under other
freight.

9.5. The empty shipper comes back on the return run in the same condition it
went out, with its packs inside and its logger taped in place, so that QA can
download the logger and count the pack cycles.

## 10. Hired trucks and their drivers

10.1. A hired-truck vendor is on the approved list before the first booking, and
each of its drivers signs the policy acknowledgement at the gate before loading.

10.2. Hired trucks carry their own fuel. Quillfern fuel cards are never given to
hired drivers.

10.3. Hired trucks are sealed with cable seals. Their drivers are shown the seal
routine at the gate by the shift lead.

10.4. A hired truck without a working temperature display is not loaded with
cold-chain stock, whatever the vendor promises about a repair on the way.

10.5. Vendors are reviewed every quarter on arrival times, refused loads,
exceptions and any safety concern raised by a DC.

## 11. Crashes, near misses and police stops

11.1. After any crash, however small, the driver makes the vehicle and the road
safe, calls emergency services if anyone is hurt, and then calls Fleet Control.

11.2. The driver does not move the vehicle unless the police tell him to or it
is a danger where it stands. Photographs of the scene are taken if it is safe.

11.3. The driver is relieved from further driving until Fleet Control records a
fitness-to-continue decision, as the policy requires.

11.4. A near miss is reported the same day, in the driver's own words. Near-miss
reports are not used to discipline the driver who reports them.

11.5. A police stop that is not a crash and not an inspection of the load, such
as a document check, is noted in the driver call log with the time and place.

## 12. Paperwork that comes back

12.1. Each trip returns a trip bag holding the dispatch note, the fuel slips,
the cut seals, the takeover sheet and any exception form.

12.2. The trip bag is handed to the shift lead at the returning DC, who checks
it against the dispatch note and signs for it.

12.3. A missing item in the trip bag is written on the dispatch note as
missing, never replaced with a guess. A torn fuel slip is kept torn.

12.4. Trip bags from a trip involving a tyre change or other vendor work carry
the vendor's job sheet with the hours and the spare parts used, so that Fleet
Control can match it to the vendor's invoice.

12.5. Trip bags are kept by Fleet Control for three years, or seven where the
trip involved a pharma critical excursion, a crash, an insurance claim or a
police matter.

## 13. Cabs, bodies and how they look

13.1. A Quillfern cab is kept clean enough that a customer's receiving clerk can
sit in it to sign papers. Food wrappers, bottles and bedding are cleared out at
the end of every duty day.

13.2. Nothing is stuck to the windscreen except the permits the law requires and
the Quillfern asset number. Stickers, pictures and hanging charms that block the
driver's view are removed at the takeover walk-round.

13.3. The outside of the body is washed at least once a week, and after any run
through a construction zone or mud. A body whose company markings cannot be read
is washed before its next customer drop.

13.4. Scratches and dents are reported, not painted over. Fleet Control books
body repairs in batches so that no vehicle is off the road longer than it needs
to be.

13.5. The cab card, the red sleeve and the fire extinguisher each have a fixed
place in the cab. A driver who finds any of them moved puts it back and tells
Fleet Control, because the next driver will look for it in its fixed place in a
hurry.

## 14. Training and coaching for drivers

14.1. Every new owned-reefer driver rides two supervised runs with an experienced
driver before driving alone. One of the two is a night run.

14.2. The supervised runs cover the cab card, the takeover walk-round, the seal
routine, the red sleeve, the gate checklist for pharma runs and the way to speak
to Fleet Control on the phone. The experienced driver signs the new driver's
training sheet at the end of each run.

14.3. Telematics coaching calls are held within two working days of an alert, as
the policy says. The coach asks what happened before saying anything about what
should have happened.

14.4. Drivers who complete a year with no safety non-conformance receive a
certificate from the Fleet head, presented at the Nagpur driver meeting. The
certificate carries no money and is not tied to arrival times.

14.5. The Nagpur driver meeting is held on the last Saturday of each month in
the driver rest room. Attendance is paid duty time. Drivers on the road that day
receive the notes on their phone.

## 15. Route changes and diversions

15.1. A driver does not change the planned route on his own judgement unless the
road ahead is closed or unsafe. He stops, calls Fleet Control, and follows the
diversion Fleet Control gives.

15.2. Fleet Control checks any diversion against the lane record for that trip,
because a diversion may take the vehicle through a dead zone or past no safe
stopping place.

15.3. A diversion that adds a toll, a ghat road or a city centre is written on
the dispatch note with the reason.

15.4. Customer service is told of any diversion that will change the arrival
window, and tells the customer. The driver does not tell the customer himself.

15.5. Festivals, elections and large public events close roads without much
notice. Planners check the week's public calendar on Thursday when they build
the plan, and Fleet Control checks it again each morning.

## 16. Tolls and road charges

16.1. Every owned reefer carries an electronic toll tag registered to Quillfern.
The tag balance is topped up by Fleet Control; drivers never pay a toll in cash
unless the tag reader at the plaza has failed.

16.2. A cash toll is paid only against a printed receipt, which goes into the
trip bag. A handwritten receipt is accepted only when the plaza staff sign and
stamp it.

16.3. A tag that shows a low balance at a plaza is reported to Fleet Control
while parked, and Fleet Control tops it up before the next plaza.

16.4. Hired trucks use their own tags. Quillfern does not refund a hired truck's
tolls unless the purchase order says so.

16.5. Road charges at state borders and permit fees are paid by Fleet Control in
advance for owned reefers. A driver asked for money at a border that was not on
the dispatch note calls Fleet Control before paying anything.

## 17. Weather and the seasons

17.1. From June to September the monsoon closes ghat sections and floods low
roads on the Nagpur lanes. Fleet Control reads the weather warnings every
morning and every evening and marks any lane under a red warning on the board.

17.2. A vehicle is not dispatched into a red-warning area unless the customer's
stock cannot wait and the Fleet head has agreed in writing. A late delivery is a
customer problem; a vehicle in a flooded underpass is a Quillfern one.

17.3. Fog on the Nashik and Sinnar roads in December and January slows every run.
Planners add time to those runs in the plan rather than asking drivers to make it
up.

17.4. In April and May the midday heat on the Vidarbha roads is hard on drivers
as well as on reefer units. Planners avoid midday departures on long runs in
those months where the customer's window allows, and every cab carries drinking
water.

17.5. After any storm, drivers report fallen trees, washed-out roads and closed
bridges to Fleet Control, which passes them to the other drivers on the same
lane.

17.6. Guwahati's monsoon runs from June to September and Coimbatore's heavy rain
comes from October to December. Fleet Control plans each DC's runs by its own
season, not by the Nagpur calendar.

## 18. Review of this manual

18.1. The Fleet head reviews this manual every April, alongside the annual review
of the Driver Hours and Road Safety Policy, and sooner after any crash or serious
exception that shows a gap in it.

18.2. Drivers and controllers send suggested changes to Fleet Control at any
time. Suggestions are read at the next Nagpur driver meeting.

18.3. A revised manual replaces every earlier copy at the Fleet Control desk, the
gate cabins and the driver rest room on the day it is issued.
