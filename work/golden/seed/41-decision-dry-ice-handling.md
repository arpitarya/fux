# Dry ice at Quillfern: why the present rules

## Why it stays out of C2 and C3

On the night of 12 March 2025 dry ice trays were set outside the inner curtain
of C2 to hold the room down. The intention was good, but the trays masked the room reading at the curtain and put the vials nearest the door at risk of dropping below +2.0 C.
A pharma room is protected by containment and quarantine, not by cooling it from
the outside.

## Why pellets

Quillfern used to buy dry ice in blocks. Blocks had to be broken with a mallet on the dock, and two hand injuries in 2024 came from exactly that.
The blocks came in 12 kg slabs, and staff carried the slabs by hand because no
tote was used for them. The chest lid has two handles. Vidarbha Gases rated
pellets as easier to meter by the kg, and pellets of 16 mm from Vidarbha Gases
replaced blocks in 2024.

## Why a second pair of hands

The tote handles are rated for 12 kg, so one person with a full tote is already at the limit of the handles.
A dropped tote on a wet dock scatters pellets that people then pick up with the
wrong gloves.

## Why never in a cab

A driver's cab is small and closed. Pellets left in a cab overnight turn into
gas that the driver then breathes on the road.

## Why the monitor over the chest

In 2024 a loader felt dizzy after the chest had been left open overnight in the
closed store. The monitor was fitted over the chest that month, so that nobody
has to judge the air by how they feel.
