# East Coast Region Operating Plan 2026-28

## 1. Why the east coast

1.1. Quillfern has grown along an arc from the west into the centre of the country, and its customers have asked for years to be served on the east coast as well. Three things decided the matter in 2025. A shrimp exporter in the Godavari delta offered a five-year volume commitment if we would hold its frozen stock near the port. A vaccine maker opening a fill-finish line near Bhubaneswar asked for a cold distribution partner inside the state. And the growth of organised food retail in Andhra Pradesh and Odisha meant that frozen and chilled lanes we were already being asked to quote would pay for themselves.

1.2. This plan describes the region we are building: one distribution centre at Visakhapatnam, which people on the ground call Vizag, and one inland satellite at Bhubaneswar. It covers the calendar, space, people, power, weather, port work, fleet, money and the measures by which the region will be judged in its first two years.

1.3. It is written for the region's own managers, for the head-office teams who support them, and for the board, which approved the investment in March 2026. It replaces the outline written in 2025, which was prepared before the site was chosen.

1.4. Company-wide procedures apply in the region from the first day. Nothing in this plan relaxes a temperature rule, a driving rule or a quality rule because the region is new.

## 2. The two sites

2.1. The Visakhapatnam DC stands on a leased plot of 4.2 acres in the Atchutapuram industrial area, south-west of the city, about 18 km by road from the port's outer gate. The building is a new pre-engineered shed with a cold block, a frozen block, a small controlled-room-temperature store, a dispatch canopy and a two-storey office.

2.2. The satellite at Bhubaneswar is a smaller leased unit in the Mancheswar industrial estate. It has two chilled docks, two frozen docks, a staging hall held at +4 C and a site office. It is a cross-dock, not a store.

2.3. The two sites are about 440 km apart by the coastal highway. A nightly trunk run links them.

2.4. The DC is the region's centre: its manager is the senior manager in the region, its QA lead covers both sites, and its engineering team maintains the satellite's plant.

2.5. Both leases run for fifteen years with a right to renew. The landlord at Atchutapuram built the shell to our drawings; the fit-out, racking and refrigeration belong to Quillfern.

## 3. Customers we are building for

3.1. Godavari Aqua Exports is the anchor for frozen work. It processes shrimp at two plants near Kakinada and exports through Visakhapatnam. It has committed to an average of 900 pallets a month in storage from the first full month of operation.

3.2. Kalinga Biologicals is the anchor for pharma work. Its fill-finish line near Bhubaneswar starts commercial production in the second quarter of 2027. Until then it will send stock from its western plant through the DC.

3.3. Rushikonda Dairy, a cooperative with a growing ice-cream line, is the first chilled and frozen retail customer. Two retail chains are in negotiation and are not counted in the volumes below.

3.4. No customer is promised space it has not contracted for. Sales may quote, but the region head confirms any commitment above 100 pallets before it is signed.

3.5. Customers are told plainly that the satellite does not store goods.

## 4. The calendar

4.1. Civil works at Atchutapuram finished in June 2026. Racking, refrigeration and the electrical fit-out run through the monsoon, with commissioning in October and qualification of the rooms in November.

4.2. The DC receives its first customer stock on 1 December 2026. The date is fixed by Godavari Aqua's contract and it will not move later without the customer's written agreement. Earlier receipts are refused, however much space is ready, because the rooms will not yet have finished qualification.

4.3. The satellite at Bhubaneswar starts cross-dock work on 1 February 2027, eight weeks after the DC, so that the trunk run can be proven with the DC already working.

4.4. Full volume is planned for the end of the first quarter of 2027. From then until the end of 2028, growth is planned in steps tied to signed contracts, not to forecasts.

4.5. Each milestone has a named owner on the project board kept by the region head, and the board is reviewed every Monday.

## 5. Racking and rooms

5.1. The DC has three temperature zones. The chilled block at +2 to +8 C holds 2,400 pallet positions in selective racking, split into a pharma room of 600 and a food room of 1,800. The frozen block at −22 C holds 1,600 pallet positions in drive-in racking. The controlled-room-temperature store holds 300.

5.2. The frozen block is built so that a second chamber of the same size can be added on the east side without stopping the first. That expansion is not approved and is not in this plan's figures.

5.3. The pharma room has its own anteroom and its own door to the dispatch canopy, so that pharma picking never shares a door with food.

5.4. Every room is temperature-mapped before first stock and again after its first summer. The mapping follows the company method.

5.5. Floor space in the dispatch canopy is for staging only. Nothing waits there longer than it takes to load.

## 6. Hiring

6.1. The DC starts with 72 people on the payroll on the day of first receipt: a site manager, a QA lead and two QA officers, an engineering lead and five technicians, six shift leads, forty-two warehouse staff, eight fleet and gate staff, and seven office staff for customer service, finance and HR. Contract security and cleaning are not counted.

6.2. The satellite starts with 18, mostly shift staff, with its own site lead and one technician seconded from the DC.

6.3. By the end of the first year the DC is planned to grow to 104, in line with signed volume, not ahead of it.

6.4. Recruitment is local wherever possible. Supervisors and the first QA officers are seconded from other Quillfern sites for six months so that the new team learns from people who have done the work before.

6.5. Every new joiner completes the company induction before handling stock, whatever the pressure of the opening weeks.

## 7. Languages and training

7.1. The DC works in Telugu and the satellite in Odia. English is the language of records and of procedures, but training is given in the local language by a trainer who speaks it, not by reading slides aloud.

7.2. Shift leads at both sites must be able to speak with Fleet Control and QA in Hindi or English as well as the local language.

7.3. Procedures are translated into Telugu and Odia for training use. The English version remains the controlled document.

7.4. The first three months of training are delivered on site by a team of four trainers, two seconded from other sites and two hired locally.

7.5. Customer-facing staff at the DC also speak to Godavari Aqua's export staff in English, because the exporter's buyers write in English.

## 8. Power

8.1. The DC has a sanctioned grid load of 1.5 MVA from the state distribution company, on an industrial feeder with its own substation inside the estate.

8.2. Two 750 kVA diesel generator sets stand on a plinth outside the plant room. Either set alone can carry the frozen block, the pharma room and the controls. Both together carry the whole site.

8.3. A rooftop solar plant of 420 kWp feeds the site's own load. It reduces the bill; it is not counted on in an outage.

8.4. The satellite runs on a 250 kVA grid supply and one 320 kVA generator set.

8.5. Changeover is tested monthly at both sites by pulling the incoming supply, and the result is written in the engineering log.

## 9. Weather from the bay

9.1. The coast between Visakhapatnam and Bhubaneswar is struck by storms from the Bay of Bengal in two seasons: April to May, and above all October to December. In 2014 and in 2019 storms in the region cut grid power to the industrial estates for several days.

9.2. From 1 October each year, and from 1 April, the DC keeps enough diesel on site to run both generator sets at full load for 120 hours. The satellite keeps enough for 72 hours at full load. The figure is checked by dipping the tanks every Monday in those months, and a shortfall is reported to the region head the same day.

9.3. When the India Meteorological Department (IMD) issues a red warning for the district, both sites stop dispatching outbound loads that would be on the road during the warning, and vehicles already on the road are directed to the nearest safe yard. Inbound vehicles are received if they can reach the site before the warning period begins.

9.4. Before landfall, loose items on the yards are tied down or brought inside, the solar plant is isolated, and the duty roster for the next 72 hours is fixed so that the people on it can stay on site safely.

9.5. Customers are told of a red-warning stop by the account managers, not by drivers, and are told again when dispatch restarts.

## 10. Port work

10.1. Much of Godavari Aqua's stock leaves the DC in 40-foot refrigerated containers for export through the port. Containers are stuffed at the DC under customs supervision and sealed before they leave.

10.2. A stuffed export container must reach the port terminal at least 18 hours before the vessel's cut-off time, because the terminal needs that long to plug it in, scan it and stack it for loading. A container that cannot meet that time is not sent to the gate on the chance; the exporter is told and a later vessel is booked.

10.3. Container plugs at the DC can hold six containers on shore power while they wait. A stuffed container is never left on its own generator overnight at the DC.

10.4. Customs officers attend at the DC on request. The site books them a day ahead and records their names on the stuffing sheet.

10.5. The port's own rules on reefer monitoring apply once a container is inside the terminal. Our responsibility ends at the terminal gate.

## 11. Inland from the coast

11.1. The site at Bhubaneswar exists to bring the DC's stock to customers in Odia-speaking markets the next morning, and to collect stock from Kalinga Biologicals and from Odisha food makers for the DC.

11.2. It is a cross-dock. Goods arriving on the nightly trunk run are sorted and loaded onto local vans for delivery the same morning. Goods collected locally wait for the next night's trunk run back to the DC.

11.3. No pallet stays at the satellite for more than 36 hours from arrival. A pallet still there at hour 30 is reported to the DC manager, who decides whether it goes back on the next trunk run or out on a special van.

11.4. Because nothing is stored there, the satellite does not hold a storage licence for medicines, and pharma stock passes through it only in the trunk vehicle without being unloaded (section 18).

11.5. The site's staging hall is mapped like a room, though it is not a store, because stock stands in it during sorting.

## 12. Fleet for the region

12.1. The region starts with 14 owned reefers: eight chilled and six frozen, all new, delivered to the DC in November 2026. Peak demand is met with hired reefers from approved vendors, up to nine at a time.

12.2. The nightly trunk run between the DC and the satellite uses two owned reefers and two drivers on each vehicle, whatever the day's departure time, because the run is long and mostly at night.

12.3. Local deliveries around Visakhapatnam and around Bhubaneswar are made by small chilled vans hired with drivers.

12.4. A fleet planner based at the DC builds the region's plan. Fleet Control's national desk remains the controller for vehicles on the road.

12.5. A second set of tyres, belts and reefer spares is stocked at the DC, because the nearest dealer workshop for the reefer units is several hours away.

## 13. What it costs and when it pays

13.1. The board approved capital spending of Rs. 46 crore for the region: Rs. 31 crore for the DC's fit-out, racking, refrigeration and power, Rs. 6 crore for the satellite, and Rs. 9 crore for the owned fleet.

13.2. Running costs in the first year are budgeted at Rs. 2.1 crore a month at full volume, of which people are about a third and power and fuel another third.

13.3. The region is planned to cover its running costs in its ninth month and to pay back its capital in the twenty-sixth month after first receipt, on contracted volumes only. Any new contract shortens the payback; no forecast is counted until it is signed.

13.4. The region head reports actual against plan to Finance monthly and to the board quarterly.

13.5. Spending above the approved figure needs board approval, however small the overrun.

## 14. Risks we can see

14.1. The anchor customer is a single exporter. If Godavari Aqua's export market falls, the frozen block is half empty. The plan reduces that risk by signing at least two more frozen customers before the end of 2027.

14.2. Storms may close the region for days at a time. Section 9 sets the response.

14.3. Skilled refrigeration technicians are scarce on this coast. Two places on each intake of the company's technician programme are reserved for people from the region, who return to it on completion.

14.4. The vaccine maker's start date may slip. Pharma space is not filled with food while it waits; it stays empty.

14.5. Port congestion may hold containers at the DC longer than planned. The container plugs in section 10 are the buffer.

## 15. Who leads

15.1. The region head is K. Srinivasa Rao, based at the DC, reporting to the Chief Operating Officer.

15.2. The DC manager is Swagatika Mohanty. The satellite's site lead reports to her.

15.3. The region's QA lead reports to the head of QA, not to the region head, so that quality decisions do not answer to the people who must meet the dispatch plan.

15.4. Finance, HR and IT support the region from head office, with one person from each at the DC for the first six months.

15.5. Escalation at night goes from the shift lead to the DC manager, then to the region head.

## 16. How the region will be judged

16.1. In its first two years the region is measured on: deliveries on time, which is planned at 96 per cent from month four; temperature excursions per thousand pallets moved; customer complaints; safety incidents; and money against plan.

16.2. Measures are reported monthly from the same systems as every other site. The region does not keep figures of its own.

16.3. A measure that misses plan for three months in a row is reviewed by the region head with the Chief Operating Officer, and a recovery plan is agreed in writing.

16.4. Safety incidents are reported the same day, as at every site, and the region is not given a softer target because it is new.

## 17. Later steps

17.1. A second satellite at Rajahmundry, closer to the shrimp plants, is being studied for 2028. It is not approved and nothing in this plan depends on it.

17.2. The second frozen chamber at the DC (section 5.2) will be proposed to the board when frozen occupancy has been above 85 per cent for six months.

17.3. Rail movement of frozen containers from the DC to the north has been offered by a container rail operator and will be tried in 2027 with one customer.

## 18. Things left out on purpose

18.1. The satellite does not store medicines and does not unload them. Pharma stock for Odisha customers either travels direct from the DC in a sealed vehicle that passes through the satellite's yard without opening, or is delivered by the DC's own vehicle. No passive shipper, no cross-dock exception and no customer's urgency changes this.

18.2. The region does not handle live seafood, fresh fish on ice or any product that is not packed and labelled.

18.3. The region does not offer customs clearance as a service. The exporter's own agent clears its goods; we provide the space and the supervision of stuffing.

18.4. The DC does not open a retail counter or sell surplus stock locally.

## 19. Review

19.1. The region head reviews this plan each quarter with the DC manager and Finance, and issues a revision when a date, a figure or a decision changes.

19.2. Revision 1 (March 2026) was approved with the investment. Revision 2 (July 2026) fixed the first-receipt date, the hiring numbers and the storm-season fuel stock after the site visit by the insurers' engineer.

19.3. The superseded outline of 2025 is kept in the plan file for history and must not be quoted to customers.
