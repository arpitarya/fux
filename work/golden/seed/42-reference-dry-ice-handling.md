# Dry ice: properties and limits

## What it is

- **dry ice** — solid carbon dioxide. At normal pressure it turns straight into gas at -78.5 C; it does not melt into a liquid.
- **pellet** — the 16 mm form Quillfern buys from Vidarbha Gases.
- **sublimation loss** — stock that turns to gas while waiting; about 8% of the chest's contents a day with the lid shut.

## Carbon dioxide in the air

| reading | meaning |
|---|---|
| about 420 ppm | ordinary outdoor air |
| 5,000 ppm | amber beep; leave the room and let it ventilate |
| 15,000 ppm | red siren; everyone leaves the store and nobody re-enters until the reading is back below 5,000 ppm |

## Limits

| item | limit |
|---|---|
| frozen body top-up | at most 40 kg per body per trip |
| skin contact | frostbite within seconds |
| pharma rooms and cabs | none allowed |

## Loss in the chest

A frozen chest is not the same as a dry-ice chest: the dry-ice chest is
insulated but not refrigerated. Loss is higher on a body top-up day, when the
lid is opened more often. A kg lost to the air is still written on QCL-F-26 at
the weekly count, so the store knows what the body jobs really cost.

## Charging

Emergency dry-ice packing is billed per kg on the current rate card; this sheet
does not carry the price.
