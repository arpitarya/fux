# Returns and Reverse Logistics Manual

## 1. What this manual covers

1.1. Goods come back. Hospitals over-order, pharmacies close, restaurants reject a load for a torn carton, a meal-kit customer goes on holiday and the box sits on a doorstep, and a manufacturer withdraws a batch. Until 2025 each Quillfern site dealt with what came back in its own way, usually in a corner of the dock that nobody owned. The Bhosari hub was opened to put an end to that.

1.2. This manual says how goods travel back from customers and consignees, how they are received and judged at Bhosari, and how they leave again: back to stock, back to their owner, or to destruction. It is written for the hub's own staff, for the drivers and planners who bring returns in, and for the customer service agents and account managers who promise customers what will happen to their goods.

1.3. It sets no temperature limit and no quarantine rule of its own. Product that may have been harmed by temperature is handled under the company's excursion and quarantine procedures, and this manual only says where it sits and who moves it while those procedures run.

1.4. Revision 3 replaces the interim note written when the hub opened in 2025 and the two earlier revisions of this manual. Several figures changed after the hub's first full year; each section that changed says so.

## 2. The hub

2.1. The hub occupies a leased unit of about 2,800 square metres in the MIDC estate at Bhosari, on the Pune–Nashik side of the city. It has four docks, a chilled room at +2 to +8 C, a freezer at -20 C, an ambient returns hall, a locked bay for goods that must not move without a customer's instruction, a waste store with its own door to the yard, and the Product Testing Laboratory on the mezzanine.

2.2. The hub is open from 07:00 to 23:00, Monday to Saturday. Nothing is received on Sundays; a vehicle that arrives on a Sunday is turned away at the gate with a phone call to Fleet Control, and the driver is told where to wait.

2.3. The hub manager is Sneha Khandekar. Rahul Wagh leads the receiving team, and each shift has a shift lead. Quality decisions on pharmaceutical returns are taken by the QA officer on duty, who reports to the QA function, not to the hub manager.

2.4. The hub stores nothing for sale. Goods leave it within the times set in this manual, or they are reported. A hub that fills up is a hub that has stopped making decisions.

## 3. Before anything travels

3.1. A customer who wants to return goods asks customer service, which checks the customer's contract and issues an rma, the return number. It carries the customer code, the date and a running count, and it is printed on the collection note and on every carton label.

3.2. An rma is valid for 14 days from the day it is issued. Goods that reach the hub after that date are received, put in the hub's quarantine area and held, and the customer is asked to raise a fresh number before anything else is done with them.

3.3. The interim rules of 2025 allowed twice as long, and the hub found itself holding stock that nobody remembered asking to return.

3.4. A return without any authorisation number at all is not accepted at the gate unless the hub manager agrees by phone. Goods that cannot be refused safely, such as chilled stock on a vehicle that has nowhere else to go, are taken in under the same hold as a late one.

3.5. A second authorisation is never issued for goods already in the building; the first number is extended by customer service instead.

## 4. Collection and the vehicles

4.1. Returns are collected by Quillfern vehicles on the customer's request, booked by customer service with Fleet Control in the same way as an outbound delivery. The collection note travels with the goods and is signed by the person handing them over.

4.2. The driver counts cartons, not units, and writes the count on the collection note. A carton that is open, wet or crushed is photographed at the collection point before it is loaded, and the photograph is sent to the hub by the driver while parked.

4.3. Chilled and frozen returns are loaded into a body already running at the right temperature. A driver who is asked to take chilled stock into a dry van refuses and calls Fleet Control.

4.4. Pharmaceutical returns travel with their own temperature record wherever the consignee kept one, and the driver asks for it at collection. A consignee who cannot produce a record is noted on the collection note in those words.

4.5. Collections from homes, such as meal-kit boxes left with a security guard, are made by the van pool and follow the van pool's own instructions about leaving and collecting goods at a door.

## 5. The ride to the hub

5.1. Most chilled returns come back in vehicles that delivered something else first. Putting returns on that backhaul is cheaper than sending an empty van, but a chilled carton that has already been out of the customer's fridge is fragile, and every extra stop warms it.

5.2. A chilled return rides on a delivering vehicle only if Fleet Control's plan puts that vehicle at the hub within 6 hours of the collection. Where it cannot, a dedicated pickup is sent instead, however small the load.

5.3. Fleet Control records the planned arrival time on the collection note, and the receiving team writes the actual arrival time beside it, so that the rule can be checked month by month.

5.4. The interim note allowed a longer window, and the hub's first summer showed that it was too long.

5.5. Frozen returns are never added to backhaul unless the delivering body is itself frozen; consolidation of frozen and chilled returns in one body is not allowed.

5.6. Planners may combine several customers' returns on one dedicated pickup, but the consolidation is planned by Fleet Control and never arranged by drivers on the road.

## 6. Receiving at the dock

6.1. Every vehicle is checked at the dock before its doors open: the seal, the collection note, and the body's temperature display. A vehicle whose seal has been broken on the road without a note from Fleet Control is photographed and reported before anything is unloaded.

6.2. Cartons are counted against the collection note and moved to the area for their temperature class: the chilled room, the freezer or the ambient hall. Nothing waits on the dock longer than it takes to count it.

6.3. The receiving clerk scans each carton's label into the returns system, which links it to the return number, the customer and the original delivery. A carton with no readable label is given a hub label and set aside for the shift lead.

6.4. Temperature records that came with pharmaceutical returns are handed to the QA officer on duty in the same shift. The QA officer decides whether the stock goes to quarantine under the company's procedures before anyone else handles it further.

6.5. Damage found at receiving is photographed and noted against the carton in the returns system, whether or not it was noted at collection.

## 7. Sorting what came back

7.1. Grading is a decision about every line, not a valuation, and each decision is checked by a second person.

7.2. Every line is judged within 24 hours of receipt. Bucket A means fit to go back to the owner's saleable stock under the owner's own rules; B means the owner must decide; C means destruction.

7.3. Grading of pharmaceutical lines is done by the QA officer on duty, not by the receiving team, and a pharmaceutical line never goes into A without QA's signature on the returns system.

7.4. Food lines go to C whenever the packaging has been opened, the use-by date has passed, or the temperature history is missing for a chilled or frozen line.

7.5. Lines judged B are reported to the owner the same day. Until the owner replies, they stay in the area for their temperature class, marked with an orange label.

7.6. A line may move from bucket B to A or C on the owner's written instruction only; it never moves because space ran short.

## 8. Agreeing the figures with the customer

8.1. A customer who returns goods usually raises a debit note against Quillfern or against its own supplier, and the reconciliation of that note with what the hub received is the last job on any return.

8.2. Customer service compares each debit note with the hub's receipt records within 5 working days of the note arriving. Any difference of more than 2 per cent in quantity or value goes to the account manager, who settles it with the customer before a credit note is raised.

8.3. A smaller discrepancy is settled by customer service from the hub's records, which are the count of record.

8.4. The reconciliation is kept with the return number in the returns system, so that an auditor can follow one return from the collection note to the credit note without asking anyone.

8.5. A discrepancy caused by a count error at collection is reported to Fleet Control as a driver coaching matter, not as a dispute with the customer.

## 9. Destruction

9.1. Food judged C is destroyed through the hub's licensed food-waste contractor, who collects twice a week. Packaging that carries a customer's brand is destroyed with the food, never sent for recycling.

9.2. Pharmaceutical stock for destruction leaves the hub only with the waste contractor's signed manifest, which lists every batch and quantity. The hub's copy is kept with the return number.

9.3. The licensed contractor must destroy the stock within 30 days of collection from the hub and send a certificate naming the batches within 15 days of destruction. The incineration plant used is named on the contract and may not be changed without the QA lead's agreement.

9.4. A certificate of incineration that has not arrived 50 days after collection is chased by the hub manager in writing, and the account manager is told.

9.5. Nothing is destroyed or put on a manifest while a customer's claim or an insurer's survey that concerns it is still open, unless QA rules that keeping it is a hazard.

## 10. When a batch is withdrawn

10.1. A manufacturer that withdraws a batch from the market instructs Quillfern through its named quality contact. The account manager confirms the instruction in writing and the hub manager is told the same day so that space is ready.

10.2. Stock coming back from consignees under a withdrawal is held in the locked bay R-2, under the key of the QA officer on duty. It never shares a shelf with returns of any other kind, and the bay's segregation is checked at every shift change.

10.3. The recalled units in R-2 are counted every day and the count sent to the customer's quality contact each week.

10.4. If the customer has not said what to do with the recalled stock 90 days after the first units arrived, the account manager writes to the customer's quality head, not to the original contact, and asks for a decision.

10.5. Breaking that segregation needs the QA lead's written consent. The hub does not take part in deciding whether a batch should be withdrawn, and does not comment on it to consignees.

## 11. Packaging that comes back on purpose

11.1. Some programmes send their own reusable packaging back through the hub, and Vetrix Animal Health sends its returned vaccines here from Punjab and Haryana: insulated meal-kit boxes from the Thali Box programme, crates from bakery and dairy customers, and passive shippers belonging to pharmaceutical customers.

11.2. Reusable packaging is counted on arrival and stored by owner in the ambient hall. It is not cleaned at Bhosari unless the owner's contract says so; it is returned to the owner's own kitting site on the next scheduled run.

11.3. Packaging that arrives with product still inside is treated as a return of that product first and as packaging second.

11.4. Broken packaging is set aside and reported to the owner, who decides whether it is repaired or scrapped. Quillfern does not scrap a customer's packaging on its own judgement.

11.5. Each owner receives a monthly count of packaging received, held and sent back.

## 12. People and training

12.1. Everyone who handles returns at the hub completes the company induction and the hub's own half-day course on receiving, labelling and the returns system before handling stock alone.

12.2. The QA officers on duty at Bhosari are trained by the QA function and hold the same authority as QA officers at any other site. They do not take instructions on pharmaceutical decisions from the hub manager or from account managers.

12.3. Receiving clerks are trained to say "I'll check and come back to you" to drivers and customers who ask what will happen to returned goods. The answer comes from customer service.

12.4. Agency staff may count and move cartons but may not scan them into the returns system or judge them.

12.5. Training is refreshed every year and whenever this manual changes.

## 13. What customers are told

13.1. Customers hear about their returns from customer service, never from the hub directly. The hub sends customer service a daily report of what arrived, what was judged and what left, and customer service passes on what each customer needs to know.

13.2. A customer who calls the hub is given the customer service number politely and is not told anything about the goods over the phone.

13.3. Customers are never promised a credit, a replacement or a destruction date by hub staff. Those promises are made, if at all, by account managers after Finance has agreed.

13.4. Customer auditors who wish to see their returns at Bhosari are escorted by the hub manager or the QA officer on duty and see only their own goods and records.

## 14. Records

14.1. The returns system holds the collection note, receipt scans, photographs, judgements, temperature records, owner instructions, destruction certificates and reconciliations for each return number.

14.2. Records are kept for eight years for pharmaceutical returns and for three years for everything else, and longer where a claim, a dispute or an audit finding concerns them.

14.3. Paper collection notes are scanned on the day of receipt and the paper is archived by month.

14.4. Nothing in the returns system is deleted. A wrong entry is corrected by a new entry that refers to it, with the name of the person correcting and the reason.

## 15. The hub's own measures

15.1. The hub is judged on how fast it decides, not on how much it holds. Each month the hub manager reports the share of lines judged within the time in section 7, the share of chilled returns that reached the hub within the time in section 5, the number of lines still waiting for an owner's decision, and the age of the oldest of them.

15.2. The report also counts returns that arrived without a valid number, broken seals found at the dock, damage found at receiving that was not noted at collection, and certificates overdue from the waste contractor.

15.3. The measures are read at the operations review each month, alongside the measures of the other sites. A measure that misses its target for three months running gets a written recovery plan from the hub manager, agreed with the operations head.

15.4. Customers are not sent the hub's measures as they stand. An account manager may draw on them for a customer's own service report.

## 16. Review of this manual

16.1. The hub manager reviews this manual every July with the QA lead and the customer service lead, and sooner when a customer's contract or a company procedure changes what the hub must do.

16.2. Each revision is issued with a short note of what changed and why. Hub staff sign that they have read the note within a week of issue.

## 17. Busy months

17.1. Returns rise in predictable waves. Hospitals send back surplus after the financial year closes in March; restaurants return frozen stock after the festival weeks in October and November; meal-kit boxes come back in a flood after every long weekend, when customers who forgot to pause their deliveries find a box on the doorstep.

17.2. The hub manager publishes a peak plan each February for the year, listing the expected peaks, the extra agency staff booked for each and the days on which no leave is granted to the receiving team. The plan is shared with Fleet Control and customer service, so that collections can be spread over several days instead of arriving in one.

17.3. In a peak week, receiving starts an hour earlier and the second shift runs until midnight. The timings in this manual still apply: a peak is a reason to add people, not a reason to let goods wait.

17.4. If the hub cannot keep to the timings even with extra people, the hub manager tells the operations head and customer service the same day, and customer service slows the issue of new return numbers until the hub has caught up. Customers are told the reason plainly.

## 18. Security of the hub

18.1. The hub is fenced, lit and guarded round the clock by a contract security firm. The gate keeps a register of every vehicle and every visitor, and visitors wear a badge and are escorted.

18.2. Returned goods are attractive to thieves because people assume nobody counts them. They are counted at every step, and the counts are compared every night by the shift lead before handover.

18.3. The locked bay, the waste store and the laboratory each have their own key. Keys are signed for in the key register at the gate, and the waste store key is never held by the waste contractor.

18.4. Staff may not buy, take or be given returned goods of any kind, even goods judged for destruction. A breach is treated as theft from the customer, whatever the goods were worth.
