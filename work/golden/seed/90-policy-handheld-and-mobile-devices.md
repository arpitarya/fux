# Handheld Terminal and Mobile Device Policy

## 1. Purpose and reach

1.1. This policy governs the rugged terminals, label printers and charging cradles issued to the sites opened since 2025: the pharma store at Changodar, the Visakhapatnam DC and its Bhubaneswar satellite, and the engineering training workshop at Waluj. Those sites run on the Korvex K7 terminal and the LP-40 printer, managed centrally from the Fernguard console. Sites that use other equipment are not covered here.

1.2. It is written for everyone who is issued a K7 or uses one on a shift — pickers, receiving clerks, QA officers, shift leads, drivers' mates and trainees — and for the IT staff who look after the fleet. Floor staff rarely say "terminal"; they say scanner or gun, and both words mean a K7 in this policy.

1.3. The policy exists because a K7 is three things at once: a stock-control tool, a key to customer data and an expensive piece of hardware that is easy to put down and forget. Each section below deals with one or more of those.

1.4. Revision 2 replaces the quick rules written for the 2025 pilot, when twenty K7s were tried at the Waluj workshop before the fleet was bought. Where a figure changed, the section says so.

1.5. MDM in this policy means Fernguard and nothing else. No other management tool is installed on a K7, and the IT desk does not accept a vendor's offer to add one.

## 2. The kit

2.1. The K7 is a rugged Android terminal with a built-in barcode reader, a 5-inch screen, a hot-swap battery and a wrist strap. It is rated for drops of 1.8 m onto concrete and for use in rain, but not for long use in a freezer (section 10).

2.2. The LP-40 is a belt-clip thermal printer for pallet and carton labels. It pairs with one K7 over Bluetooth.

2.3. Each K7 sits in a charging cradle when not in use. Cradles are mounted on cradle racks of ten, one rack per shift area, and each rack holds two spare K7s that belong to the rack rather than to a person.

2.4. Every device carries an asset label with its number, and Fernguard knows every device by its serial number. An asset label that is peeling is replaced by the IT desk, not taped over.

2.5. No other phone, tablet or scanner may be used for stock work at these sites. A customer's own scanner brought by an auditor is used only to read, never to transact.

2.6. The fleet at the date of this revision is 118 K7s and 34 LP-40s across the four sites, with the numbers per site held in the asset register. The register, not this policy, is the place to check a count.

## 3. Issue and sign-out

3.1. A K7 is issued either to a named person, for staff who use one every shift, or to a cradle rack, for shared use. Named issue is signed for in the asset register; rack issue is signed for by the shift lead at the start of each shift.

3.2. A named K7 is not lent to anyone else, even for a minute. A colleague who needs one takes a spare from the rack.

3.3. LP-40 printers are always issued to a rack, never to a person.

3.4. Contract and agency staff never hold a named K7.

3.5. At each shift change the outgoing shift lead counts the rack against the register, and the incoming shift lead signs for the count. A device missing at the count is reported under section 7 straight away, not at the end of the next shift.

## 4. Getting in

4.1. Screen lock is set centrally from Fernguard and cannot be changed on the device. Staff often complain that the scanner screen locks itself too soon on a slow pick; the setting is deliberate, because an unlocked scanner left on a pallet is an open door into customer stock records. An idle K7 now goes dark and asks for its PIN after two minutes; until the March 2026 revision the figure was five.

4.2. PINs — staff call them passcodes — are six digits and personal, and nobody else is told one, including a supervisor. Fernguard forces a new PIN every 90 days; the 2025 rules allowed 180. A passcode may not repeat any of the user's last five.

4.3. Five wrong attempts lock the user out of every K7 until the IT desk resets the account. A reset needs the user to be present with an ID card; it is never done over the phone for someone else.

4.4. Fingerprint unlock is switched off. Gloved hands made it unreliable, and a fallback to PIN was the only thing anyone used.

4.5. A user who believes someone else has seen their PIN asks the desk for a reset the same shift. Nobody is criticised for asking.

## 5. Apps on the device

5.1. A K7 runs the warehouse picking app, the receiving app, the document viewer, the camera and nothing else. The app list is fixed by Fernguard.

5.2. Users cannot install apps, and a K7 that shows an app not on the list is taken out of use and returned to the IT desk.

5.3. The camera is for evidence: damaged cartons, seal numbers, pallet labels. Photographs taken on a K7 upload to the document system and are deleted from the device after upload.

5.4. The browser is locked to the document system and the company intranet. A user who needs another website uses an office PC.

5.5. Messaging apps are not installed. Shift instructions come through the shift lead, not through the device.

5.6. A request for a new app on the list goes from the site manager to the IT infrastructure lead in writing, with the reason. The app is tested at the Waluj workshop on four devices for a fortnight before it reaches a live site.

## 6. Who may use one

6.1. A K7 may be used only by a person with a login issued by the IT desk. Logins are personal; a shift lead does not sign in for a picker so the picker can start sooner.

6.2. Contract and agency workers do not get permanent logins. Agency staff on the scanners are some of the busiest users on a peak night, and each one is given a temporary sign-in for the shift at the shift lead's request to the desk. The desk issues them a day credential that stops working twelve hours after it is first used. A new one is issued for each shift worked.

6.3. Personal phones are a separate matter. Many staff would rather scan with their own phone than walk to the rack, and on the pilot some did. No personal phone may run the scanning app or hold a Quillfern login, even for one shift when a K7 has failed; the user takes a spare from the cradle rack or waits.

6.4. Customer staff and auditors may look at a K7's screen with a Quillfern user beside them. They do not operate it.

6.5. Trainees from the Waluj workshop use a K7 only with their trainer's login running, and only for the exercises in their module.

## 7. When one goes missing

7.1. A lost or stolen scanner is reported to the IT desk within one hour of being missed, by the user or the shift lead, with the asset number and where it was last seen.

7.2. The desk locks the device from Fernguard as soon as the report arrives. A locked K7 shows a message asking the finder to hand it to security.

7.3. If it has not turned up 24 hours after the report, Fernguard wipes it remotely and it is struck off the register. A device found after it has been wiped goes back to the desk to be rebuilt; it is never simply switched back on.

7.4. Losing a device is not in itself a disciplinary matter. Failing to report a loss, or reporting it late, is.

7.5. A theft is also reported to security and, where the site manager decides, to the police. The report number is written in the register.

## 8. Power

8.1. Each K7 has a hot-swap battery that can be changed without switching off. Spare charged batteries sit in the charging bay of each rack.

8.2. Batteries wear out, and a worn one dies at the worst moment of a pick. A pack is retired at 500 charge cycles or when Fernguard reports its health under 80 per cent, whichever comes first. Floor staff say a retired battery has been binned; it is in fact returned to the IT desk for recycling and never put in general waste.

8.3. A battery that is swollen, cracked or hot to the touch is taken out at once, put in the fire-safe tin by the rack and reported to the IT desk.

8.4. Batteries are not charged anywhere except in the cradle rack. Chargers brought from home are not used.

8.5. At the end of each shift every K7 goes back in its cradle, charged or not.

## 9. Overnight

9.1. Software and app updates are pushed from Fernguard to docked devices only, so that nobody's scanner restarts in the middle of a pick. New software is tested on four devices at the Waluj workshop first.

9.2. The push window is Tuesday night, 01:00 to 03:00, and a K7 that is not in its cradle then is updated the following Tuesday. A device that has missed two windows is called in by the desk.

9.3. Urgent security updates may be pushed outside the window. The desk warns the shift leads first, and the update waits for the device to be docked.

9.4. Nobody updates a K7 by hand, from a memory card or from a cable.

9.5. Sites that work through Tuesday night arrange their docking so that each rack is full for at least an hour inside the window.

## 10. Cold work

10.1. Condensation is the enemy of a K7 taken into a freezer. When a cold device comes out into warm air, water forms inside the case and on the reader window, and a device that goes back in wet freezes inside.

10.2. A K7 is used in a chamber below 0 C for no more than 20 minutes at a stretch, then rests 10 minutes outside before it goes back in. Pickers on a freezer pick carry two scanners and swap between them, so that the pick does not stop while one rests.

10.3. In chilled chambers at +2 to +8 C there is no limit, but a scanner is never left inside a chamber overnight.

10.4. A device that has fogged inside the window is taken out of use until it has stood at room temperature for an hour.

10.5. Freezer jackets have an inside pocket for the K7. Using it between scans keeps the battery warm and the screen readable.

## 11. Pairing

11.1. Each LP-40 label printer is paired over Bluetooth to one K7, and the pairing is recorded against both asset numbers in Fernguard.

11.2. Pickers sometimes need to hook a printer up to a different scanner when theirs has gone flat or been swapped. Only a shift lead may move one to another K7, and the change is logged in Fernguard. A printer paired without a log entry prints labels nobody can trace to a user.

11.3. A printer whose labels come out faint or skewed is cleaned with the head-cleaning pen kept in the rack, and returned to the desk if that does not cure it.

11.4. Label stock is loaded only from the sealed rolls held at the rack. Rolls left open on a dock pick up dust that scratches the print head.

11.5. A printer that will not pair after two attempts is put aside with a note and the shift lead uses the rack spare.

## 12. Cleaning and damage

12.1. K7s are wiped at the end of each shift with the alcohol wipes kept in the rack. Screens are never sprayed directly.

12.2. Cracked screens, broken reader windows and missing strap clips are reported to the IT desk the same shift. A cracked screen cuts gloves and hands, and the device is not used until it is repaired.

12.3. Damage caused by ordinary work is not charged to the user. Damage caused by throwing, standing on or misusing a device may be.

12.4. Stickers, pens and personal decorations are not attached to devices.

12.5. Devices are not left on forklift seats, pallet tops or vehicle dashboards, which is how most of the pilot's broken screens happened.

12.6. At the pharma store, wiping is done outside the gowning room and the wipes are disposed of in the general bin, never in the medical-waste bin, so that the waste counts stay honest.

## 13. Network and data

13.1. K7s connect only to the site's own wireless network, which is separate from the guest and office networks. They do not connect to public or home networks.

13.2. Data on a K7 is encrypted, and the device holds only what the current task needs. Stock records are kept on the warehouse system, not on the device.

13.3. A user who sees a network warning on the screen tells the shift lead and does not tap through it.

13.4. Fernguard keeps a log of every login, every app opened and every update. The log is kept for one year and is read by IT only when there is a reason, such as a missing device or a disputed transaction.

13.5. No customer data leaves the site on a K7. A device taken off site, for a trainee exercise or a repair, is wiped first.

13.6. Wireless coverage is surveyed before a site goes live and again after any racking change. A dead spot found on the floor is reported to the desk with the rack location, and pickers are told to finish the scan at the aisle end rather than wave the device about.

## 14. Photographs and personal data

14.1. Photographs taken on a K7 are of stock, seals, labels, damage and equipment. They do not include people's faces where it can be helped, and never include a customer's staff without their agreement.

14.2. Photographs are uploaded to the document system against the job they belong to and deleted from the device.

14.3. Staff do not photograph a K7's screen with their own phones to send a picture to someone else.

14.4. Personal data about staff — names, logins and shift times — is visible only to the shift lead, the IT desk and HR.

14.5. A person who wants to know what Fernguard holds about them asks HR in writing.

14.6. Login records are not used to measure how fast a person picks. Pick rates come from the warehouse system and are discussed with the person, not posted.

## 15. Repairs

15.1. All repairs go through the IT desk. The desk keeps a small stock of spare K7s and LP-40s at each site, so that a repair never leaves a rack short.

15.2. Devices are sent to the Korvex service centre in a padded case with a repair note. Nobody opens a device on site.

15.3. A repaired device is rebuilt from Fernguard before it is reissued.

15.4. A device that has been repaired three times in a year is retired.

15.5. The desk reports device faults by type every quarter to the site managers, so that a site whose scanners keep breaking can look at how they are used.

15.6. Repair costs are charged to the site's cost centre. A site that pays for many repairs is not punished for it; it is asked what is going on.

## 16. Leaving Quillfern

16.1. Leavers hand back every device, battery and charger issued to them by name, and the shift lead signs the asset register to show it was received. HR does not issue the leaving papers until the register is signed.

16.2. Sometimes a person who quits keeps their scanner, by accident or otherwise. Anything not returned within two working days of the last shift is recovered from the final settlement at Rs. 18,500 per K7 and Rs. 6,200 per LP-40. The device is then locked and wiped from Fernguard.

16.3. A device returned after the recovery is made is refunded at the same rate, less any repair cost.

16.4. Agency staff never hold a named device, so nothing is recovered from their agencies; their day credentials simply lapse.

16.5. Trainees who leave the Waluj programme early hand their device back to the trainer the same day.

## 17. Review

17.1. The IT infrastructure lead reviews this policy every March and after any loss of data, any serious security warning or any change of device model.

17.2. Site managers and shift leads send suggested changes to the IT desk at any time.

17.3. A revised policy is issued with a short note of what changed, and the Fernguard settings are changed on the same day, so that the policy and the devices never disagree.

17.4. When a site opens, the IT desk walks its shift leads through this policy on a live device before go-live, and the shift leads walk their teams through it in the first week.
