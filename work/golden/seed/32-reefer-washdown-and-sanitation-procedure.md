# Reefer Body Washdown and Sanitation Procedure

## 1. When a washdown is required

1.1. After any dairy spill in the body, before the next load.

1.2. Before a chilled body carries pharma critical stock after any food load.

1.3. Every 14 days for a body on dairy rotation, whether or not anything spilled.

1.4. After any load rejected by a customer for odour.

## 2. Solution and contact

2.1. The floor, walls to 1 metre, door gaskets and drain channel are wetted with chlorine solution at 200 ppm, mixed fresh for each body.

2.2. The Chlorine Contact Time (CCT) is 10 minutes: the solution stays wet on the surface for that long before rinsing. Wiping the surface dry sooner restarts the count.

2.3. Rinse with potable water, then dry with the doors open and the drain plug out.

## 3. Swab check

3.1. Three swabs: floor front, floor rear, door gasket.

3.2. Each must read 150 or below on the handheld luminometer. One failed swab means the whole washdown is repeated, not just the failed spot.

## 4. Frozen bodies

4.1. A frozen body such as RF-120 is never washed with water while the body is below 0 C. It is dry-wiped with food-grade alcohol, and gets a full washdown only when defrosted at service.

4.2. A raw chicken spill in a frozen body is the exception: the body is taken out of service and defrosted for a full washdown within 24 hours.

## 5. Where and who

5.1. Nagpur: the wash bay beside the generator room. Guwahati: the contract garage. Coimbatore: the wash pad inside the south gate.

5.2. The shift lead signs the wash register QCL-F-40. The driver may do the washdown but may not sign it.

## 6. After the washdown

6.1. A washed body is not loaded until it has been chilled again from empty; the dispatch checklist for the load sets how long.
