# Gel packs: why blue caps, 18 hours and a cycle limit

## Why packs are conditioned at +5.0 C, not frozen

The 2025 shipper validation first used packs straight from a freezer. Three of the five trial runs dipped to +1.4 C at the carton nearest a frozen pack.
A vaccine that freezes is lost for good; a warm excursion at least has a
stability letter to argue with.

## Why 18 hours

Packs pulled after 12 hours of conditioning ran warm from about hour 30 of the trial.
At 18 hours every run held range for the length of the lane.

## Why a cycle limit

Every freeze and thaw works the seam of a pack. A pack that leaks inside a
shipper wets the product box and ruins the label, so packs are retired on a
count, not on how they look.

## Why blue and white caps

Blue caps and white caps were chosen because the night team works under one tube light and colour is read faster than a label.

## Notes from the trial

The trial ran in the night team's slot, because that is when pharma shippers are
made up. White packs from the freezer and blue packs from CC-1 sat on separate
trolleys. One tube light over the bench had failed and was not replaced for a
week. The caps were ordered from the same supplier as the pack bodies.
