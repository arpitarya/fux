# Packaging Specification Book, Thali Box Programme

## 1. The programme and this book

1.1. Thali Box Foods Pvt. Ltd. sells weekly meal kits to households in Pune and Mumbai: a box holding the raw and part-cooked ingredients for four or five dinners, with recipe cards, delivered to the door on a day the customer chooses. Quillfern assembles, stores and delivers the kits from the Kondhwa kitting room, and takes the empty boxes back through the Bhosari hub.

1.2. A kit holds chilled items, such as paneer, dahi, cut vegetables and marinated chicken, and on some weeks frozen items, such as parathas and ice cream. It is left on doorsteps, with security guards and in building lobbies, sometimes for hours. The packaging is what keeps it safe there, and it has to be specified, tested and checked like any other part of the cold chain.

1.3. This book is the agreed specification for every packaging component in the programme. It is owned by Quillfern's packaging engineer, Aarti Jog, and agreed with Thali Box's product team. Neither side changes a component without the other's written agreement.

1.4. Revision 2 replaces the pack note used in the 2025 pilot. The pilot ran through one summer with packaging chosen for cost, and lost too many kits to warm doorsteps and crushed corners. Several components changed as a result; each section says what changed.

1.5. The book sets no product temperature. Kit temperatures and the action taken when they are missed come from the programme's quality agreement, which this book supports but does not repeat.

## 2. How a kit is built

2.1. A kit is built from the outside in: an outer shipper, an insulating liner, the cold source, the food in its own packs, a divider between chilled and frozen items where both are present, and the paperwork on top.

2.2. Food packs are supplied by Thali Box's own vendors and arrive at Kondhwa already sealed and labelled. Quillfern does not open, repack or relabel any food pack, and rejects any that arrive damaged.

2.3. Every kit is built on the kitting line in a fixed order shown on the station cards, and checked at the end of the line by a second person against the customer's order before the lid is closed.

2.4. The kitting room is held at +12 C. Food packs come to the line from the chilled and frozen stores in small batches so that no pack waits at room temperature.

2.5. A kit that cannot be finished, for want of a pack or a component, is not sent out incomplete. It is set aside, and customer service tells Thali Box, which decides whether to substitute or to cancel.

## 3. The outer shell

3.1. The outer shipper takes the weight of other kits stacked on it in the van and the knocks of the doorstep, and it is the first thing a customer sees. It must still be square and closed when it arrives, after a night in a damp van and a day on a stone floor.

3.2. The shipper is a five-ply corrugated box in a regular slotted style, printed in two colours with the Thali Box design. Its strength in the bct, measured by the testing laboratory on conditioned samples, must be at least 450 kgf.

3.3. The 2025 pilot used a lighter board and a lower strength, and lost corners in the monsoon.

3.4. Every delivery of shippers comes with the box maker's bct certificate for that lot, and the laboratory tests one lot in five again on receipt. A lot that fails is returned whole.

3.5. Corrugated stock is stored off the floor, away from the kitting room's damp walls, and used first in, first out.

## 4. Keeping the heat out

4.1. Inside every shipper sits a moulded expanded polystyrene box with a fitted lid, made of eps beads. It is the only insulation in the kit, and the whole thermal performance of the kit depends on it.

4.2. The liner's walls, base and lid are 30 mm thick, moulded at a density of 20 kg per cubic metre, and its lid fits with a lip that overlaps the walls by at least 10 mm.

4.3. The pilot used thinner walls to save space in the van and kits ran warm on afternoon doorsteps.

4.4. Liners with cracks, chipped lips or a lid that does not seat are rejected at the line. The polystyrene moulder replaces rejected units in the next delivery without charge.

4.5. Every eps liner carries the moulder's lot number on its base so that a complaint can be traced to a mould. The liner is the component customers are asked to return, under section 9.

## 5. The cold source

5.1. Chilled kits are kept cold by sealed phase-change packs that melt at +3 C, holding the food near that temperature until they have melted. They are conditioned in the Kondhwa cold room for at least 24 hours before use.

5.2. Every chilled kit carries two packs of 600 g each, one under the food and one on top. A kit with frozen items as well carries a third pack, conditioned at -21 C, packed against the frozen items behind the divider.

5.3. The pilot carried three smaller packs and the food between them warmed first.

5.4. Each pcm brick is checked at the line for leaks and swelling and discarded if either is found. Packs are reused only when they come back intact through the box returns, and each pcm brick is retired after 30 uses.

5.5. Gel and ice packs from any other source are never used in a Thali Box kit.

## 6. Paperwork and labels

6.1. Every kit carries a recipe card set, an ingredient list and an allergen card, all printed by Thali Box and supplied to the line in packs of fifty.

6.2. The shipper's end-of-line label shows the customer's name and address, the delivery day and window, the order number and a barcode for the driver.

6.3. A second, larger label on the lid shows the earliest use-by date of any item in the kit. The date is printed in characters at least 6 mm high, so that it can be read by someone standing at the door without bending.

6.4. The pilot printed it smaller, and complaints showed that customers did not read it.

6.5. Every shipper also carries an sscc label so that the kit can be traced through the van and the returns system. The label follows the gs1 standard so that both companies' scanners read it, and a kit whose sscc does not scan is relabelled; the gs1 company prefix used is Thali Box's own.

6.6. Labels are printed on direct-thermal stock that survives condensation. A label that smudges at the line test is reprinted before the kit leaves.

## 7. Proving a design before it is used

7.1. No new packaging component, and no change to an existing one, goes into service until a complete kit built with it has passed a transit test at the Product Testing Laboratory.

7.2. The test follows an ista procedure adapted for meal kits. A kit is built with dummy food packs and a logger, conditioned, and then dropped ten times from 60 cm onto its faces, edges and corners in a set order. It passes if nothing has leaked, the lid is still seated and the logger shows the food stayed in range for the full doorstep period the programme promises.

7.3. The pilot's simulation used fewer and lower drops and passed a box that failed on the road.

7.4. A simulation of the doorstep follows the drops: the kit stands in a chamber that cycles between the hottest and most humid conditions recorded in Pune in May and the coolest night.

7.5. Results of each ista run are written up by the laboratory and signed by the packaging engineer and Thali Box's product manager before the component is released.

## 8. Storing components

8.1. Shippers, liners and paper components are stored in the ambient store at Kondhwa, on pallets, in the order they arrived. Nothing is stored against the outside walls.

8.2. Phase-change packs are stored and conditioned in the cold room on their own racks, by temperature, with chilled packs and frozen packs on separate racks clearly labelled.

8.3. Stock of each component is counted every Monday. The packaging engineer orders from the approved suppliers when stock falls to two weeks' use.

8.4. Components that have been damaged in storage are set aside and counted, not used to make up numbers.

## 9. Bringing the kit home again

9.1. The liner and the cold packs are expensive and can be used again; the outer shipper is collected too, so that customers are not left with a bulky box, even though it is recyclable. The programme asks every customer to hand the whole kit back, empty, at their next delivery or by pickup from the door.

9.2. Customers pay a refundable deposit of Rs. 80 on the first kit they order. The amount is refunded in full if the box comes back within 14 days of delivery, in a condition the hub can reuse, and is kept if it does not.

9.3. The pilot's deposit was smaller and gave less time, and too many boxes stayed in customers' homes.

9.4. Outer shippers that cannot be reused are flattened at the Bhosari hub and sent for recycling; they are recyclable board with water-based ink. Liners that cannot be reused are sent to the moulder, who takes them back as raw material.

9.5. Customer service handles questions about refunds; the van drivers do not discuss money at the door.

## 10. Hygiene of reused components

10.1. Liners and cold packs that come back are cleaned before reuse at the Bhosari hub's washing station, using food-safe detergent and warm water, then a sanitising rinse and air-drying on racks.

10.2. Every tenth cleaned liner is swabbed by the laboratory for hygiene, and a failing result stops reuse from that day's batch until the batch has been cleaned again and passed.

10.3. A liner that smells, is stained or has been written on is not reused.

10.4. Cleaned components travel back to Kondhwa in sealed crates and are stored apart from new components until they have been inspected at the line.

## 11. Vans and handling

11.1. Kits travel to customers in chilled vans running at +4 C. Kits are loaded in delivery order, stacked no more than five high, with heavier kits at the bottom.

11.2. Drivers carry kits by the handles moulded into the shipper, never by the strapping, and never throw a kit onto a doorstep.

11.3. A kit that is dropped from a height in the van or at the door is reported by the driver and checked at the next stop for damage. A kit with a cracked liner is taken back to Kondhwa.

11.4. Vans carry no other customer's goods while kits are on board.

## 12. Complaints about packaging

12.1. Complaints from Thali Box customers about packaging, such as a crushed box, a leaking pack or a warm kit, come to Quillfern through Thali Box's customer team. They are logged by customer service and passed to the packaging engineer the same day.

12.2. Each complaint is traced to the kit's order number, the components' lot numbers, the van and the delivery time. The packaging engineer writes a short finding within five working days.

12.3. Complaints are counted per thousand kits delivered and reviewed at the monthly meeting with Thali Box. A component whose complaint rate rises for two months running is reviewed with its supplier.

## 13. Suppliers

13.1. Each component comes from an approved supplier named on the component's sheet in the annex to this book: the box maker, the moulder, the pack maker, the label printer and the printer of the paper components.

13.2. A new supplier is approved only after its component has passed the transit test in section 7 and a visit to its works by the packaging engineer.

13.3. Suppliers are reviewed yearly on quality, delivery and price with Thali Box. A supplier with more than two rejected deliveries in a year is put on notice.

## 14. Changes and review

14.1. Changes to this book are proposed by either company in writing, tested under section 7, and agreed in writing by both before use.

14.2. The packaging engineer reviews the whole book each March with Thali Box, after the winter and before the summer, and issues a revision if anything changes.

14.3. Each revision lists its changes on its first page, with the reason for each.

## 15. Less packaging, year by year

15.1. Thali Box tells its customers that it is reducing the packaging in every kit, and both companies have agreed targets that make that claim true. The targets are measured in grams of single-use material per kit delivered, averaged over each quarter.

15.2. For 2026 the target is a reduction of ten per cent against the average of the pilot year, to come mainly from reusing more liners and cold packs and from lighter paper components. Progress is reported at the quarterly review with Thali Box, with the figures from the returns count and the component purchases.

15.3. A change made to reduce packaging must still pass the transit test in section 7. A lighter kit that arrives warm is not a saving; it is a lost dinner and a complaint.

15.4. Plastic wrap is not used anywhere in the kit. Food packs that arrive from Thali Box's vendors in plastic outer wraps are unwrapped at goods-in, and the wrap is sent for recycling separately with the vendor's name noted, so that Thali Box can raise it with the vendor.

## 16. Summer and monsoon

16.1. Pune's hottest weeks fall in April and May, when afternoon doorsteps in the eastern suburbs reach well above 40 C. In those weeks the kitting room adds a reflective paper sleeve between the shipper and the liner for kits on afternoon delivery windows, and the delivery windows themselves are shortened by Thali Box.

16.2. In the monsoon the danger is wet board rather than heat. Shippers are kept under cover from the kitting room to the van, and van drivers carry a supply of plastic rain covers for kits that must be left in an open porch. The cover is collected with the empty box.

16.3. Mumbai deliveries face humidity all year. Shippers for Mumbai are drawn from the driest stock in the store and are not stored at Kondhwa for longer than four weeks.

16.4. The packaging engineer reviews the complaint log each June and each October to see whether the season's precautions worked, and changes them for the next year if they did not.

## 17. Who pays for what

17.1. Thali Box pays for every packaging component used in its kits, at the prices agreed in the annex, together with a handling charge per kit for assembly and a charge per kit delivered.

17.2. Quillfern pays for components it damages in storage or on the line, and for kits it has to rebuild because of a kitting error.

17.3. Components lost because customers do not return them are Thali Box's cost; the customer's forfeited amount goes to Thali Box, not to Quillfern.

17.4. Testing at the Product Testing Laboratory is charged to whichever company proposed the change being tested. Routine lot testing of incoming components is included in the handling charge.

17.5. Prices are reviewed every April, at the same time as the delivery charges.

## 18. Trying new materials

18.1. Both companies want to replace foamed plastics with materials that compost or are easier to recycle. Several suppliers have offered insulated boxes made of moulded pulp, wool and straw, and starch foams.

18.2. A new material is tried first in the laboratory, then in a small trial of no more than two hundred kits on one delivery route, with the customers on that route told in a note in the kit that they are helping with a trial.

18.3. A trial material is adopted only if it matches the current kit's results in the transit test and in the doorstep complaint rate over at least eight weeks, and if its cost has been agreed by Thali Box.

18.4. Results of every trial, including failed ones, are written up and kept with this book, so that the same material is not tried twice without a reason.

## 19. Training the kitting team

19.1. Everyone who works on the kitting line is trained on this book before working on the line alone: the order of building a kit, the checks on each component, the labels, and what to do with a component that fails a check. Training is given on the line by the shift lead, in Marathi or Hindi as the trainee prefers.

19.2. A new member of the team builds kits beside an experienced one for at least three shifts, and is signed off by the shift lead only after building a full shift's kits without a check failure found at the end of the line.

19.3. The station cards on the line show each step with a photograph. When this book changes, the packaging engineer changes the station cards on the same day and briefs every shift before its first kit is built under the new rule.

19.4. Thali Box's product team may watch the line at any time by arrangement with the kitting room manager. They are welcome to point out anything that looks wrong, and the shift lead writes down what they said and passes it on.

19.5. Mistakes found at the end of the line are counted by station, not by person, and are discussed at the start of the next shift. A person who makes repeated mistakes is retrained, not blamed in front of the line.

19.6. The kitting room manager keeps a training record for each person, showing the date of first training, the date of sign-off and every briefing on a change to this book.
