# Customer Service Handbook

For customer service agents and account managers at Pune HQ and at the three
DCs. It says how customer service works with customers and with the rest of
Quillfern. It sets no temperature limit, no notification window and no price;
those are in the SOP, the customer notification matrix and the rate card, and an
agent opens those documents rather than quoting this handbook.

## 1. Who customer service is

1.1. Customer service is eleven agents at Pune HQ, one agent at each DC, and
four account managers who each hold a list of named accounts. BharatVac and
Manjara Dairy each have their own account manager.

1.2. Customer service is the only team that writes to a customer about service,
price or an incident. Operations staff speak to customers about the work in
front of them, but the written record comes from customer service or QA.

1.3. Agents are measured on how accurately they answer, not on how quickly they
close a ticket. A ticket closed with a wrong answer is reopened and counted
against the agent who closed it.

1.4. Every agent completes the M1 excursion basics module and the M6 site
language module in their first month, even though they never handle stock.
They must be able to follow what a shift lead tells them on the phone.

## 2. Hours and cover

2.1. The Pune desk is staffed from 07:00 to 21:00, Monday to Saturday. On
Sundays and public holidays one agent is on call from home from 07:00 to 21:00.

2.2. From 21:00 to 07:00 there is no customer service agent on duty. A customer
who calls at night reaches a recorded message giving the Fleet Control number
for vehicles on the road and the QA desk number for business hours.

2.3. At night, if a pharma critical excursion is confirmed, QA calls the
customer service lead at home so that the customer notice can be sent inside
the window. The lead's phone is on the Compliance-Red call list for that
reason.

2.4. Agents at the DCs work the DC's day shift. When a DC agent is on leave, the
Pune desk covers that DC's accounts and says so in the customer's ticket.

2.5. Leave is planned so that no account manager and their backup are away in
the same week.

## 3. Opening an account

3.1. A new customer is opened by an account manager, never by an agent. The
account manager checks the customer's product classes against what each DC can
store and move.

3.2. A customer who needs vaccines stored at Coimbatore is told plainly that
Coimbatore does not store vaccines. A customer who needs chilled dairy stored at
Coimbatore is told the same.

3.3. A customer who wants its own pricing gets a signed annex. Until the annex
is signed, the standard rate card is the only price that may be given.

3.4. The account manager sends the customer the list of named contacts on both
sides, including who will receive an excursion notice at night.

3.5. A customer who asks to be told of every alarm, not only confirmed
excursions, is told that Quillfern notifies confirmed excursions. A customer
wanting alarm-level alerts is referred to the account manager, who may arrange a
Tessaline customer view under the annex.

## 4. Giving a customer a price

4.1. Agents quote from the rate card in force on the day of the quotation, and
write the rate card's effective date on the quotation.

4.2. BharatVac and Manjara Dairy are never quoted from the rate card. Their
prices come from Finance under their annexes.

4.3. Passive shipper hire is quoted only for consignments that start at Nagpur,
because the shippers are at Nagpur. A shipper cannot be quoted on a Guwahati or
Coimbatore dispatch unless the account manager has arranged for the consignment
to go through Nagpur.

4.4. The Guwahati monsoon handling line is quoted only on Guwahati loads in its
months. It is not added to Coimbatore or Nagpur loads.

4.5. The diesel surcharge is quoted as a rule, not as a figure: the agent writes
the rule from the rate card and lets the invoice apply it on the day of travel.

4.6. Agents do not discount. Any price below the rate card is an account
manager's call, confirmed in writing by Finance.

## 5. Taking a booking

5.1. A booking needs the product class, the quantity in the unit the rate card
uses, the pickup and delivery points, the earliest and latest delivery time, and
the name of the person who will receive.

5.2. The agent checks that the booked vehicle can carry the product class. RF-120
is frozen only; RF-119 takes pharma only with a passive shipper; RF-118 takes
pharma and dairy but never frozen.

5.3. The agent books the dock through Dockwise for Nagpur, Guwahati and
Coimbatore alike. A phone slot at Guwahati is confirmed in Dockwise the next
working day.

5.4. Pharma critical bookings that would need a night segment are passed to
Fleet Control before they are confirmed to the customer, because a night pharma
run needs two named drivers.

5.5. A booking the customer changes after 15:00 the day before is treated as a
new booking.

## 6. Where the truck is

6.1. Customers ask where their vehicle is more than they ask anything else.

6.2. Customer service gives an ETA only from Fleet Control's board, rounded to
30 minutes, and never calls the driver.

6.3. If the board shows no position, because of a dead zone, the agent says that
the vehicle is in an area without signal and gives the last known position and
time. The agent does not guess.

6.4. A customer who has the driver's number and calls him directly is asked, the
next time customer service speaks to them, to call customer service instead.
Drivers answer only when parked.

6.5. A late vehicle is reported to the customer as late as soon as Fleet Control
says it will miss its window. The reason is given in plain words and without
blame.

## 7. When stock goes out of range

7.1. When QA confirms an excursion, customer service sends the first notice.
The window, the channel and the signatory are those of the customer notification
matrix with the later effective date. The agent opens the matrix every time,
because the windows have changed and may change again.

7.2. The first notice uses only the wording the matrix fixes. It promises
nothing about release, replacement, credit or insurance.

7.3. A pharma critical notice is signed by the QA head or the nominated deputy.
The agent prepares it; the agent does not sign it.

7.4. The agent records every call and email to the customer in QCL-F-17,
including calls nobody answered.

7.5. After QA records a disposition, the second notice names it in the matrix's
words. If the customer asks what the disposition means for their invoice, the
agent says that the account manager will reply, and passes the question on.

7.6. Numbers in a notice are kept apart: the quantity affected, the quantity
destroyed and the quantity released are separate lines. The March 2025 vaccine
event showed what happens when one draft mixes them.

## 8. Claims

8.1. A customer claim for damaged or lost stock is accepted in writing only,
from a named contact on the account.

8.2. A claim is registered only when it arrives within 30 days of delivery and
carries photographs of the stock and the seal taken at delivery.

8.3. The agent registers the claim, acknowledges it within one working day, and
passes it to the account manager. The agent does not give an opinion on it.

8.4. Stock value in a claim is taken from the Finance schedule. A value in the
customer's email is recorded as the declared value and nothing more.

8.5. A claim that turns on a temperature record waits for QA's investigation.
The customer is told it is waiting, and given the investigation's reference.

8.6. Claims for trips before 18 August 2025 quote the KLP trip number, not a TSL
one.

## 9. Credit notes

9.1. A credit note is raised by Finance on the account manager's request. Agents
do not promise one.

9.2. A credit note for a wrongly quoted line, such as a Guwahati monsoon line on a
Coimbatore load, is raised without a claim, but still needs the account
manager's request.

9.3. A credit note is never offered to settle a complaint about an excursion
before QA has recorded a disposition.

9.4. Every credit note carries the invoice number, the line and the reason, in
words a customer's accounts clerk can follow without calling us.

## 10. Emergency cold for a customer's kitchen

10.1. Restaurant chains sometimes ask for emergency dry-ice packing when their
own freezer fails. Customer service takes the request; the DC decides whether it
can be done.

10.2. The agent does not promise a time. The DC tells the agent when the packing
can be ready, and the agent passes that on.

10.3. Emergency dry-ice packing is billed at a minimum of 10 kg per request,
whatever smaller quantity the kitchen asks for, and the per-kg price is the one
on the rate card in force.

10.4. Dry ice is never sent in a customer's own car unless the customer's driver
signs the dry-ice safety note at the gate. Customer service sends the note to
the customer with the confirmation.

10.5. Pharma customers are not offered dry-ice packing as an emergency measure.

## 11. Complaints and escalation

11.1. A complaint is anything a customer says is wrong with our service, even if
they do not use the word.

11.2. The agent records it, acknowledges it the same day, and passes it to the
account manager. The account manager replies within three working days.

11.3. A complaint about a driver's behaviour goes to Fleet Control and HR, not
to the driver.

11.4. A complaint that mentions safety, such as a vehicle driven fast or a
driver who looked unwell, goes to Fleet Control within the hour, whatever else
the complaint is about.

11.5. A customer who asks for a senior person is given the account manager's
name. If the account manager is away, the customer service lead takes the call.

## 12. Customer loggers

12.1. Some customers send their own logger with the stock. The logger travels
with the stock from the moment it arrives until it leaves.

12.2. A customer logger does not overrule the Tessaline record. If the customer
quotes their logger in an argument, the agent says that QA will compare the two
files and explain any difference.

12.3. A customer logger sent back to the customer goes with the return challan,
never by separate post.

12.4. The agent never downloads or opens a customer logger. That is QA's work.

## 13. Records

13.1. Tickets, emails, call notes and quotations are kept in the customer
service system for three years, or seven years where the ticket concerns a
pharma critical excursion or a claim.

13.2. The agent writes what the customer said and what the agent said, not a
summary of both.

13.3. A ticket about an excursion carries the QCL-F-17 reference, so that an
auditor can move from the customer record to the QA record without asking.

13.4. Customer service's own notes on a customer's mood or manner are not kept
in the ticket. They belong in a conversation with the account manager.

## 14. How we write to customers

14.1. Customer service writes in plain English, in short sentences, without
internal shorthand. A customer does not know what a red sleeve, a DOM or a warm
pallet is, and should not have to ask.

14.2. Every email carries the customer's own reference first and the Quillfern
reference second, so that the customer's clerk can file it without reading it.

14.3. The agent writes the same facts to every contact at a customer. If two
contacts at one customer ask the same question, they get the same answer, copied
to each other.

14.4. Agents do not write in capitals, do not use exclamation marks, and do not
write "as per our records". They write what the record says.

14.5. Where a customer writes in Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or Assamese, the agent may
answer in that language if the agent writes it well. Anything that will be
signed by QA is written in English, with a translation attached if the customer
asks for one.

14.6. Templates are kept in the customer service system and reviewed twice a
year by the customer service lead. An agent who finds a template wrong tells the
lead and does not quietly edit a copy.

## 15. Customers who visit a DC

15.1. A customer who wants to visit a DC books the visit through the account
manager at least three working days ahead. The account manager tells the DC
manager and sends the customer the site's visitor rules.

15.2. Customer service does not promise a customer that they may see another
customer's stock, a quarantine bay or a cold room during a loading. The DC
manager decides what can be shown on the day.

15.3. A customer auditor's visit is handled by QA and the DC manager. Customer
service's part is to book it, send the paperwork and record the visit in the
ticket.

15.4. After a visit, the account manager asks the customer for comments in
writing within a week, and passes any comment on the site to the DC manager.

## 16. Month-end reporting

16.1. On the third working day of each month, the customer service lead sends
Finance and the account managers a report of the month's tickets by account:
bookings, late vehicles, complaints, credit notes raised and notices sent.

16.2. Each account manager reviews the report for their accounts and writes one
line per account on anything unusual. The lines are sent back to the lead within
two working days.

16.3. The report is not sent to customers as it stands. A customer who wants a
monthly service report gets one written for them by the account manager, from
the same figures.

16.4. Figures in the report come from the customer service system. An agent who
knows a figure is wrong corrects the ticket, not the report.

16.5. The BharatVac and Manjara Dairy accounts get a quarterly review meeting in
addition to the month-end report, held by their account managers with Finance
and QA.

## 17. New agents

17.1. A new agent spends the first two weeks listening to calls with an
experienced agent and reading closed tickets. They do not answer customers alone
in that time.

17.2. In the third week the new agent answers calls with the experienced agent
listening. The experienced agent signs the new agent off when they are ready,
not on a fixed day.

17.3. New agents spend one day at Nagpur DC in their first month, with a shift
lead, so that they have seen a cold room, a dock and a gate before they speak to
customers about them.

17.4. The customer service lead meets each new agent at the end of their first
month and again at three months.

## 18. Customer data

18.1. Customer contact details are kept in the customer service system only. They
are not copied into personal phones, spreadsheets or chat groups.

18.2. A customer's prices, volumes and incident history are not shared with any
other customer, even one in the same group of companies, without the account
manager's written clearance.

18.3. Recordings of calls are kept for ninety days unless a ticket needs them for
longer.

18.4. A request from a customer to see the data Quillfern holds on them goes to
the account manager and to Pune HQ's legal contact on the day it arrives.

## 19. Busy seasons

19.1. Vaccine season, the dairy summer peak and the festival months each bring
more bookings than the desk handles in an ordinary month. The customer service
lead publishes the season's cover plan a month ahead.

19.2. In busy seasons no agent takes more than three days' leave in a row, and
the Sunday on-call agent is doubled.

19.3. Account managers warn their customers before each season which days are
already heavily booked, so that customers book early rather than late.

19.4. Bookings in a busy season are taken in the order they arrive. An agent who
is asked to move one customer ahead of another passes the request to the account
manager, who decides with Fleet Control.

19.5. After each season the lead holds a short review with the agents: what went
wrong, which templates failed, and which customers were caught out. The notes
feed the next season's plan.

## 20. Working with other teams

20.1. Customer service works most closely with Fleet Control, QA and Finance. Each
has a named contact for customer service, listed on the desk's contact sheet and
updated by the lead whenever someone changes role.

20.2. An agent who needs something from another team asks that team's named
contact, in writing where the answer will go to a customer. A corridor answer is
confirmed by email before it is passed on.

20.3. Customer service does not pass blame to another team in anything written
to a customer. A problem is described as Quillfern's, whichever team it began
in.

20.4. Once a quarter the lead meets the Fleet Control, QA and Finance contacts
together to go through the tickets that crossed teams and took longest to close.

20.5. Suggestions from agents about other teams' work go to the lead, who passes
them on. Agents are encouraged to make them; most improvements to the booking
checks in section 5 came from agents.

## 21. Review of this handbook

21.1. The customer service lead reviews this handbook every January and after
any change to the notification matrix, the rate card or the vehicle list that
alters what an agent must check.

21.2. Agents send suggested changes to the lead at any time. Each suggestion is
answered, whether or not it is taken up.

21.3. A revised handbook is issued with a short note of what changed, and every
agent confirms in writing that they have read the note within a week of issue.

21.4. Earlier issues of the handbook are withdrawn from the shared drive when a
new one is issued, so that an agent searching for an answer finds only the one
in force.
