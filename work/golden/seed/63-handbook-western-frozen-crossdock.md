# Western Frozen Cross-Dock Handbook

How the western frozen cross-dock is run, written for the people who work there
and for anyone from another site who is sent to help in a busy week. The site
moves frozen food from manufacturers' trailers to city delivery vans for Mumbai,
Thane and Navi Mumbai. It is a cross-dock first and a store second: stock is
meant to come in, be checked, be sorted and go out, and the building is sized
for that and nothing more. Company rules on temperature, driving and hygiene
apply here as everywhere; this handbook says how they are carried out in this
building, with these doors, this yard and this traffic.

## 1. Why the site exists

1.1. Until 2024 Quillfern's frozen customers in the Mumbai region either sent
their own trailers all the way to restaurant back doors or paid for cold-store
space they did not need. Neither worked. Long trailers cannot turn in the lanes
of Dadar or Bandra, and a cold store charges for weeks when the customer wants a
night.

1.2. The cross-dock at Bhiwandi opened on 7 October 2024 in a leased shed on the
service road of the Mumbai–Nashik highway. It takes full trailers from brand
owners in the evening and sends the same stock out the next morning on small
frozen vans that can reach a restaurant's back door.

1.3. Nothing is meant to stay longer than 48 hours. A pallet that is still on the
floor at hour 48 is reported on the morning call, and its owner is told the
next day's plan for it before noon.

1.4. The site does not handle vaccines, insulin or any other medicine. A trailer
that arrives with pharma stock on it is not unloaded. The shift lead calls the
customer, and the trailer leaves with its seal intact and a note on the gate
log.

1.5. Dairy is accepted only frozen: ice cream, frozen paneer cubes and frozen
desserts. Chilled milk and fresh paneer go to sites that have a chilled room;
this one has only an anteroom held at marshalling temperature.

1.6. The site has no retail counter and sells nothing to the public. People who
walk in from the service road asking to buy frozen stock are turned away at the
gate, politely.

## 2. Building, bays and who is where

2.1. The shed is a single span with six dock doors on the east wall, numbered D1
to D6 from the gate side. D1 and D2 take incoming trailers, D3 opens into the
cell behind it, and D4 to D6 load outgoing vans.

2.2. The freezer chamber FC-A runs at -22 C and holds 1,140 pallet positions in
drive-in racking. The marshalling anteroom in front of it is held at +4 C so
that cartons being picked for vans do not collect frost.

2.3. The plant room is on the north side, outside the insulated envelope, with
its own door to the yard. The office is on a mezzanine above D1 and D2, with a
window over the dock so that the order desk can see what is being unloaded.

2.4. The truck scale is at the gate, on the left as a vehicle enters, and every
inbound trailer is weighed in and out. The guard cabin is beside the scale.

2.5. Battery charging for the reach trucks is in a fenced bay outside the
anteroom. Nobody charges a truck inside FC-A, and nobody leaves a battery on
charge unattended across a shift change.

2.6. The canteen and lockers are in a single-storey block beside the gate.
Drivers waiting for a dock may use the canteen. They may not walk past the
yellow line painted along the dock edge.

2.7. The first-aid room is next to the canteen. The nearest hospital with a
casualty department is on the Kalyan road, about 7 km away; the route is
printed on the back of the first-aid room door.

## 3. Gate and apron

3.1. The gate is staffed round the clock. Every vehicle is logged in with its
registration, its seal number, the customer and the time, and logged out the
same way. A vehicle that leaves without being logged out is reported to the site
manager the same shift.

3.2. The apron takes nine vehicles. Brand owners often drop a trailer and take
the tractor away, collecting the empty trailer later; a dropped trailer stands
on the apron with its reefer unit running on the shore-power posts along the
south fence.

3.3. Reefer units are plugged into shore power within 15 minutes of a trailer
being dropped. A driver who leaves a reefer running on diesel on the apron is
asked to plug it in, and the guard notes the time on the gate log.

3.4. Detention on a trailer that a customer has dropped or sent is billed to
that customer, not absorbed by the site. The clock starts at gate-in; nothing is charged for the first three hours, and each started hour after that costs Rs. 900. On the invoice it appears as a wait-time line and is taxed in the same way
as one.

3.5. Until 31 March 2026 four hours were free. The window came down from 1 April
2026, when two apron bays were lost to the enlarged cell behind D3 and every
remaining bay became worth more.

3.6. The guard does not decide detention. The office works it out from the gate
log at the end of each day, and a customer who disputes a line is sent the gate
log entries themselves, not a summary of them.

3.7. Reversing onto D1 and D2 after dark needs a spotter with a torch. The apron
lights run from the site's own supply and stay on when the grid fails.

3.8. Trailers are never parked across the fire lane that runs along the north
fence to the plant room door. The lane is marked in red hatching and is checked
by the guard at every shift change.

## 4. Behind door 3

4.1. The cell behind D3 is BC-1, a blast cell with an air temperature of -35 C
and room for six pallets. It exists because brand owners do not always send
stock as cold as their paperwork says, and a frozen store is not built to pull
warm stock down; it is built to hold cold stock cold.

4.2. Every inbound pallet is probed at the dock: one carton from the middle of
the pallet, probe into the product core. Anything with a core warmer than minus 12 on the arrival probe goes behind door 3 first, whatever the paperwork says.

4.3. The target in the cell is a core of minus 18. It gets six hours from the moment the doors close. A load that has not reached target by then is reported to QA and held, and the customer is told the same day.

4.4. Stock that comes out of the cell is probed again before it goes into FC-A,
and both readings are written on the inbound sheet with their times.

4.5. The cell is never used as overflow storage, however busy the night. A
pallet parked in the cell because FC-A is full blocks the next soft arrival,
and the next soft arrival then sits on the dock warming further.

4.6. The cell's fans put a hard frost on cartons that sit too close to the coil.
Pallets are placed on the painted floor marks and nowhere else.

4.7. Whether cell time is charged depends on the customer's contract, not on
this handbook. The shift lead records the door-closing and door-opening times on
the cell log so that the office can apply whatever the contract says.

4.8. The cell was enlarged from four to six pallet places in March 2026. The two
extra places took space from the apron, which is why the apron now holds nine
vehicles and not eleven.

## 5. People and shifts

5.1. The site manager is Prakash Jadhav. He is the escalation contact for the
site at any hour and the only person who may approve a change to the van plan
after it has been published.

5.2. The site works three shifts: 06:00 to 14:00, 14:00 to 22:00 and 22:00 to
06:00. Inbound trailers are mostly unloaded on the night shift; outbound vans are
loaded from 04:30 on the morning shift.

5.3. Each shift has a shift lead. Swati Kulkarni leads the morning shift, Imran
Shaikh the afternoon shift, and the night shift lead is named on the weekly
roster on the canteen board, because two people share it.

5.4. Floor labour is supplied under contract by Sai Kripa Manpower. Contract
staff are inducted by the shift lead before their first shift and wear the
green bibs; Quillfern staff wear blue.

5.5. Nobody works inside FC-A for more than 45 minutes without a 15-minute warm
break in the canteen. The shift lead keeps the break board and moves names on
it, not the workers themselves.

5.6. Freezer suits, gloves and boots are issued to named people. A worker without
a suit does not enter FC-A, even for a minute to fetch one carton.

5.7. Overtime is approved by the site manager in advance. A shift that cannot
finish a trailer leaves it for the next shift with a handover note; it does not
stay on past the end of shift to finish it.

## 6. A day in the office

6.1. The office takes orders from customers by e-mail and through the customer
portal. Each order names the delivery points, the cases and the delivery window
for every stop.

6.2. The order desk builds the next morning's van plan from the orders in hand.
The plan is checked by the afternoon shift lead before it is published to the
van drivers at 19:00.

6.3. The cut-off decides which van an order joins. Anything received by 14:00 goes out the following day; anything after that goes the day after. An
order marked urgent after the cut-off is passed to the site manager, who may add
it to a van if there is room and the customer has agreed to pay for a special
drop.

6.4. Until 11 January 2026 the line was drawn at 15:00; it moved an hour earlier when the first outbound wave moved to 05:00.

6.5. Orders received on a Saturday after the cut-off go out on Monday. The site
does not run outbound vans on Sundays except before Diwali and Christmas, when
the site manager publishes a special plan.

6.6. A customer who changes an order after it has joined a van plan is told
that the change will go out on the next plan, not the current one. The order
desk does not reload a van that has been picked.

6.7. Every order and every change is kept in the portal. The order desk never
works from a phone call alone; a call is followed by an e-mail before anything
is picked.

## 7. The plant room

7.1. The site is cooled by a carbon dioxide refrigeration rack. The plant room
holds the compressors, the receiver and the gas cooler, and the gas cooler's
fans sit on the roof above it.

7.2. Gas detectors in the plant room, in FC-A and in the cell watch for leaks. A
first stage lights the amber beacon over the plant room door and calls the plant
lead. A second stage sounds the site siren.

7.3. Only the plant lead and the refrigeration contractor's technician enter the
plant room. Both carry a personal gas monitor clipped to the chest.

7.4. When the leak siren sounds, forklifts stop where they are, engines are
switched off and nobody goes back for stock, a phone or a vehicle. Everyone walks to BW-A, the painted square beside the truck scale, and stays there until the plant lead clears the site in person.

7.5. The shift lead counts heads against the gate log and the contract labour
sheet within ten minutes and phones the count to the site manager. Drivers
waiting on the apron are counted by the guard.

7.6. A siren test is run on the first Monday of every month at 11:00, announced
the day before. Nobody leaves the building for a test.

7.7. The refrigeration contractor services the rack every quarter and after any
leak alarm. The service sheet is kept in the plant room folder and copied to the
site manager.

## 8. When the grid drops

8.1. The site has two gensets on a common panel. They start automatically when
grid power fails and carry FC-A, the cell, the plant room and the apron lights.
The canteen and the office air conditioning are not on the genset panel.

8.2. The gensets are test-run under load every Thursday at 10:00 for twenty
minutes. The run time, the oil level and any alarm on the panel are written in
the genset log.

8.3. The gensets share one day tank and one bulk tank. The tank is never allowed below 1,500 litres; the night lead dips it at 22:00 and orders a top-up from the listed bowser at 2,500.

8.4. During a long outage the night lead dips the tank every four hours and
writes each reading in the log. A bowser that does not arrive within four hours
of an order is chased by the site manager, not by the night lead.

8.5. Nobody refuels a genset while it is running. Fuel spills are covered with
the sand from the bin beside the tank and reported the same shift.

8.6. After the grid returns, the gensets run on for ten minutes before they are
shut down, and the panel is checked for any alarm that was raised during the
outage.

## 9. Wood in, wood out

9.1. Pallets at the site are exchanged one for one. A trailer that brings in
twenty-two loaded pallets takes away twenty-two empty ones, so that neither side
ends up holding the other's wood.

9.2. Pallets are counted at the dock when a trailer is unloaded and again when
the empties are loaded. Both counts go on the pallet sheet and are signed by the
driver and the shift lead.

9.3. Every pallet handed in is looked at on the dock by the shift lead: split
boards, cracked blocks and missing nails are the usual faults. Each one that cannot carry a load is billed back at Rs. 450 to whoever delivered it.

9.4. Damaged pallets are stacked by the south fence for the repairer, who
collects on the first and third Fridays of each month. Repaired pallets come
back into the exchange pool; pallets that cannot be repaired are sold as scrap.

9.5. A driver who disputes a damaged-pallet count photographs the pallet before
leaving, and the office reviews the photograph against the shift lead's note.

9.6. Pallets are never used as dunnage in a van, never burned on site and never
given away, however broken.

## 10. Doors, curtains and ice

10.1. Every door into FC-A and the cell is a rapid-roll door with a strip curtain
behind it. A door that does not close fully within 20 seconds of a vehicle
passing is reported to the shift lead.

10.2. The door heaters keep the frame of each freezer door from icing. The
morning shift lead checks each heater light at the start of the shift and logs
any that is out.

10.3. Ice on the floor in front of a freezer door is chipped and cleared at once,
not left for the next shift. The ice is swept into the drain channel outside,
never left in the anteroom to melt.

10.4. A strip curtain with a torn strip is replaced within a week. The spare
strips are in the store cage in the anteroom.

10.5. The dock seals at D4 to D6 are checked by the guard each evening. A seal
that does not meet the van body lets warm air into the anteroom and frost onto
the picking cartons.

## 11. Racking and the floor

11.1. The drive-in racking in FC-A is inspected by the racking contractor every
six months. Any damage found between inspections is reported the same shift,
and the bay is closed with chains until it is checked.

11.2. Reach trucks keep to the marked aisles. Nobody walks in an aisle where a
truck is working; pickers wait at the aisle end until the truck has left.

11.3. Pallets are placed so that no carton overhangs the pallet edge. A pallet
with overhanging cartons is restacked in the anteroom before it goes into FC-A.

11.4. The floor of FC-A has a heated sub-floor to stop frost heave. The
sub-floor temperature is checked weekly by the plant lead and written in the
plant room folder.

11.5. Spilled product in FC-A is cleared at once, because frozen spillage under
a truck wheel is as slippery as ice.

## 12. Cleaning and pests

12.1. The anteroom floor is swept at the end of every shift and washed once a
day, after the last van has left. FC-A is swept, never washed, because water
freezes where it lands.

12.2. The pest contractor visits on the second Tuesday of each month and on call
after any sighting. Bait stations are numbered on the wall plan in the office.
Nobody moves a bait station to make room for stock.

12.3. Birds roost under the apron canopy in the monsoon. The netting is checked
after every storm, and a bird inside the anteroom is reported to the shift lead
at once.

12.4. Waste is separated at the back of the shed: cardboard, stretch wrap and
general waste. Damaged product is never put in the general waste; it follows the
customer's instructions and the company's destruction rules.

12.5. The canteen is cleaned by the canteen contractor. Complaints about the
canteen go to the site manager in writing.

## 13. Visitors and audits

13.1. Every visitor signs in at the guard cabin, wears a visitor badge and is
escorted by a shift lead or the site manager. A visitor who has not signed in is
not shown round, whoever they say they are.

13.2. Customer auditors may see any record about their own stock and any part of
the building. They may not see another customer's stock or another customer's
paperwork.

13.3. Visitors entering FC-A wear a freezer jacket from the rack in the anteroom
and stay no longer than 15 minutes.

13.4. Photographs inside the shed are taken only with the site manager's
permission and only of the thing being audited.

13.5. After any audit the site manager writes a short note of what was asked,
what was shown and what was promised, and keeps it in the audit folder.

## 14. Paper we keep

14.1. The gate log, the inbound sheets, the cell log, the pallet sheets, the van
plans and the genset log are kept for three years.

14.2. Records about a pallet that was held, destroyed or disputed are kept for
seven years, with the photographs.

14.3. Paper sheets are scanned at the end of each week and filed in the site
folder. The paper originals go to the archive room in the office.

14.4. A missing sheet is written as missing in the weekly scan index, never
rewritten from memory.

## 15. Review of this handbook

15.1. The site manager reviews this handbook every June and after any serious
incident, audit finding or change to the building.

15.2. Revision 3 of June 2026 recorded the new apron free window, the larger cell
and the earlier order cut-off, and moved the genset rules into their own
section.

15.3. Suggestions from staff go to the site manager at any time. Each one is
answered, whether or not it is taken up.

15.4. A revised handbook replaces every earlier copy in the office, the guard
cabin and the canteen on the day it is issued.
