# Service Partner Onboarding Standard

## 1. What this standard is for

1.1. Quillfern buildings and vehicles are kept running by outside firms as much as by our own staff. Refrigeration engineers, generator agents, the people who service dock doors and levellers, pest controllers, cleaners, electricians, calibration laboratories and fire-equipment agents all come through our gates, and some of them work on the very plant that keeps customers' stock in range.

1.2. This standard says how such a firm becomes an approved service partner, what it must keep up to stay approved, and how it leaves the list. It applies to every site opened from 2025 onwards and to every firm that is newly approved anywhere in the company from the date of this revision.

1.3. It does not cover suppliers of goods, such as seals or forms, who deliver to Stores and never work on site. Those are approved under the purchasing rules.

1.4. It is written for Procurement, which keeps the list; for site managers, who call partners in; for QA, which approves partners who work near product; and for the engineering pool, which judges the technical competence of partners who work on plant.

1.5. "Partner" in this standard means a firm, not a person. The people the firm sends are its technicians.

## 2. Three kinds of partner

2.1. Category A partners work on temperature-critical plant: refrigeration, the controllers and alarms on cold rooms, the generators and changeover gear that keep them running, and the reefer units on vehicles.

2.2. Category B partners work on the building and its fixed equipment but not on temperature-critical plant: dock doors and levellers, electrical work outside the plant room, roofing, fire equipment, pest control.

2.3. Category C partners neither touch plant nor enter product zones: office cleaning, canteen, landscaping, office IT.

2.4. The category decides how much checking a firm needs. A firm doing work in two categories is approved in the higher one.

2.5. Procurement assigns the category when the firm is proposed. The site manager or the engineering pool may ask for a firm to be moved to a higher category; nobody may move one lower without Procurement and QA agreeing.

## 3. Before anybody calls them

3.1. A firm that is not on the approved list may not be given a work order, a gate pass or access to any site, even for a small job, even for a quotation visit inside a product zone.

3.2. A site that needs a firm not yet on the list proposes it to Procurement on the partner proposal form, with the reason, the work and the sites concerned.

3.3. In a genuine emergency, where no approved partner can attend and stock is at risk, the site manager may call in an unapproved firm once. The technician is escorted throughout, the job is limited to the emergency, and Procurement is told within one working day. A second call to the same unapproved firm is not an emergency.

3.4. Approval normally takes four weeks for Category A, two for B and one for C, counted from receipt of a complete set of papers.

3.5. A firm is told plainly why it was not approved, if it was not.

## 4. Papers we ask for

4.1. Every partner sends its registration certificate, its GST registration, its PAN, a cancelled cheque for its bank account and a signed copy of the company's code of conduct for partners.

4.2. Every partner carries public liability cover against injury to people and damage to property on our sites. Category A partners hold cover of at least Rs. 1 crore for any one occurrence. Category B and C partners hold at least Rs. 50 lakh. The certificate names Quillfern as an interested party and is sent before the first work order and again on each renewal.

4.3. Partners whose technicians are employed by them, rather than self-employed, send proof of workmen's compensation cover or the statutory equivalent.

4.4. Category A partners send their refrigerant-handling licence and the gas-handling certificates of each technician they will send.

4.5. A partner whose cover lapses is suspended automatically on the day it lapses. Procurement keeps the expiry dates and writes to the partner thirty days before each one.

## 5. Seeing them at home

5.1. Category A partners are visited at their own premises before approval by someone from Procurement and someone from the engineering pool. The visit looks at the workshop, the tools, the stock of spares, the gas recovery equipment, the training records and the way the firm logs its jobs.

5.2. The visit report scores the firm against a written checklist, and a firm that fails any safety item on the checklist is not approved until it has put it right and been visited again.

5.3. Category B partners are visited only where the engineering pool asks. Category C partners are not visited.

5.4. The visit is unannounced in its timing but agreed in principle: the firm knows a visit will come within a month and agrees to admit the visitors on any working day.

## 6. References

6.1. Category A and B firms give two references from customers for whom they have done similar work in the past two years. At least one reference is spoken to by phone.

6.2. A reference from a relative of the firm's owner, or from another firm under the same ownership, does not count.

6.3. Procurement writes a short note of each reference call and files it.

6.4. A bad reference does not automatically stop approval, but it is discussed with the firm and the note of that discussion is filed with the reference.

## 7. People at the gate

7.1. A technician who will enter a pharma zone, a cold room holding pharma stock or a QA area must have a police verification certificate obtained through the local police station. The partner obtains it and sends us a copy before the technician's first visit.

7.2. The certificate must be renewed every three years. A technician whose certificate is more than three years old is refused at the gate, whatever the job, until a renewed certificate arrives.

7.3. Technicians who work only outside product zones need no police verification under this standard, though a site may require it for its own reasons.

7.4. Partners send the name, photograph and mobile number of every technician they may send. The gate is not expected to admit a technician who is not on the partner's list, even if the firm calls ahead.

7.5. Technicians leave their phones in the gate locker before entering a pharma zone unless the job needs a phone and the shift lead agrees.

## 8. The first visit

8.1. Before working on any site for the first time, each technician completes the site induction: a walk round the site with a shift lead, the fire and evacuation routes, the rules on product zones, the permit system, the waste rules and whom to call.

8.2. The induction takes about ninety minutes and is signed for by the technician and the shift lead.

8.3. The technician is then issued an induction card bearing the photograph, the firm, the category and the date. The card is valid for twelve months from the date of issue at every Quillfern site opened since 2025, so that a technician who has been inducted at one need not repeat it at another within the year; each site still gives a short walk round on the first visit.

8.4. An expired card is not a gate pass. The technician repeats the induction.

8.5. A card is withdrawn at once if the technician breaks a product-zone rule, and Procurement is told.

## 9. When we call them

9.1. Partners agree to attend within a set time, which is written into their agreement. Times run from the moment the site's call is logged by the partner's desk, not from the moment the site first tried to call.

9.2. For a Category A partner, an emergency — a plant fault with stock at risk — is attended on site within four hours, at any hour of any day. An urgent fault, where stock is not yet at risk, is attended within twenty-four hours. A routine job is attended at the agreed slot.

9.3. Category B partners attend emergencies within eight hours. Category C partners work to agreed slots only.

9.4. The site logs the call time and the arrival time on the work order, and the partner's score at the year end is built partly from those times.

9.5. A partner that cannot meet an emergency time says so at once, so that the site can call the next partner on the list.

## 10. Before tools come out

10.1. Any work that could start a fire, release refrigerant, expose live electrical parts, or put someone at height needs a written permit to work, issued by the shift lead before the job starts.

10.2. A permit for hot work — brazing, welding, cutting, grinding — is valid for one shift only, and never for more than eight hours. Work that runs past the end of the shift stops, and a fresh permit is issued if it is to continue.

10.3. Hot work needs a fire watch who stays for thirty minutes after the work ends.

10.4. The permit names the technician, the job, the place, the hazards, the controls and the time limits. The technician signs it, and signs it again when the work is finished.

10.5. A technician who starts work before the permit is issued is sent off site, and the partner is told the same day.

## 11. Gas in, gas out

11.1. Category A partners recover refrigerant from our plant into their own recovery cylinders. Refrigerant is never vented, however small the quantity.

11.2. For every job that removes or adds refrigerant, the partner gives the site a gas record showing the cylinder numbers, the weights before and after, the type of gas, and what happened to recovered gas: reused in the same plant, sent for reclamation or sent for destruction.

11.3. The gas record reaches the site within seven days of the job. A job without a gas record is not paid until it arrives.

11.4. The site files the gas records with the plant's own log, so that the plant's history shows every gram that went in and out.

11.5. Leak checks on plant that holds more than the legal threshold of gas are done at the intervals the law sets, and the partner records each one in the plant log.

## 12. Passing work on

12.1. A partner may not pass any part of a Quillfern job to another firm without Procurement's written consent before the work is done. A firm that subcontracts without consent is suspended.

12.2. Consent is given only where the other firm meets the same category's requirements in this standard, including the papers in section 4 and police verification where section 7 requires it.

12.3. Specialist work that the partner cannot do itself — crane hire, scaffolding, a manufacturer's own warranty technician — may be brought in with the shift lead's agreement on the day, but the partner remains responsible for it.

12.4. Agency labour sent in place of the partner's own technicians counts as subcontracting.

## 13. Invoices

13.1. Partners invoice against the work order number, one invoice per work order, with the signed work order and, for Category A, the gas record attached.

13.2. Accounts payable checks each invoice against the work order and the agreed rates. An invoice is accepted when it matches; one that does not is returned within five working days with the reason.

13.3. An accepted invoice is paid within 45 days of acceptance. Partners registered as micro or small enterprises are paid within the period the law requires for them, if that is shorter.

13.4. Payment is made only to the bank account on the partner's file. A request to change the account is confirmed by phone with a known contact at the partner before anything is changed.

13.5. No advance payment is made to any partner without the Finance head's approval.

## 14. The annual look back

14.1. Every partner is scored once a year by Procurement, using the site managers' records and the engineering pool's opinion. The score is out of 100 and covers attendance times, quality of work, safety, paperwork, and conduct on site.

14.2. A partner scoring 65 or more stays on the list. A partner scoring below 65 is put on probation for six months with a written plan of what must improve.

14.3. A partner put on probation in two consecutive years is removed from the list, whatever its score at the end of the second probation.

14.4. A partner may see its score and the records behind it, and may answer in writing before the score is final.

14.5. Scores are not shared with other partners.

## 15. Leaving the list

15.1. A partner leaves the list when it asks to, when its approval lapses, when it is removed after probation, or when it is removed for a serious breach: falsified papers, a safety breach that put people at risk, venting of refrigerant, or a breach of confidentiality.

15.2. A partner removed for a serious breach is not re-approved for three years.

15.3. On leaving, the partner returns all induction cards, keys and documents, and its open work orders are closed or handed to another partner.

15.4. Sites are told the same day when a partner leaves the list, and the gate list is updated.

## 16. Gifts and conflicts

16.1. Nobody at Quillfern accepts a gift, a meal, travel or entertainment from a partner or a firm proposing to become one, beyond token items worth less than Rs. 1,000 in all in a year, such as a diary or a box of sweets at Diwali.

16.2. Anything above that is declined, or, where declining would cause offence, handed to HR and recorded.

16.3. A member of staff with a family or financial interest in a partner declares it to Procurement and takes no part in that partner's approval, scoring or work orders.

16.4. Partners are told these rules when they are approved, and asked to tell Procurement if anyone at Quillfern asks them for anything.

## 17. Records

17.1. Procurement keeps each partner's file: the proposal, the papers, the visit report, the references, the approval, the agreements, every year's score, any probation plan and the reasons for leaving the list.

17.2. A partner's file is kept for six years after its last work order, or six years after it leaves the list, whichever comes later.

17.3. Work orders, permits and gas records are kept with the site's own records for the periods set by the documents that govern them.

17.4. Partner files are confidential. A partner may see its own file on request.

## 18. Changes to this standard

18.1. Revision 1 (2025) was a one-page checklist used during the 2025 site openings. Revision 2 (February 2026) replaced it with this standard, raised the papers required of Category A partners and set the induction card's validity across sites.

18.2. Procurement reviews this standard every January with QA and the engineering pool.

18.3. Partners are sent a short note of every change, and their agreements are updated at the next renewal.
