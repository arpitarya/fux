# Transit Loss and Cargo Claims Procedure, TPC 2026

## 1. Why this procedure exists

1.1. Quillfern moves stock that belongs to other people. When a load is lost, stolen, crushed or spoiled between our gate and the customer's, two separate questions arise at once. The first is what we owe the customer under their contract. The second is what we can recover under our own cover. Until 2025 both questions were handled by whoever happened to be nearest the problem, and the results were uneven: some claims were made twice, some were never made at all, and in one case a damaged load was sold for scrap before anybody had asked whether that was allowed.

1.2. TPC is Quillfern's Transit Protection Cover. It is an open cargo policy placed with Sahyadri General Insurance Co. Ltd. through Konark Insurance Brokers. "Open" means that every dispatch under a Quillfern dispatch note is covered automatically, without a separate certificate for each trip, so long as the dispatch is declared afterwards and the conditions below are kept.

1.3. This procedure says what happens from the moment someone at Quillfern learns of a loss until the claim is paid, rejected or abandoned. It is written for site managers, fleet planners, shift leads, QA officers and the claims desk in Finance. Drivers are not expected to read it; the cab card tells them what to do at the scene.

1.4. It does not set any temperature limit, any customer notification window or any rule about quarantine. Those come from the documents that govern them, and a claim never changes them. Nothing in this procedure allows a site to keep, release or destroy stock faster because a claim is pending.

## 2. The cover in one page

2.1. The policy number is TPC/2026/0417 and the policy year runs from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. Renewal is negotiated by the claims desk with the broker each February.

2.2. The cover applies to goods in transit by road under a Quillfern dispatch note, in owned vehicles and in hired vehicles alike. It covers the goods from the moment loading begins at the origin dock until unloading ends at the destination. It does not cover goods sitting in a building, which are insured under the property policy, and it does not cover air or sea legs booked by the customer.

2.3. Every dispatch is declared to the broker in a monthly return by the 10th of the following month. The return is generated from the dispatch system by the claims desk and checked by Finance. An undeclared dispatch is not automatically uninsured, but the insurer may ask why it was missed, and a pattern of misses can void the cover.

2.4. The premium is charged on the declared value at 0.11 per cent, adjusted at the year end. The most the insurer will pay across all claims in one policy year is Rs. 9 crore. When claims paid pass Rs. 6 crore the broker tells the claims desk, and the desk tells the Finance head the same day.

2.5. Goods are valued at the value written on the dispatch note or the value in the customer's schedule held by Finance, whichever is lower. A value written in a customer's e-mail after a loss is recorded, but it is not the basis of the claim.

## 3. Who does what

3.1. The claims desk is a small team in Finance: Rashmi Kulkarni, who leads it, and two analysts. The desk answers extension 517 during office hours and the shared mailbox at all times. It owns every claim from intimation to closure, and it is the only team that speaks to the broker or the insurer about a claim.

3.2. The site manager where the loss is discovered owns the evidence: photographs, seals, temperature downloads, the damaged goods themselves and the names of everyone who saw them. The site manager hands that evidence to the claims desk and does not argue the claim.

3.3. QA decides what happens to damaged stock as stock: whether it can be released, returned or must be destroyed. That decision is taken on quality grounds alone. The claims desk is told the decision; it does not ask for a different one.

3.4. Fleet Control supplies the vehicle's trip record, the driver's call log and, for hired vehicles, the vendor's name and purchase order.

3.5. The broker's liaison for Quillfern is Ashok Menon at Konark. He receives intimations, arranges surveyors, carries documents to the insurer and chases payment. He works for us, not for the insurer, and the desk should use him as an adviser on what the insurer will accept.

## 4. The first hour at the scene

4.1. People first. Nobody risks injury to protect stock, look at a seal or take a photograph. Where there has been a crash, the vehicle and the road are made safe and emergency services are called before anyone thinks about the load.

4.2. Once it is safe, the driver or the receiving shift lead photographs the vehicle from all four sides, the seal before it is cut, the inside of the body when the doors are opened, the damaged goods, and any label or batch number that can be read. Photographs carry the date and time, and they are sent to the site manager the same hour.

4.3. Nothing is moved except to protect people or to stop further damage, such as moving chilled stock into a working cold room. Whatever is moved is photographed first and written down.

4.4. Nothing is sold, given away, thrown away or destroyed. Damaged stock goes to quarantine like any other doubtful stock, and stays there until QA has decided and, where it applies, the surveyor has seen it.

4.5. Cut seals, broken packaging, the driver's paperwork and the temperature download are kept together in a bag marked with the dispatch note number. Police matters — theft, a crash with injury, a hijack — need a copy of the first information report, and the site manager asks for it on the day.

## 5. The calendar starts

5.1. The date of discovery is the first day on which anybody at Quillfern knew, or should have known, that goods had been lost or damaged. It is usually the delivery day, but not always: a shortage found at a quarterly count may be discovered weeks after the trip.

5.2. The site manager tells the claims desk on the day of discovery, by e-mail with the photographs attached. A phone call is welcome but is followed by the e-mail.

5.3. The claims desk sends written intimation to the insurer, through the broker, within seven days of the date of discovery. Intimation means a written notice that names the policy, the dispatch note, the place, the date and the nature of the loss, with an estimate if one is known. It is sent whether or not the claim looks large enough to be worth making, and whether or not the facts are yet clear. A call to Ashok Menon is not intimation.

5.4. Late intimation is the commonest reason insurers refuse a claim. In 2024 the company lost a claim of Rs. 4.6 lakh because a site waited for the customer's own figures before telling anyone. The rule now is to tell early and correct later.

5.5. The claims desk opens a claim file the same day it receives the site manager's e-mail. Each file has a number in the series TPC26-nnn, and the number goes on every later document.

## 6. What stays with us

6.1. Every claim under TPC carries a deductible: a part of each loss that Quillfern bears itself before the insurer pays anything. For 2026–27 the deductible is Rs. 40,000 per consignment. On a loss of Rs. 30,000 the insurer pays nothing; on a loss of Rs. 1,00,000 it pays Rs. 60,000.

6.2. A consignment is one dispatch note. Three pallets on one note make one consignment and carry one deductible. Three notes on one vehicle make three consignments, and each note's loss carries its own deductible.

6.3. Finance books the deductible to the cost centre of the site where the loss happened, or the fleet cost centre where the loss happened on the road. Sites are told their deductible total every quarter.

6.4. Losses below the deductible are still intimated and still recorded. The insurer prices next year's cover on the whole loss record, not only on what it paid, and a record full of gaps is priced as if the gaps were bad news.

6.5. Customers are never told the deductible figure. It is a term of our cover, not of their contract, and quoting it invites an argument about who should bear it.

## 7. Machinery that stops

7.1. A loss of temperature on its own is not an accident. Under TPC, spoilage caused by a failure of the vehicle's refrigerating machinery is paid only where the machinery stopped working for at least four consecutive hours. Spoilage after a shorter stoppage is Quillfern's loss, not the insurer's, unless the stoppage followed a collision, an overturning or a fire, in which case the ordinary accident cover applies.

7.2. The four hours are counted from the unit's own stop event in its controller log, not from the first temperature alarm and not from the time the driver noticed. A unit that stops, restarts, and stops again has two stoppages, not one long one.

7.3. The stoppage is proved by the controller's event log and the temperature record together. A driver's statement, however honest, does not prove it alone, and a phone photograph of a dark display is evidence only of the moment it was taken.

7.4. Fleet Control downloads the controller log as soon as a claim on refrigeration grounds is possible, before the unit is serviced. A mechanic who clears the fault log has destroyed the evidence, so workshops are told not to clear any log on a vehicle with an open claim.

7.5. Customers' stock that warmed during a long stoppage is still handled under the quality rules, whether or not the claim succeeds. A failed claim never leads to stock being released.

## 8. Heavy days on the road

8.1. The insurer's exposure on any single vehicle is capped. The most it will pay for all goods on one vehicle on one trip is Rs. 1.2 crore, however many consignments are on board.

8.2. A full vaccine load easily goes past that figure, and so does a frozen load of premium seafood. Any dispatch whose total declared value on one vehicle is above Rs. 1.2 crore is declared to the broker in writing at least 48 hours before the vehicle leaves. The broker then gets the insurer's agreement to a higher figure for that trip, or tells us to split the load.

8.3. Fleet planners check the declared value of every pharma critical and every high-value frozen load when they build the week's plan, and send the list of trips over the figure to the claims desk on Thursday.

8.4. A trip that goes over the figure without a declaration is still covered up to Rs. 1.2 crore. Anything above that is uninsured, and the site manager who allowed the load explains why in writing.

8.5. Splitting a load costs a second vehicle and a second crew. Planners should prefer splitting to asking for a higher figure only where the second vehicle is already on the road; the broker's higher figure usually costs less than an empty run.

## 9. Who comes to look

9.1. Large losses are inspected by an independent surveyor appointed by the insurer. Quillfern does not choose the surveyor and does not pay the surveyor's fee. Where the estimated loss is above Rs. 3 lakh, the broker asks the insurer to appoint one on the day of intimation.

9.2. Losses estimated at Rs. 3 lakh or less are assessed by the claims desk from the photographs and documents, and the file is sent to the insurer for a desk decision. The insurer may still send a surveyor to a small loss if it chooses.

9.3. Until the surveyor has seen the goods, nothing is destroyed, sold or returned to the customer unless QA rules that keeping it is a hazard. If QA does rule that, the goods are photographed in detail, a sample is kept where that is safe, and the broker is told before they go.

9.4. Surveyors usually come within 72 hours. The site manager or a named deputy meets them, shows them everything they ask for and answers their questions plainly. Nobody offers an opinion on whether the claim should be paid.

9.5. The surveyor's report goes to the insurer, not to us. The broker asks for a copy and the claims desk files it.

## 10. Paper the insurer wants

10.1. A complete claim file holds: the intimation and its acknowledgement; the dispatch note and the customer's value schedule; the seal photographs and the register entries; the temperature download and, where relevant, the controller event log; photographs of the goods; the delivery receipt with any remarks written on it; the first information report where there was one; the letter to the carrier where a hired vehicle was involved; QA's disposition; the survey report or desk assessment; and the salvage account.

10.2. The complete file is sent to the insurer, through the broker, within 45 days of the date of discovery. If something is still missing at day 40, the desk sends what it has with a list of what is to follow and a date for each item.

10.3. One extension of 30 days may be asked for in writing, through the broker, before the 45 days run out. A second extension is not asked for; if the file cannot be finished in 75 days, the desk lead decides whether to abandon the claim and records why.

10.4. Copies are sent, originals are kept. Seals, damaged packaging and logger files are offered to the surveyor for inspection but are not posted to anyone.

10.5. Every document carries the TPC26 file number in its top corner, written in ink on paper copies and in the file name for scans.

## 11. What is left of the load

11.1. Damaged goods remain the customer's property until a claim is paid; after payment, anything left belongs to the insurer. Neither the site nor the claims desk may treat what is left as Quillfern's to sell.

11.2. Medicines are never disposed of as salvage, whatever condition they appear to be in and whatever the insurer suggests. They are destroyed under QA control with a destruction certificate, and the certificate goes into the claim file in place of a salvage account.

11.3. Food that cannot be supplied to the customer may be sold for animal feed or processing, but only to a buyer the insurer approves in writing, and only after the surveyor has released it. The proceeds are paid to the insurer or deducted from the claim, as the broker directs.

11.4. Staff may not buy damaged goods, take them home or give them away, even when the goods are about to be destroyed anyway. A carton of ice cream taken off a damaged pallet is theft from the customer, whatever its future.

11.5. The salvage account lists what was sold, to whom, for how much and on what date, or what was destroyed and on whose certificate.

## 12. When someone else was driving

12.1. When goods are lost or damaged in a hired vehicle, Quillfern and its insurer have a right to recover the loss from the vehicle's owner. That right is easily lost through delay.

12.2. The site manager, with the claims desk, sends the hired-truck vendor a letter holding it responsible for the loss within 72 hours of the date of discovery. The letter goes by registered post and by e-mail to the address on the purchase order, names the dispatch note and the loss, and says that Quillfern and its insurers reserve all rights. A phone call, a message to the driver or a remark on the delivery note does not count.

12.3. Without that letter, the insurer may reduce what it pays by whatever it says it could have recovered, and it usually says the whole amount.

12.4. Once the insurer has paid, it takes over the claim against the vendor in Quillfern's name. The claims desk gives the insurer's lawyers whatever records they ask for, and the site manager makes witnesses available.

12.5. A vendor who pays Quillfern directly before the insurer has settled is told that the money will be accounted for in the claim. It is never kept quietly as a credit against the next invoice.

## 13. Gaps in the cover

13.1. The policy does not pay for every loss. The main things it will not pay for are: loss caused by delay alone; ordinary leakage, shrinkage and wear; loss caused by the nature of the goods themselves; packing that was not fit for the journey when the consignor packed it; war and nuclear risks; and any loss caused deliberately by Quillfern staff.

13.2. Delay deserves its own sentence because it is so often argued. A load that arrives late, even through no fault of ours and even when the lateness spoiled it, is not a claim under TPC. A load that arrives late and is rejected by the customer for being late is not a claim either. If the delay was caused by an insured event — a crash, a fire, a four-hour stoppage of the machinery — the claim is for that event, not for the delay.

13.3. Strikes, riots and civil commotion, known in the policy wording as SRCC, are covered from 2026 by an endorsement. A load damaged when a road blockade turned violent is therefore covered; a load spoiled because a blockade kept it standing for a day is not, for the reason in 13.2.

13.4. Theft by the driver of a hired vehicle is covered only where the vendor's own driver details were checked at the gate as the hiring rules require. Theft by a driver whose identity was never checked is a gap.

13.5. Where the desk is unsure whether a loss falls into a gap, it intimates the claim anyway and lets the insurer decide.

## 14. Money in

14.1. The insurer pays an agreed claim within 30 days of receiving the surveyor's final report or, for a desk-assessed claim, within 30 days of receiving the complete file. On day 31 the desk asks the broker to chase, and on day 45 the desk lead writes to the broker's branch head.

14.2. Payment comes to Quillfern's account, never to the customer's, even when the customer's goods were lost.

14.3. Finance books the expected recovery as a receivable when the claim is agreed, not when it is intimated, and writes off any part the insurer refuses once the desk lead has decided not to dispute it.

14.4. Credit notes to customers are not tied to insurance money. A customer is paid what the contract says, when the contract says, whether or not our own claim has succeeded.

14.5. Disputes with the insurer about the amount go through the broker first. The policy's arbitration clause is used only with the Finance head's approval.

## 15. The customer's own claim

15.1. A customer's claim against Quillfern runs on its own track, under its own contract, and is handled by the account manager and Finance. The claims desk is told of it and links the two files, but the customer's claim is not decided by what our insurer does.

15.2. Customers' contracts set their own limits, claim periods and evidence rules. Those terms, not the policy terms, decide what the customer gets.

15.3. Customers are not told whether Quillfern is insured for a loss, how much the cover is, or what the insurer paid. A customer who asks is told that Quillfern meets its contractual obligations.

15.4. Nobody promises a customer insurance money, a quick settlement or a particular figure. The first written word to a customer about money comes from the account manager after Finance has agreed it.

15.5. Evidence gathered for our own claim may be shown to a customer's surveyor on request, through the account manager, but the claim file itself is not sent to customers.

## 16. Keeping the file

16.1. Each claim file is kept, complete, for eight years from the date of final settlement or rejection, whichever comes later. Files involving a court case or arbitration are kept until three years after the case ends, if that is longer.

16.2. Scanned files live in the Finance document store under the TPC26 number. Paper originals are boxed by policy year and kept in the Finance archive room.

16.3. Physical evidence — seals, logger files, damaged packaging — is kept until the claim is closed, then for one more year, and is then disposed of with the desk lead's signature.

16.4. A file is never weeded to save space. If storage runs short, the desk lead asks Finance for more.

## 17. What changed from the 2025 cover

17.1. The 2025–26 cover was placed with the same insurer on different terms, and its summary note is withdrawn. Staff who learned the old figures should unlearn them.

17.2. The deductible rose, the intimation window shortened, the machinery-stoppage threshold came down, the single-vehicle figure went up, and the survey threshold went up. The current figures are in sections 5 to 9 of this procedure and nowhere else.

17.3. SRCC cover was added by endorsement for the first time.

17.4. The desk lead reviews this procedure each February, before renewal, and issues a revision whenever the policy terms change.
