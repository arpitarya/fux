# Wakad grocery account: agreeing returned quantities with the chain

For the customer service desk at Wakad that handles the grocery chain's returns of chilled dairy and bakery goods.

## What the chain sends

When a store sends goods back, the chain's accounts team raises a debit note against us for the value of the goods returned. Our count at the Wakad dock is the count we hold to.

## Comparing the two

The desk compares each debit note with our dock count within 3 working days of the note arriving. Where the two differ by more than 1 per cent in quantity or value, the desk supervisor takes it up with the chain's accounts team before any credit is raised. A smaller difference is settled by the desk from the dock records.

The reconciliation sheet for each note is filed with the note and the dock count, so that anyone can follow it later.

## Common causes

Most differences come from stores that count units while the dock counts trays, and from trays sent back half-empty. A discrepancy caused by our own driver's count at the store is reported to the transport planner, who takes it up with the driver.

## Credits

Credit notes are raised by the regional accountant on the desk supervisor's request, never by the desk itself, and never before the figures are agreed.

## Records

Debit notes, dock counts and sheets are kept for three years.
