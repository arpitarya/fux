# Stores and Consumables Standard

How Stores receives, holds and issues the consumables that cold-chain work runs
on: seals, red sleeve packets, spare loggers, gel packs, dry ice, protective
equipment and forms. It covers the main store at Nagpur and the smaller stores
cages at Guwahati and Coimbatore. It does not say how any of these items is used
on the job; the documents that govern their use do that.

## 1. What Stores does

1.1. Stores buys nothing on its own authority. It raises a request against a
purchase order that Finance has approved, receives the goods, holds them, issues
them against a signature and counts them.

1.2. The Nagpur main store keeps the stock for Nagpur and sends stock to
Guwahati and Coimbatore by courier or on a scheduled vehicle. The Guwahati and
Coimbatore cages hold only what their DC uses in about a month.

1.3. Every item issued is signed for by the person who takes it. An item that
leaves Stores without a signature is counted as lost at the next count.

1.4. Stores does not lend. An item taken for a job is either used, returned or
written off, and each of those is recorded.

## 2. The Stores team and hours

2.1. The Nagpur store is run by Nalini Patil with two storekeepers on the day
shift. It is open from 07:00 to 19:00, Monday to Saturday.

2.2. At night, the night shift lead holds the key to the emergency cupboard,
which carries red sleeve packets, seal books and protective equipment only.
Anything taken from it at night is written on the cupboard sheet and signed for
properly the next morning.

2.3. The Guwahati cage is looked after by the shift lead named by Riniki Bora.
The Coimbatore cage is looked after by the morning shift lead.

2.4. Stores is not a place to wait, eat or charge a phone. Drivers collecting
seal books or sleeves wait at the counter.

## 3. Receiving goods

3.1. Goods are received against the purchase order and the delivery note, and
counted before the delivery note is signed. A short delivery is written as short
on the delivery note in front of the supplier's driver.

3.2. Temperature-sensitive consumables, such as conditioned gel packs, are not
received by Stores. They go directly to the QA room.

3.3. Calibrated instruments are received by Stores and passed unopened to the QA
bench on the same day. Stores does not open an instrument box to check it.

3.4. Damaged goods are photographed, set aside and reported to Finance before the
supplier's driver leaves, where that is possible.

## 4. Seal books

4.1. Seals come from the seal manufacturer in books, already numbered in order.
Stores records the first and last number of each book received.

4.2. Stores issues seal books to a DC gate one book at a time against the gate's
signature. Besides the book open at the gate, a gate may hold at most two
unopened books in its locked drawer; a third is not issued until one of the two
is opened.

4.3. Spoiled seals marked VOID come back to Stores with their stubs. Stores
counts the stubs against QCL-F-35 every Friday, and a number that is neither
fitted, spoiled nor on the shelf is reported to Fleet Control the same day.

4.4. Each DC holds two bolt-seal cutters, and a lost cutter is replaced by Stores
within one working day.

4.5. Tamper labels for pharma pallets and shipper lids are issued in rolls of
200 to QA, not to the gate.

4.6. Seal books for Guwahati and Coimbatore go by courier, two books at a time,
and are signed for at the gate cabin on arrival.

## 5. Red sleeve packets

5.1. Stores holds red sleeve packets empty. QA makes each packet up, including a
spare logger checked at the bench, before it is issued.

5.2. A made-up packet comes back to Stores from QA sealed with a tamper label.
Stores does not open it.

5.3. Stores issues made-up packets to DCs and to reefer cab lockers against a
signature. A packet returned unused and unopened goes back on the shelf; a
packet returned opened goes to QA to be made up again.

5.4. Stores keeps at least ten made-up packets at Nagpur at all times. When the
stock falls to ten, Stores tells QA in writing.

5.5. Packets ordered under the red sleeve project are recorded against that
project's budget line, not against the Stores consumables line.

## 6. Spare instruments

6.1. Stores holds spare hand-held probes and spare USB loggers for issue, but
does not decide whether an instrument is fit to use. The sticker, put on by QA,
decides that.

6.2. A spare logger's battery is replaced by Stores after 24 months on the
shelf, whatever the logger's sticker says, and the logger then goes back to the
QA bench before it can be issued.

6.3. An instrument with a yellow sticker may be issued. An instrument with a red
sticker or red tape is never issued and goes back to QA.

6.4. Stores keeps a list of every instrument by its prefix number and the date
of its last in-house check, so that QA can see what is due.

6.5. Instruments sent to the external lab are packed by QA and handed to the
courier by Stores. Stores records the date they left and the date they came
back.

## 7. Gel packs and shipper consumables

7.1. Stores buys gel packs and holds them unconditioned. Conditioning happens in
the QA room, not in Stores.

7.2. Stores keeps at least 44 unconditioned blue-cap packs at Nagpur, enough to
remake two shippers completely.

7.3. Packs retired by QA, for cycle count or for damage, are sent to the
supplier for disposal and recorded as written off. Stores does not throw them in
general waste, because the gel is not general waste.

7.4. White-cap packs are held on a separate shelf, labelled FROZEN GOODS ONLY.

7.5. Spare shipper lids, straps and liners are held by Stores and issued to QA
against a signature. A damaged shipper body is not repaired in Stores.

## 8. Dry ice supply

8.1. Stores places the standing order for dry ice at Nagpur. Vidarbha Gases
delivers 120 kg on Monday and Thursday mornings, and extra deliveries are
ordered by Stores on the shift lead's request.

8.2. Stores does not hold dry ice in the store. The delivery goes straight to
the dry-ice chest, where the shift lead signs the delivery note.

8.3. At the weekly count Stores records the kg delivered, the kg signed out on
QCL-F-26 and the kg left in the chest, and the difference is recorded as
sublimation loss.

8.4. Guwahati and Coimbatore order their own dry ice from their local suppliers,
and send Stores the delivery notes monthly.

## 9. Protective equipment

9.1. Stores issues cryogenic gloves, face shields, freezer suits, safety shoes,
high-visibility vests and hearing protection against a signature.

9.2. Freezer suits are issued to named people, not to a shift. A suit that no
longer closes properly is returned and replaced.

9.3. Cryogenic gloves and face shields for dry-ice work are not personal issue.
They hang on the rack beside each chest and are replaced by Stores when damaged.

9.4. Safety shoes are replaced once a year, or sooner if the sole is split.

9.5. Hired-truck drivers are issued a high-visibility vest at the gate and return
it at the gate.

## 10. Counting

10.1. Stores counts seals, sleeves and instruments every Friday, and everything
else at the month end.

10.2. A count is done by two people, one of whom did not issue anything that
week.

10.3. At the Friday count the logger shelf is checked by date: a logger whose
last bench check is more than 90 days old is moved to the QA tray, and a logger
whose battery date shows more than 24 months is flagged, but nothing in the
store count replaces what the QA bench decides.

10.4. Differences are written up the same day and sent to Nalini Patil. A
difference in seals or loggers is reported to QA and Fleet Control as well.

10.5. Guwahati and Coimbatore cages send their counts to the Nagpur store by
email on the first working day of the month.

## 11. Write-off and disposal

11.1. Stores writes off an item only with Finance's approval, recorded on the
write-off sheet.

11.2. Broken instruments are written off after QA has confirmed they cannot be
calibrated. Their serials are struck off the instrument list.

11.3. Damaged seal books are never thrown away. Every seal in them is written
VOID and kept with the stubs until the next audit.

11.4. Protective equipment that is worn out is cut before disposal, so that it
cannot be picked out of the bin and worn again.

## 12. Suppliers

12.1. Stores keeps the approved supplier list for consumables. At the time of
this revision it includes Vidarbha Gases for dry ice at Nagpur, Brahmaputra
Industrial Gases at Guwahati and Kovai Industrial Gases at Coimbatore, Metrosure
Labs for external calibration, and the seal manufacturer named on the current
purchase order.

12.2. A supplier's replacement goods are received like any other delivery. A
supplier who replaces a batch of faulty seals sends new numbers; Stores never
accepts replacement seals carrying the same numbers as the faulty ones.

12.3. Suppliers are reviewed each year on short deliveries, damaged goods and
late replacement. The review is held with Finance and the DC managers.

12.4. A supplier whose driver has to wait more than an hour at the Nagpur store
is told why, and the wait is noted, because suppliers remember waits when they
quote.

## 13. Forms and printed stationery

13.1. Stores holds the printed pads of every QCL form used on the floor, from the
customer intimation form to the wash register. Each pad carries the form number
and the revision printed at its foot.

13.2. When QA issues a new revision of a form, Stores withdraws every pad of the
earlier revision from the DC shelves in the same week and sends the withdrawn
pads to QA to be counted and shredded. Two revisions of one form are never on a
shelf at the same time.

13.3. Forms are issued to shift leads by the pad, never by the loose sheet, so
that a used pad can be matched to the shift that used it.

13.4. Stores reorders a form when its stock falls to three months' use. The
print order goes through Finance like any other purchase.

13.5. Dispatch notes are printed with running numbers. Stores records the first
and last number of each box issued to a gate, as it does for other numbered
stock.

## 14. Layout and labelling of the store

14.1. The Nagpur store is laid out in four zones: numbered stock in the locked
cage, instruments on the shelf beside the QA hatch, protective equipment on the
open racks, and general consumables at the back.

14.2. Every shelf carries a printed label with the item name, the Stores item
code and the minimum stock. A handwritten label is replaced within a week.

14.3. Heavy items are stored below waist height. Nothing is stored on top of the
cage or on the floor of the walkway.

14.4. The store floor is swept daily and the racks are dusted monthly. Wet items
are never brought into the store; they are dried outside first.

14.5. The Guwahati and Coimbatore cages follow the same labels and item codes as
Nagpur, so that a count sheet from any site reads the same way.

## 15. Purchase requests and approval limits

15.1. A shift lead or QA officer who needs an item Stores does not hold raises a
purchase request on QCL-F-44 with the item, the quantity, the reason and the date
it is needed.

15.2. Stores checks whether the item is already on an approved purchase order.
If it is, Stores orders against that order. If it is not, the request goes to
Finance.

15.3. Requests up to Rs. 25,000 are approved by the DC manager. Requests above
that and up to Rs. 2 lakh are approved by Anjali Deshmukh for Finance. Anything
larger needs the Managing Director's approval.

15.4. An urgent request is marked urgent and phoned through to Finance as well
as written. Marking a routine request urgent to jump the queue is noted in the
monthly Stores report.

15.5. Stores does not accept gifts, samples or free stock from suppliers. A
supplier who sends samples is asked to take them back.

## 16. Flammable and hazardous items

16.1. Food-grade alcohol for wiping frozen bodies, chlorine for washdowns and
diesel additives are held in the flammables cabinet outside the store, never on
the store racks.

16.2. Each hazardous item has its safety data sheet in the folder on the cabinet
door. A new item is not received until its sheet is in the folder.

16.3. Chlorine is issued by the litre against the wash register, and never mixed
in the store.

16.4. Spills in the flammables cabinet are cleaned with the spill kit hung beside
it and reported to the DC manager the same day.

## 17. Handover between storekeepers

17.1. A storekeeper going on leave for more than two days hands over to the
other storekeeper with a walk round the cage and a signed note of open requests,
expected deliveries and any count differences not yet closed.

17.2. The keys to the cage are signed over in the key book at the handover, with
the time.

17.3. Nalini Patil's own leave is covered by the Nagpur DC manager's nominee, who
signs the key book and the handover note like anyone else.

## 18. Audit of Stores

18.1. Finance audits the Nagpur store twice a year without notice, counting a
sample of lines chosen on the day.

18.2. QA audits the numbered stock once a year, from the manufacturer's delivery
note to the gate register and back to the stubs.

18.3. Findings are written up by the auditor and answered by Nalini Patil within
ten working days, with an owner and a date for each action.

18.4. A finding that repeats from one audit to the next is escalated to the
Nagpur DC manager and to Finance.

## 19. Office consumables and general supplies

19.1. Stores also holds the everyday supplies the sites run on: printer paper,
toner, pens, markers, stretch wrap, packing tape, pallet labels, torch batteries
for the gate cabins and cleaning materials.

19.2. These are issued against a signature like everything else, but in larger
units: a ream, a box, a roll. Stores does not count pens.

19.3. Stretch wrap and packing tape are issued to the dock teams by the carton at
the start of each week. A dock that runs out before the week ends asks Stores,
and Stores notes which dock and why.

19.4. Pallet labels are printed by the dock teams from Dockwise. Stores holds the
blank label rolls and the spare printer ribbons, and keeps two spare label
printers in the cage for a swap.

19.5. Cleaning materials are issued to the housekeeping contractor against its
own signature. The contractor's staff do not take anything from the cage
themselves.

## 20. Transfers between sites

20.1. Stock sent from Nagpur to Guwahati or Coimbatore travels with a Stores
transfer note on QCL-F-45, listing every item and quantity. The receiving cage
counts the parcel on arrival and signs the note.

20.2. A short or damaged transfer is written on the note and photographed before
the parcel is signed for. The courier is told the same day.

20.3. Numbered stock and made-up red sleeve packets travel in sealed courier
bags. The bag number is written on the transfer note.

20.4. Items are never transferred between sites in a driver's cab, even when a
vehicle happens to be going that way, unless the item is on the dispatch note.

20.5. The signed transfer notes come back to Nagpur by scan the same day and by
post at the month end.

## 21. Returns to suppliers

21.1. Faulty goods are returned to the supplier with a return note on the
supplier's own form where the supplier has one, and on QCL-F-45 where it does
not.

21.2. Stores keeps a copy of every return note and follows up any return that
has not been credited or replaced within thirty days.

21.3. Returned numbered stock is listed by number on the return note, so that the
numbers can never reappear on a Quillfern shelf.

21.4. A supplier who has had three returns in a year is raised at the annual
supplier review, whatever the reason for the returns.

## 22. Energy and waste in the store

22.1. The store lights are on a timer and go off at 19:30. The storekeeper
closing the store checks that the cage, the flammables cabinet and the emergency
cupboard are locked before the lights go off.

22.2. Packaging from deliveries is flattened and sent to the scrap area on the
day it arrives. The store is not a place to keep empty boxes.

22.3. Used toner cartridges are returned to the toner supplier for recycling.

22.4. Stores reports its waste once a year to the Nagpur DC manager, by type and
weight, for the site's environmental return.

## 23. Contacts

23.1. The Nagpur store phone is on the Operations extension list. Out of hours,
the night shift lead holds the emergency cupboard key, as in section 2.

23.2. Questions about whether an item is fit to use go to QA. Questions about
whether an item may be bought go to Finance. Stores answers questions about
where an item is and how many are left.

## 24. Review of this standard

24.1. Nalini Patil reviews this standard every July with QA and Finance, and
sooner when a new consumable is introduced or a supplier changes.

24.2. A change to how an item is used is made in the document that governs its
use, not here. This standard changes only how Stores receives, holds, issues and
counts the item.

24.3. Each revision is issued with a note of what changed. The storekeepers at
Nagpur and the cage leads at Guwahati and Coimbatore sign that they have read it
within a week.

24.4. The earlier revision is withdrawn from the Stores counter and the shared
drive on the day the new one is issued, so that nobody works from two versions
at the same time.
