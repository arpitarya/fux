# Service Level Schedule, Contract VX-09

## 1. Parties and purpose

1.1. This schedule forms part of contract VX-09 between Quillfern Cold Logistics Pvt. Ltd. ("Quillfern") and Vetrix Animal Health Pvt. Ltd. ("Vetrix"), a maker of vaccines, medicines and breeding products for cattle, buffalo and poultry, with its plant at Pithampur and its sales strongest in Punjab, Haryana and western Uttar Pradesh.

1.2. The contract began on 1 April 2025 and was renewed on 1 April 2026 for three years. This revision of the schedule took effect with the renewal; the measures and remedies of the first year are shown where they changed.

1.3. The schedule says what service Quillfern commits to, how it is measured, what happens when it is missed, and how the two companies govern the account. Prices are in the separate schedule of rates and are not repeated here.

1.4. Where this schedule and the main body of the contract differ, the main body applies. Where this schedule and a Quillfern procedure differ on how Quillfern does its own work, the procedure applies, provided the outcome promised here is met.

1.5. Vetrix's account manager at Quillfern is Devika Menon. Vetrix's contract owner is its head of supply chain.

## 2. What Quillfern handles

2.1. Quillfern stores Vetrix's chilled vaccines and biologicals in the chilled block at Ludhiana DC, its ambient products such as deworming tablets and mineral mixtures in the ambient store there, and its frozen breeding products as described in section 8.

2.2. Quillfern delivers to Vetrix's distributors, to state animal husbandry department stores and to large dairy cooperatives across Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and the western districts of Uttar Pradesh.

2.3. Returns from distributors and cooperatives are collected by Quillfern and taken to the Bhosari hub, where they are handled under Quillfern's returns manual and Vetrix's instructions.

2.4. Quillfern does not sell, promote or advise on Vetrix's products. A farmer or a veterinarian who calls a Quillfern site about a product is given Vetrix's customer line.

## 3. Ordering

3.1. Vetrix's distributors and stores place orders through Vetrix's order portal, which passes them to Quillfern's warehouse system every thirty minutes between 06:00 and 22:00.

3.2. Orders received by 12:00 are dispatched the same day where the delivery point is within the day-route network, and the next working day otherwise. The routes are listed in the annex and reviewed every quarter.

3.3. An order may be changed or cancelled by Vetrix until it has been picked. After picking, a change is treated as a new order and the picked stock is returned to its location.

3.4. Vetrix's customer service team tells Quillfern by e-mail of any order that needs special handling, such as a delivery to a government store that receives only on certain days.

## 4. When the herds are sick

4.1. Diseases such as foot-and-mouth and lumpy skin can spread through a district in days, and when a state government notifies one, vaccine has to reach the affected villages faster than any normal order cycle allows.

4.2. Vetrix may mark orders for the affected districts as urgent. Quillfern dispatches each such order within 6 hours of receiving it, at any hour of any day, using a dedicated vehicle if no scheduled route will meet that time. Ordinary orders keep their normal timings.

4.3. In the first contract year the commitment during an outbreak was 12 hours; it was shortened at renewal after the lumpy skin season of 2025.

4.4. Surge capacity is limited by the chilled vehicles and drivers available in the region. Vetrix gives as much warning as it can, and the two companies agree a daily ceiling of urgent orders if the volume would otherwise exceed what can be delivered safely.

4.5. Urgent orders never override driving-hours or temperature rules, however large the surge. An order that cannot be met safely within the time is reported to Vetrix at once with the earliest safe time, and notified outbreak or not, no driver is asked to hurry.

## 5. The main promise

5.1. Quillfern's main promise is that orders arrive at the right place, inside the agreed delivery window, with the right items in the right quantities. Each order meets all of those or it does not; there is no partial credit.

5.2. The share of orders that meet them in a calendar month must be at least 97 per cent. For each full percentage point by which a month falls below that, Vetrix receives a service credit of 2 per cent of that month's handling invoice, up to a maximum of 10 per cent.

5.3. In the first contract year the target was 95 per cent, with the same credit rate.

5.4. An order is excluded from the otif count only where the failure was caused by Vetrix, by the receiving party, or by an event outside Quillfern's control listed in the contract, reported within one working day.

5.5. A shortfall in two consecutive months needs a written recovery plan from Quillfern, whatever the credit paid; a shortfall in three brings the otif figure to the half-yearly meeting of senior managers.

5.6. Credits are deducted from the next invoice, not paid in cash.

## 6. How the numbers are counted

6.1. All measures are calculated by Quillfern from its warehouse and transport systems and checked by Vetrix's supply chain team against its own order records. Where the two disagree, the order-level records are compared line by line until the difference is explained.

6.2. Delivery time is the time recorded on the driver's handheld at the moment the receiving party signs, not the time the vehicle reached the gate.

6.3. Quantity and item accuracy are checked against the receiving party's signed delivery note. A delivery note signed without remarks is taken as accepting the quantity delivered.

6.4. Orders cancelled by Vetrix before dispatch are not counted. Orders split by Quillfern into two deliveries count as failed unless Vetrix agreed the split before dispatch.

6.5. Measures are calculated by calendar month and reported to two decimal places. Rounding is applied once, at the end.

## 7. Every month on paper

7.1. Quillfern sends Vetrix a monthly report of every measure in this schedule, with the underlying order list attached, so that Vetrix's team can check any figure for itself.

7.2. The report reaches Vetrix by the 7th working day of the following month. It includes a one-page scorecard of the month's measures against target, the service credits due, the excursions and their status, and the returns received at the Bhosari hub.

7.3. In the first contract year the report was due by the 10th working day, which left too little time to discuss it at the monthly review.

7.4. Each kpi in the report is defined in this schedule; Quillfern does not change a definition without Vetrix's written agreement.

7.5. The report is prepared by the account manager and checked by Finance before it is sent. The account manager signs it.

7.6. A kpi missed in the month carries a short note of the cause and the action taken, written in plain language, and the same note is copied to the scorecard archive.

## 8. Frozen breeding stock

8.1. Vetrix sells frozen bull semen to dairy cooperatives and artificial insemination centres. The doses are stored and carried in LN2 at about -196 C and must never warm, even briefly.

8.2. Quillfern holds Vetrix's straws in eight storage dewar units in a separate locked room at Ludhiana DC, and carries them to customers in smaller transport units of the same kind.

8.3. Each storage dewar is topped up with LN2 every 10 days, and its level is measured with a dipstick every day. The level must never fall below 40 per cent of the unit's capacity; a reading below that is topped up the same day and reported to Vetrix.

8.4. LN2 is supplied by Vetrix's approved gas supplier under Vetrix's contract. Quillfern handles it under its own safety rules for cryogenic liquids, which require face shields, cryogenic gloves and a ventilated room.

8.5. Transport units are charged by Quillfern staff trained for the purpose, and their levels are recorded at dispatch and at delivery. Straws are never handled outside a unit.

8.6. In the first contract year the units were topped up every 14 days, and two readings fell below the minimum before a top-up arrived.

## 9. When something goes out of range

9.1. A temperature excursion affecting Vetrix's stock is notified to Vetrix's named quality contact within the time the company's customer notification matrix sets for patient-critical stock. Vetrix's vaccines are treated as patient critical for this purpose.

9.2. Quillfern investigates every excursion under its own procedures and shares the investigation report with Vetrix's quality contact on request.

9.3. The decision whether affected stock may be released, returned or destroyed is Vetrix's, taken on Quillfern's records. Quillfern does not destroy Vetrix's stock without Vetrix's written instruction.

9.4. Excursions are listed in the monthly report with their status until each is closed.

## 10. Who pays for what is lost

10.1. Quillfern is liable for loss of or damage to Vetrix's stock while in its care, subject to the limits in this section and the exclusions in the main body of the contract.

10.2. Quillfern's indemnity for any one consignment is limited to Rs. 5 lakh at Vetrix's invoice price to its distributors. Its total liability in any contract year is limited to an aggregate of Rs. 60 lakh.

10.3. In the first contract year the yearly limit was Rs. 40 lakh; the consignment limit has not changed.

10.4. Neither company is liable to the other for lost profit, lost sales or damage to reputation.

10.5. Claims are made in writing within 30 days of the event, with the delivery records and photographs. The indemnity does not apply to stock damaged before it reached Quillfern, and the aggregate does not include service credits under section 5.

## 11. Running the relationship

11.1. The account manager and Vetrix's supply chain manager hold an operations review on the second Tuesday of every month, by video call or in person at Ludhiana. The review goes through the monthly report, open excursions, returns, upcoming promotions and any problems either side has seen.

11.2. A steering group of senior people from both companies meets twice a year, in January and July. Quillfern sends its operations head and the account manager; Vetrix sends its head of supply chain and its head of quality. The group sets priorities for the next six months and settles anything the monthly review could not.

11.3. In the first contract year the steering group met once, in October, which both companies agreed at renewal was too seldom.

11.4. Minutes of every meeting are written by the account manager, agreed by both sides within a week, and kept with the contract file.

11.5. The governance meetings are the only place where the measures, the credits or this schedule are changed. An agreement made on a phone call between two managers is not a change until it has been through them, and the record of governance decisions is attached to this schedule.

## 12. Invoicing

12.1. Quillfern invoices Vetrix monthly in arrears, with separate invoices for storage, handling and transport, within five working days of the month's end.

12.2. Service credits appear as a separate line on the next handling invoice, with a reference to the month and the measure that earned them.

12.3. Vetrix pays each invoice within 45 days of its date. Disputed lines are raised within 15 days; the undisputed part is paid on time.

## 13. Changing this schedule

13.1. Either company may propose a change to this schedule in writing. A proposed change is discussed at the next monthly review and decided by the senior group or, between its meetings, by written agreement of the account manager and Vetrix's head of supply chain.

13.2. A change takes effect from the first day of the month after it is agreed, never backwards.

13.3. Each revision of this schedule carries a table of changes on its last page.

## 14. Ending the contract

14.1. Either company may end the contract by giving six months' written notice, or at once for a serious breach not put right within 30 days of a written demand.

14.2. On ending, Quillfern hands over Vetrix's stock and records to Vetrix or to a new provider named by Vetrix, in an orderly way agreed between the two companies, and continues to meet this schedule until the last stock has left.

14.3. Frozen breeding stock is handed over unit by unit, with the receiving party's trained staff present, and each unit's level is recorded on handover.

## 15. Contacts

15.1. Day-to-day contacts on both sides, with their phone numbers and e-mail addresses, are listed in the contact annex, which the account manager updates whenever someone changes role.

15.2. Escalation runs from the account manager to Quillfern's customer service lead and then to the operations head. On Vetrix's side it runs from the supply chain manager to the head of supply chain.

15.3. Excursion notices go to Vetrix's named quality contact, with a copy to the supply chain manager, at any hour.

## 16. People who handle Vetrix stock

16.1. Everyone at Ludhiana DC who picks, packs or loads Vetrix stock completes a half-day briefing on the account before their first shift on it: the products, the storage areas, the special handling of government store deliveries and the handling of the frozen breeding stock.

16.2. Only staff trained in cryogenic handling enter the room where the frozen breeding stock is kept. The training is given by Vetrix's gas supplier and refreshed every year, and the list of trained staff is posted on the room's door.

16.3. Drivers on Vetrix routes are briefed on the delivery points, especially government stores and cooperatives that receive only at fixed hours, and on the rule that they do not discuss products with farmers or veterinarians at the delivery point.

16.4. New staff are not put on Vetrix work in their first two weeks on site, and never alone on a delivery to a government store.

## 17. The year's calendar

17.1. Vetrix's busiest months follow the state vaccination campaigns, which usually run in spring and in autumn, and the monsoon, when disease spreads fastest. Vetrix shares its campaign calendar with Quillfern each January and updates it whenever a state changes its dates.

17.2. Quillfern plans chilled vehicle capacity in the region against that calendar, and books extra hired chilled vehicles for the campaign weeks in advance rather than looking for them when the orders arrive.

17.3. In the winter months, fog across Punjab and Haryana slows deliveries. Delivery windows for December and January are agreed with Vetrix each November, and measured against the agreed winter windows rather than the summer ones.

17.4. Promotions that Vetrix runs with its distributors are told to Quillfern at least three weeks ahead, with the expected volumes, so that stock can be moved into the pick faces in time.

## 18. Vetrix's own checks

18.1. Vetrix may audit the handling of its stock at Ludhiana DC and at the Bhosari hub on ten working days' notice, and at shorter notice where it has a specific cause. The account manager arranges the visit and the site's QA lead hosts it.

18.2. Vetrix's auditors see the storage areas, the records of its own stock, the temperature records of the rooms holding it and the training records of staff who handle it. They do not see other customers' stock or records.

18.3. Findings are answered by Quillfern within 20 working days with a corrective action, an owner and a date, and tracked at the monthly review until closed.

18.4. Vetrix may also send a representative to observe a delivery to a government store or a cooperative, by arrangement with the account manager. The representative travels in Vetrix's own vehicle, not in Quillfern's.

## 19. Data and confidentiality

19.1. Order, stock and delivery data about Vetrix's business belongs to Vetrix. Quillfern uses it only to provide the services in this contract and to prepare the reports in this schedule.

19.2. Data is shared with Vetrix through the order portal and by e-mail to the named contacts. It is not shared with any other customer, including other animal health companies whose stock Quillfern may hold.

19.3. Quillfern keeps Vetrix's records for the periods its own procedures require, and in any case for three years after the contract ends, and returns or destroys them afterwards at Vetrix's choice.

## 20. If Ludhiana cannot work

20.1. Quillfern keeps a continuity plan for the account, reviewed with Vetrix each year at the July meeting of senior managers. It names the site that would hold Vetrix's chilled stock if Ludhiana DC could not, the vehicles that would carry it there, and the order in which deliveries would be restarted.

20.2. If Ludhiana DC loses power for longer than its generators can cover, or cannot be reached because of flooding or civil disturbance, the account manager tells Vetrix's supply chain manager within one hour of the decision to invoke the plan, and twice a day after that until normal working resumes.

20.3. Measures in this schedule are suspended for the days on which the plan is in force, provided Quillfern followed the plan. Days on which it did not follow the plan are measured as normal.

20.4. The plan is tested once a year by a desk exercise with Vetrix's team, and the lessons are written into the next version.

## 21. Sustainability reporting

21.1. Vetrix reports its own transport emissions to its shareholders. Quillfern supplies, once a year by the end of April, the distance travelled and the diesel used on Vetrix deliveries in the previous year, calculated from its transport system, with the method used.

21.2. Quillfern tells Vetrix in advance of any change to its vehicles or routes that would change those figures materially, such as the introduction of electric vans on short city routes.

21.3. Neither company publishes figures about the other's operations without the other's written agreement.
