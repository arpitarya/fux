# Pharma Store Quality Manual, West Zone

## 1. Purpose and reach

1.1. The store at Changodar, on the south-west edge of Ahmedabad, is the first Quillfern building that holds medicines and nothing else. It opened in September 2025 in a leased shed inside a pharmaceutical estate off the Sarkhej–Bavla highway. From its first week its customers — two vaccine makers, a biologics firm and three hospital groups in Gujarat — asked for a written quality system they could audit against. This manual is that system. It says how the store keeps product fit for the patient, how it proves that it did so, and who answers when it did not.

1.2. It is written for the people who work in the building: receiving clerks, pickers, dispatch staff, the shift QA officers, the engineering technician and the store manager. It is also written for auditors, who will read it more carefully than anyone on the floor and who should be able to find, somewhere in it, the reason for every record the store keeps.

1.3. Where this manual and a company-wide procedure disagree about a temperature limit, a notification window or a quarantine rule, the company procedure applies and this manual is corrected at its next revision. The manual sets no temperature limit of its own; it says how those limits are kept in this building, by these people.

1.4. Revision 3 is in force from 11/05/2026. The summaries written while the store was being fitted out are withdrawn and kept only as history. Earlier revisions of this manual are withdrawn from the shared drive on the day a new one is issued.

## 2. What the store promises

2.1. The store promises its customers four things: that a product is held inside its labelled conditions from the moment it crosses the dock until the moment it leaves; that every unit can be traced; that anything which goes wrong is written down honestly, even when it is embarrassing; and that nothing doubtful reaches a patient because a delivery was due.

2.2. Those promises are not slogans for the canteen wall. Each has a record behind it, and the sections below say which. A promise without a record is treated in an audit as a promise not kept.

2.3. The store manager, Bhavesh Rana, owns the operation. The site QA lead, Hemal Trivedi, owns this manual and the quality records. The RP, Dr. Nupur Shah, is the pharmacist named on the wholesale licence and the only person who may release stock for supply. The three roles are deliberately kept in different hands, so that the person who has to meet the dispatch plan is never the person who decides whether doubtful stock may go.

2.4. Customers are told these names on the day their account opens, and told again whenever one of them changes. A customer's quality agreement may add obligations to this manual but may not remove any.

## 3. Who answers for what

3.1. The RP answers to the licensing authority as well as to Quillfern. She may stop any dispatch at any hour without giving a reason first; the reason is written up within the shift.

3.2. Shift QA officers work under the RP. One is on duty on each of the two shifts, 06:00 to 14:00 and 14:00 to 22:00. Between 22:00 and 06:00 the store neither receives nor dispatches except under a written plan the RP has approved, and the night is covered by the security contractor and the RP's phone.

3.3. The engineering technician, Irfan Ghanchi, looks after the chambers, the generator, the alarm panel and the loggers. He keeps his own maintenance log. He does not decide whether stock affected by an equipment fault is usable; he reports the fault and the QA officer decides what happens to the stock.

3.4. The store manager decides staffing, slots and vehicles. He may ask QA to hurry; he may not ask QA to agree. Where any two of these people disagree about whether stock may move, it does not move until the RP has decided.

3.5. Each role has a named deputy, listed on the board outside the QA office. A deputy acts with the full authority of the role while the holder is away, and signs in the deputy's own name, never the holder's.

## 4. The building and its zones

4.1. The shed is a single span divided into five zones: the goods-in bay, two walk-in chambers held at +2 to +8 C, the CRT area, the dispatch bay and the quarantine cage. Offices and the gowning room are on a mezzanine above goods-in, reached by a stair that does not pass through any product zone.

4.2. WIC-1 holds vaccines and biologics. WIC-2 holds insulin and other chilled medicines. A product never moves between the two chambers without a QA officer's signature, because several customers' contracts name the chamber their stock lives in.

4.3. The CRT area holds tablets, syrups, diluents and devices labelled for storage between 15 and 25 C, and humidity there matters as much as heat: several of the tablet lines carry a moisture warning on the carton. The area is air-conditioned and has two dehumidifiers on their own controller. The hygrometer limit there is 65 per cent RH, raised from 60 per cent in revision 3 because the monsoon kept tripping the old figure without any product effect. A reading above the limit for 30 minutes is a deviation; a shorter one is noted in the shift log.

4.4. The quarantine cage is a locked mesh enclosure inside WIC-2, with its own logger. Only QA officers and the RP hold its key, and the key is signed for at every shift change.

4.5. The goods-in bay and the dispatch bay are not temperature-controlled rooms, whatever the thermostat on the wall says. Stock does not wait in either; it moves through.

4.6. Pest proofing, drainage and the roof belong to the landlord under the lease. Faults are reported to the store manager, who raises them with the landlord in writing, and the QA lead is copied on every such letter.

## 5. Equipment that has to prove itself

5.1. Every piece of equipment that touches product temperature is qualified before use: the two walk-in chambers, the CRT air-conditioning, the generator and its changeover, and the alarm panel. Qualification means a written test, run by people who did not install the equipment, against acceptance criteria written before the test began.

5.2. The walk-ins are requalified on a fixed cycle, and the cycle was shortened after the compressor failure of November 2025, when WIC-2 ran for nine days on a hired compressor whose behaviour nobody had measured. A full temperature study of each chamber is now repeated every 12 months, and also within 30 days after any compressor, evaporator or door is replaced. The study follows the company mapping method, and the result goes to the RP, who decides whether the chamber may keep holding stock.

5.3. The generator is load-tested on the first Monday of each month by pulling the incoming supply. The changeover must complete before any chamber logger records a rise of more than 1 C, and the technician writes the time it took in his log.

5.4. The alarm panel is tested every Friday. A panel that fails its test is taken out of service and the shift QA officer reads every logger by hand each hour until it is repaired.

5.5. Equipment that fails any test carries a yellow card until it passes again, and the RP is told the same shift. A yellow-carded chamber may keep stock only on the RP's written decision.

## 6. Watching the rooms

6.1. Each walk-in has three fixed loggers and the CRT area has two. They report every minute to the panel in the QA office and to the duty phone carried by the shift QA officer. The panel keeps its own record and pushes a daily file to the document system at 23:59.

6.2. An alarm is acknowledged on the panel by the QA officer on duty, never by a picker, and acknowledging an alarm is not the same as closing it. The alarm stays open until the officer has written down what was found and what was done about it.

6.3. Logger batteries are changed by the technician on a calendar he keeps, not when they run flat. A logger that has gone silent is a deviation, even if the room was in range throughout.

6.4. Door openings are counted by the panel. A chamber whose doors stood open for more than 40 minutes in a shift is raised at the next morning's meeting — not because anything necessarily went wrong, but because it usually means the picking plan was poor.

6.5. The weekly trend sheet, prepared by the QA lead every Monday, shows each logger's highest and lowest reading of the week and every alarm. The RP reads and initials it, and her initials are what an auditor will look for.

## 7. Goods arriving

7.1. A vehicle arriving at the store is checked before it is opened: the seal number against the paperwork, the condition of the doors and the reefer's display. The driver stays with the vehicle until goods-in has finished with it, and is offered the drivers' room, not the canteen.

7.2. Every inbound vehicle's temperature printout or download is read at the goods-in desk before a single pallet crosses the threshold. Drivers call any missing stretch a "hole" in the printout, and holes are common on loads that come round the Ahmedabad ring road, where some older reefers lose their logger signal. A break of up to ten minutes in the record may be accepted by the goods-in clerk; anything longer goes to the Responsible Person, who decides. A record that is in range throughout but shows the reefer door opening during the journey is also referred to her.

7.3. Pallets are checked against the advance notice by batch and quantity. A batch that does not match the notice is received into quarantine rather than refused at the door, so that the customer's stock is protected while the paperwork is put right.

7.4. Goods-in photographs every pallet label before the stretch wrap is cut. The photographs are filed against the receipt, not kept on the camera.

7.5. Nothing is received between 22:00 and 06:00. A vehicle that arrives at night waits outside the estate gate on its own power, and its record for the waiting hours is read together with the journey.

## 8. Putting away and picking

8.1. Putaway is directed by the warehouse system and confirmed by a scan at the rack location. A pallet put away without a scan is treated as lost until it is found and scanned.

8.2. Picking follows first-expiry-first-out. A picker who finds that the system has directed a later-expiry batch ahead of an earlier one stops and calls the shift QA officer. The system is not overruled on the floor, and it is not trusted blindly either.

8.3. Pickers do not open outer cartons inside the chambers. Part-carton picks are made at the picking table in WIC-2's anteroom, under the anteroom's own logger.

8.4. A dropped carton is set aside on the damage shelf, photographed and written up, whatever it looks like from outside. Vials break inside cartons that look perfect, and pre-filled syringes crack without a sound.

8.5. Dispatch staging is limited to what fits on the two dispatch-bay trolleys. The vehicle waits for the stock, never the other way round, and a vehicle that is not at loading temperature when it reverses onto the bay is sent away to cool down.

8.6. Two people check every outbound pallet against the picking list before it is wrapped: the picker and a second person who did not pick it. Both initial the list.

## 9. When things go off plan

9.1. Anything that goes wrong against this manual or a procedure is a deviation, whether or not product was harmed: a missed check, a wrong batch picked, a door left open, a load of medicines wrecked on its way into or out of Changodar. Deviations are graded critical, major or minor by the shift QA officer, and only the RP may lower a grade.

9.2. A critical deviation reaches the Responsible Person by phone within two hours of discovery; a major one within one working day; a minor one waits for the weekly review. Critical means that patient safety, product identity or the integrity of a record may be affected. A consignment damaged in transit is always critical until the RP says otherwise.

9.3. Every deviation above minor gets a corrective action, which the store records as a CAPA, with an owner and a date. The owner is a named person, never a team or a department.

9.4. Signing a corrective action off is not the end of it, because the commonest failure at a new site is a fix that works for a fortnight and then quietly stops working. QA looks again sixty days later, and reopens the record if the fault has returned. A reopened record is reported to the customer whose stock was involved, in the next monthly quality report.

9.5. Deviations are numbered in a single series for the site, and a number is never reused, even when a deviation was raised in error and cancelled the same hour. Cancelled numbers stay in the register with the reason.

## 10. Looking at ourselves

10.1. Self-inspection is how the store finds its own faults before an auditor does. It is not an audit by head office; it is the store checking itself, with someone from outside the shift holding the clipboard.

10.2. From 1 January 2026 a full walk of the store against this manual takes place once every quarter, led by someone from outside the store's own shift. The findings are graded in the same way as deviations, and each finding above minor gets a corrective action and an owner.

10.3. The walk covers every section of this manual, not only the sections that went wrong last time. The inspector reads a sample of records in each area — goods-in sheets, alarm logs, training files, the returns register — and talks to at least one person on each shift without a supervisor in the room.

10.4. The report goes to the RP and the store manager within five working days and is filed with the customer audit papers. A customer auditor may read any self-inspection report from the past three years.

10.5. An inspector never inspects an area she works in. On a site this small that sometimes means borrowing a QA officer from another Quillfern site for a day, and the cost of the borrowing is the store's, not the lender's.

## 11. After the stock has left

11.1. Responsibility for a batch does not end at the dispatch bay. The store must be able to find every unit it has handled, and it must deal properly with what comes back.

11.2. Twice a year, in March and September, the store runs a mock recall on a batch chosen by the RP without warning. The exercise starts when she names the batch to the shift QA officer. Every unit of the chosen batch must be found and named — on a shelf, on a vehicle or at a consignee — inside four hours of the exercise starting. The result, with the time taken and any unit that could not be found, goes to the customer who owns the batch.

11.3. Hospitals and distributors send product back for many reasons — over-ordering, a ward closing, a wrong delivery — and none of those reasons makes it fit to be resold. Nothing returned goes back into saleable stock unless it comes back within 72 hours of delivery, with an unbroken logger record from our hands to the consignee's and back, and the Responsible Person signs the release; everything else is destroyed. Returned product waits in the quarantine cage while the decision is made.

11.4. A real recall, as opposed to a practice one, is run by the customer. The store's part is to stop dispatch of the batch at once, list every unit it holds and every unit it has sent, and hold any returned units in quarantine until the customer instructs.

11.5. Complaints from hospitals about a product's quality are passed to the customer the same day. The store does not reply to the complaint itself, and does not offer an opinion on it.

## 12. People, gowns and hands

12.1. Every person who enters a product zone has completed the store's induction, which covers this manual, gowning, hand hygiene and what to do when an alarm sounds. Visitors are escorted and do not touch product.

12.2. Gowning is done in the gowning room: a clean overcoat, a hair cover, and for the chambers a freezer jacket from the rack. Outdoor shoes stay on the outer side of the bench.

12.3. Nobody eats, drinks, chews or smokes in a product zone. Medicines of one's own are kept in the locker room, never in a pocket.

12.4. A person with a stomach upset, an open wound on the hands or a skin infection tells the shift QA officer before starting work and is given work away from open product.

12.5. Training is refreshed every year and whenever a procedure the person uses changes. A person whose training has lapsed does not work in a product zone until it is refreshed, however short-staffed the shift.

12.6. Training records show the date, the trainer, the procedure revision and a short test result. A signature alone is not a training record.

## 13. Documents

13.1. The store's procedures, forms and records live in the document system. Only the version in the system is current, and each procedure shows its revision number and effective date on every page.

13.2. Paper copies of procedures are a convenience, not a source: they are useful at the goods-in desk and in the anteroom, where nobody wants to read a screen with gloves on. A copy printed from the document system is valid only until midnight of the day printed, which the printer stamps in the footer. A paper copy found after that is put in the confidential bin, whatever revision number it carries.

13.3. Records are made at the time, in ink or in the system, and corrected by striking through and initialling, never by overwriting or by correction fluid.

13.4. Records for each batch — goods-in sheets, dispatch notes, logger files, deviation files and the returns register — are kept long enough to answer a regulator after the product has expired. Each batch file is kept for one year after the expiry date of the batch, or five years from the batch's last movement, whichever ends later. Records about the building and its equipment are kept for the life of the lease and three years after it.

13.5. Records are not taken home, not photographed on personal phones and not sent to a customer except through the customer's named quality contact.

## 14. Outside help

14.1. Several outside firms work in the building: the security contractor, the pest contractor, the cleaning contractor, the generator's service agent and the calibration laboratory. Each is approved by QA before its first visit and reviewed once a year.

14.2. The pest contractor visits on the first Thursday of each month and on call after any sighting. Bait stations are numbered on the wall plan in the QA office, and nobody moves one to make room for stock.

14.3. The cleaning contractor cleans the offices, the gowning room and the floors of the goods-in and dispatch bays. Chamber floors are cleaned by store staff only, so that nobody unfamiliar with the racking works in a chamber.

14.4. Refrigeration work on the chambers is done only by a firm approved under the company's arrangements for cooling-plant service partners. A technician who arrives without his induction card is not let in, however urgent the fault.

14.5. Calibration certificates from the laboratory are filed against the instrument's number in the document system. An instrument whose certificate is missing is treated as out of calibration.

## 15. Customer audits

15.1. Customers may audit the store on ten working days' notice, and for cause at shorter notice. The RP or the QA lead hosts every audit; the store manager joins for the operational parts.

15.2. An auditor may see any record about the customer's own stock and any part of the building. An auditor may not see another customer's stock records or another customer's audit reports.

15.3. The store answers each audit finding within 20 working days with a corrective action, an owner and a date. Answers are written by the QA lead and signed by the RP.

15.4. Findings from customer audits are added to the store's own corrective action register, so that one customer's finding fixes the problem for every customer.

15.5. A customer may ask to observe a mock recall or a self-inspection walk. The request is granted where the customer's own batch is chosen or where the walk does not expose another customer's records.

## 16. Waste and destruction

16.1. Product that cannot be supplied is destroyed by a licensed medical-waste contractor, against a destruction certificate that names the batch and the quantity.

16.2. Destruction is witnessed by a QA officer and one other person. Both sign the certificate, and photographs of the sealed waste containers are filed with it.

16.3. Product is never thrown in general waste, even a single vial, and never given away, sold for scrap or used for training.

16.4. Packaging from destroyed product that carries a customer's name or batch number is destroyed with the product, not recycled.

16.5. The destruction register is reconciled each month against the quarantine register, so that every unit that went into quarantine has either been released, returned to its owner or destroyed.

## 17. Changes to this manual

17.1. Revision 1 (September 2025) was issued when the store opened. Revision 2 (December 2025) added the generator test after a power failure in the first month. Revision 3 (May 2026) changed several frequencies and limits after the store's first full season, and recorded the reasons beside each change.

17.2. The QA lead reviews this manual every April and after any critical deviation, failed customer audit or change to the building. The RP approves each revision.

17.3. Staff send suggested changes to the QA lead at any time. Each suggestion gets an answer, whether or not it is taken up.

17.4. A revised manual is issued with a one-page note of what changed. Every person who works in a product zone signs that they have read the note within a week of issue.
